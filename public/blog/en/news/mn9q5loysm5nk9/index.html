EU Responds to Security Threats, Considering Establishment of Its Own Intelligence Organization

Recently, it has been reported that the European Union (EU) is considering the possibility of establishing a new intelligence organization to respond to geopolitical threats. Piotr Serafin, the EU Commissioner for Budget, emphasized the need to strengthen the EU's security response capabilities amid the complex international situation, stating that the establishment of an intelligence organization is crucial. This organization is expected to operate based on existing expertise within the EU administration and will collaborate with the European External Action Service (EEAS). However, a specific timeline has yet to be determined.

This article illustrates the complex security environment the EU is facing and its response strategies, while also exploring the potential impact on future international relations.

Why Background Matters

In recent years, the EU has faced various geopolitical threats, including Russia's invasion of Ukraine, China's expanding economic influence, and terrorism. In this context, the need for the EU to strengthen cooperation among member states and establish a more systematic security response framework has emerged. The consideration of establishing an intelligence organization is part of this ongoing trend.

Why It Matters

This news illustrates how the EU's security policy is evolving. The EU is showing a willingness to enhance collective security among member states amid a more complex international situation than in the past. This is expected to contribute to increased transparency and efficiency in the policy-making process, going beyond mere information collection.
