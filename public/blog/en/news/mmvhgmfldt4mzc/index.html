Despite the Implementation of the Yellow Envelope Law, the Door of the Primary Contractor in Jeonbuk Remains Firmly Closed···“Real Boss, Respond!”

Recently, subcontracted workers in the Jeonbuk region have begun to demand collective bargaining with primary contractors based on the revised Labor Union Act, or the Yellow Envelope Law. On the 18th, the Jeonbuk branch of the Korean Confederation of Trade Unions held a press conference in front of the Jeonbuk Provincial Office, urging a responsible response from the primary contractors. The implementation of the Yellow Envelope Law has provided a legal basis for subcontracted unions to negotiate directly with the primary contractors, who are the actual employers, but the primary contractors in the Jeonbuk region are still showing a passive attitude. In particular, the Jeonbuk provincial government is maintaining a cautious stance regarding the bargaining demands, raising concerns that the purpose of the law may be undermined.

This issue is not limited to the Jeonbuk region but presents a new turning point for the exercise of rights by subcontracted workers nationwide. Especially in a situation where the structure that allows primary contractors to evade responsibility still exists, this demand for bargaining could become an important case that determines the future direction of the labor movement.

  • There is an increase in demands for bargaining to hold primary contractors accountable following the implementation of the Yellow Envelope Law.

  • The Korean Confederation of Trade Unions is urging immediate responses from primary contractors regarding bargaining.
  • The demand for bargaining is spreading beyond the public sector to various industries.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The Yellow Envelope Law is designed to allow subcontracted workers to negotiate directly with primary contractors who effectively determine working conditions. This measure targets primary contractors who have evaded responsibility due to the subcontracting structure, acting as a legal mechanism for protecting workers' rights. However, even after the law's implementation, the passive attitude of primary contractors in the Jeonbuk region continues, which is pointed out as a risk factor that could undermine the law's purpose.

Why It Matters

This incident highlights the issues of rights and responsibilities between labor and employers. If primary contractors, who directly influence the treatment of subcontracted workers, do not come to the bargaining table, the protection of workers' rights will become increasingly difficult. This is not merely a problem in the Jeonbuk region but could become a challenge across industries where the subcontracting structure is widespread.

Points of Attention

In my view, the most critical issue in this incident is the problem of primary contractors evading responsibility. Only when primary contractors take direct responsibility for the treatment and rights of subcontracted workers and engage in bargaining can true protection of workers' rights be achieved. Additionally, the passive response in the Jeonbuk region could influence other regions, necessitating solidarity and action from the labor movement.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One point that may be overlooked in this issue is that having a legal basis does not resolve all problems. The attitudes of primary contractors and the differences in response speed between regions significantly impact workers and could greatly influence the future strategies and directions of the labor movement.
