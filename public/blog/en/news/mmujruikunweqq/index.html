If the Paris Agreement target of 1.5 degrees is reached, there is a ‘163-day wildfire risk’

The impact of climate change on our lives is becoming increasingly severe. According to a recent study by a team of professors at KAIST, there is a warning that even a rise of 1.5 degrees in global average temperature, the target of the Paris Agreement, could lead to an increased wildfire risk in South Korea for 163 days a year. This research analyzes the effects of climate change on the timing and duration of wildfires, clearly illustrating how serious future climate change could be.

This study goes beyond mere warnings, presenting the tangible risks that climate change poses to our society and ecosystems in numerical terms. In particular, the possibility that wildfire risks could increase year-round due to rising temperatures suggests that this is not just an environmental issue but could expand into social and economic problems.

Key Summary

  • The wildfire risk period in South Korea increases to 163 days annually with a 1.5-degree rise, as per the Paris Agreement target.
  • The onset of wildfire risks is accelerating, and the duration of risk periods is lengthening with rising temperatures.
  • The mitigating effect of summer monsoons on wildfires is diminishing.
  • In a 2.0-degree rise scenario, the average risk period increases to 202 days, and to 214 days in a 4.0-degree scenario.
  • The intensification of climate change could lead to wildfires occurring regardless of season or region.

Key Scenes

  • Increase in Wildfire Risk Period: 163 days with a 1.5-degree rise, 202 days with a 2.0-degree rise, and 214 days with a 4.0-degree rise.
  • Seasonal Changes: Expansion of wildfire risks into winter, with the potential for year-round occurrences.
  • Diminishing Monsoon Effects: The wildfire mitigation effect of summer monsoons is disappearing.
  • Need for National Strategy: A long-term, nationwide response strategy is necessary to address the increased wildfire risks.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This study was conducted at the request of the international environmental organization Greenpeace and analyzed the impact of climate change on wildfires using high-resolution weather data. Historically, wildfire risks were concentrated in spring, but due to rising temperatures, they are now extending into winter, indicating that climate change is already posing a tangible threat in our surroundings.

Why It Matters

Wildfires are not merely natural disasters. They significantly impact ecosystems, human lives, and the economy. The damage caused by wildfires extends to agriculture, tourism, and the safety of local communities. Therefore, these research findings will serve as essential information for governments and businesses in developing strategies to respond to climate change.

Points to Note

As climate change intensifies, the increase in wildfire risks has already been predicted. However, this study quantifies how specific and tangible those risks are. In particular, the disappearance of the summer monsoon's effects will pose serious challenges for agriculture and forest management.

Hidden Variables to Consider

A key point emphasized in this study is that the rise in temperatures is not simply a matter of temperature alone. Climate change operates through complex factors, which can affect the ecosystems and economies of various regions in diverse ways. Therefore, responses to climate change must not be limited to greenhouse gas reductions but should include comprehensive approaches that encompass ecosystem conservation and community safety.

Current Checkpoints

  • Changes in Individual Awareness of Climate Change: It is important to reconsider the impact of climate change on our daily lives through the findings of this study.
  • Government and Corporate Response Strategies: As awareness of climate change increases, attention should be paid to how governments and businesses will respond.

FAQ

Q: What damages can occur if wildfire risks increase?
A: Wildfires can lead to ecosystem destruction, loss of life, agricultural losses, and air pollution, among various other damages.

Q: What is the role of individuals in addressing climate change?
A: Individuals can respond to climate change by reducing energy consumption and using recycling and eco-friendly products.

Q: What actions should the government take?
A: The government should strengthen policies for wildfire prevention and response and develop programs to enhance education and awareness about climate change.

Q: How should businesses respond to climate change?
A: Businesses should contribute to greenhouse gas reductions through sustainable management and develop strategies to manage risks associated with climate change.

In conclusion, we must not overlook the increased wildfire risks brought about by climate change. It is necessary to raise awareness of climate change immediately and reflect on our individual roles.
