OpenAI Shutting Down ‘Sora’... Focusing on Profitable AI

OpenAI has decided to terminate its AI video generation tool ‘Sora’. This decision, made two years after the service began, stems from the uncertainty surrounding the revenue model for video AI. Instead, OpenAI is focusing on business-to-business (B2B) operations, recently hiring an advertising executive from Meta and introducing ads to ChatGPT users to improve profitability. OpenAI explains that this change is part of its efforts to restructure its business ahead of the planned initial public offering (IPO) this year.

This decision is not merely a strategic shift for OpenAI alone. It reflects a significant trend among big tech companies shifting their focus from competition in AI model performance to profitability. Naver has also decided to terminate its conversational AI service ‘CLOVA X’, indicating a move towards securing revenue models for AI. This highlights the importance of achieving profitability in the current AI market.

  • Instead of the uncertain revenue model of video AI, it will focus on business-to-business (B2B) operations.

  • Naver and Kakao are also restructuring their AI businesses in a similar direction.

  • Strategic Acquisition: OpenAI is pursuing strategic changes to improve profitability, such as hiring advertising executives.

Why This Background Matters

OpenAI gained significant popularity by providing a platform that allows for easy creation of high-quality video content through ‘Sora’. However, the uncertainty surrounding the revenue model for video AI appears to have led to the decision to terminate the service. Additionally, the collapse of a $1 billion contract with Disney has further solidified the direction of the business. This decision can be interpreted as part of OpenAI's strategy to secure profitability ahead of its IPO.

Why It Matters

This news goes beyond the mere termination of a service by one company. The fact that large AI companies like OpenAI are prioritizing profitability indicates a significant trend in the overall direction of the AI market. This could also have a substantial impact on small and medium-sized enterprises and startups. While AI technology continues to advance, the challenge of how to monetize that technology will become increasingly pressing.

In my view, OpenAI's decision can be interpreted as a signal emphasizing the importance of revenue models in the future AI market, beyond just the termination of the Sora service. Particularly in a situation where AI technology is advancing but revenue models remain uncertain, companies will need to move in a more practical direction.

Hidden Variables to Consider

A key point to note in this decision is that OpenAI is making choices and focusing on profitability. This can serve as an important lesson for other companies: success cannot be achieved solely through technological performance.
