Legal Rights and Obligations to Know When Signing a Jeonse Contract

A jeonse contract is an important transaction that provides residential stability. However, if you do not understand the legal rights and obligations, you may suffer disadvantages. In this article, we will take a detailed look at the legal rights and obligations that you must know when signing a jeonse contract.

Understanding the Basic Concept of Jeonse Contracts

A jeonse contract is an agreement where the tenant deposits a certain amount of money with the landlord in exchange for the right to use the residential space for a specified period. The jeonse amount and the lease term are closely related, and generally, the longer the contract period, the higher the jeonse amount tends to be. Rights and obligations arise between the parties according to the contents specified in the contract.

Checkpoints

  • Essential Items in the Contract: The contract must include the deposit, lease term, and conditions for termination.
  • Consider Notarization: It is advisable to have the contract notarized to ensure legal validity.

Legal Rights of the Tenant in a Jeonse Contract

The tenant has the right to reside stably during the contract period and also has the right to claim the return of the deposit. Additionally, the tenant has the right to demand the maintenance of a safe and clean living environment.

Example

If the landlord does not return the deposit after the contract ends, the tenant can file a claim in court. In this case, the contract serves as important evidence.

Legal Rights of the Landlord in a Jeonse Contract

The landlord has the right to collect rent from the tenant and also has the right to terminate the contract. Furthermore, the landlord can demand compliance if the tenant fails to fulfill their obligations under the contract.

Checkpoints

  • Protection of Landlord's Rights: The landlord can demand contract termination from a tenant who does not fulfill the conditions specified in the contract.

Obligations of the Tenant in a Jeonse Contract

The tenant must comply with the terms of the contract and should not make changes to the facilities without the landlord's consent. The tenant also has regular management and maintenance obligations.

Action Tips

  • Regular Inspections: Regularly check the condition of the living space and promptly notify the landlord of any issues.

Obligations of the Landlord in a Jeonse Contract

The landlord must provide a safe living space and fulfill the conditions specified in the contract. The landlord also has the obligation to protect the tenant's rights.

Checkpoints

  • Safety Inspections: The landlord should regularly check the safety of the living space and promptly carry out necessary repairs.

Important Considerations When Signing a Jeonse Contract

When entering into a jeonse contract, there are several important considerations. Do not overlook the importance of the contract and ensure that you understand the role and responsibilities of the broker.

Importance of the Contract

The contract must include essential clauses, and it is important to verify these. Brokers play a significant role in the contract signing process, so it is advisable to choose a trustworthy broker.

Checklist to Prevent Jeonse Fraud

  • Verify the Contract: Always check the deposit, lease term, and termination conditions.
  • Verify the Landlord's Identity: Confirm the landlord's identity through their ID and proof of ownership.
  • Research Market Prices: Use real estate platforms to investigate the market prices of similar conditions to ensure fairness.

How to Resolve Disputes Related to Jeonse Contracts

If a dispute arises during the jeonse contract, how can it be resolved? There are several methods available.

How to Respond When a Dispute Arises

If a dispute occurs, you should first attempt to resolve it through discussions with the landlord. If it remains unresolved, you may need to consider legal procedures.

Guidance on Legal Procedures and Support Organizations

You can file a lawsuit in court and seek assistance through legal consultations. Additionally, you can resolve issues through mediation and arbitration procedures at the housing dispute mediation committee in your area.
