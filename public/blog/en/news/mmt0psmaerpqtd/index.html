What is the Secret Behind Korea's Leap as a Powerhouse in the Winter Paralympics?

At the 2026 Milan-Cortina d'Ampezzo Winter Paralympics, Korea achieved 2 gold medals, 4 silver medals, and 1 bronze medal, ranking 13th overall. This marks the highest number of medals for Korea in a single Paralympic event, a result of the athletes' exceptional individual skills and a systematic nurturing system. Notably, 20-year-old Kim Yoon-ji won 5 medals in 6 events, setting a new record in Korea's Paralympic history.

This achievement is not merely a coincidence but is evaluated as a result of the Korea Paralympic Committee's talent discovery program and systematic nurturing system. It is particularly emphasized that 12 out of the 20 athletes grew through this program. However, there are significant calls for a sustainable support system to ensure that this success is not a one-time event.

  • Korea ranked 13th overall at the 2026 Winter Paralympics with 2 golds, 4 silvers, and 1 bronze.

Key Highlights

  • Medal Achievement: Korea recorded the highest number of medals in history, establishing itself as a powerhouse in the Paralympics.
  • Key Athlete: Kim Yoon-ji gained attention by winning the most medals in a single event.
  • Nurturing System: The Korea Paralympic Committee's talent discovery program significantly contributed to the athletes' success.
  • Sustainability: The importance of a stable and continuous support system for successful athlete development has been emphasized.

Why Background Context Matters

Korea's success in the Winter Paralympics is a result of overcoming the 'no medal' embarrassment at the 2022 Beijing Games, supported by systematic athlete development and support. The Korea Paralympic Committee operates talent discovery camps every summer and winter, focusing on identifying promising athletes. Notably, Kim Yoon-ji has benefited from this program since elementary school, marking an important case for the future of Korean Paralympics.

Why is This Important?

This achievement demonstrates that Korea can establish itself as a powerhouse in the Winter Paralympics. It can serve as a wake-up call for the need to change perceptions and expand support for disabled sports. Additionally, the successful athlete development system could serve as a model applicable to other sports fields.

My Thoughts

It is particularly noteworthy that alongside individual achievements of athletes, there is a systematic system that allows them to grow. To produce more talents like Kim Yoon-ji, the current nurturing system needs to be strengthened, and support from businesses and the government should be aligned. It is important to ensure that promising athletes selected through annual camps can achieve good results in future competitions.

Hidden Variables to Consider

There is concern that this achievement may be evaluated solely based on medal counts. Just as important as winning medals is the sustainability of these achievements. If an environment is not established for athletes to train and compete stably, this success may remain a one-time event.

Current Points to Check

  • What are the government and corporate support plans for future Paralympics and disabled sports?
  • How will nurturing systems like the talent discovery camps evolve?

FAQ
