US-Iran 'Long War' Signs Increase Domestic and Foreign Financial Market Instability

As the war between the US and Iran shows signs of prolongation, the instability of oil prices and exchange rates is increasing. At a recent seminar held by the Korea Financial Investment Association, experts warned that if oil prices exceed $108 and the won-dollar exchange rate reaches the 1500 won range, the Bank of Korea may have to raise interest rates. While mentions of the possibility of easing sanctions on Iranian oil and the potential for an early ceasefire by Israel are interpreted as efforts to avoid the worst-case scenario, volatility continues in global financial markets.

This situation is expected to have a significant impact on the South Korean economy. In particular, South Korea, which heavily relies on Middle Eastern oil, may suffer even more severe damage due to rising oil prices and exchange rate instability. Experts warn that this instability is unlikely to be resolved in the short term, which is expected to further increase volatility in the financial markets.

Why Background Matters

This issue arises amidst escalating military tensions between the US and Iran. With Iranian oil exports restricted due to sanctions, a prolonged war could make oil supply even more difficult. This would lead to a rise in oil prices globally and could significantly impact the South Korean economy, which is heavily reliant on Middle Eastern oil. Experts warn that this situation is unlikely to be resolved in the short term.

Why It Matters

This issue goes beyond mere changes in international relations. Fluctuations in oil prices and exchange rates directly affect consumer prices, which can lead to inflation. Such changes can have serious implications for economies, especially for countries like South Korea that depend on Middle Eastern oil. Therefore, it is crucial to monitor this issue closely.

Points to Note

In my view, the war between Iran and Israel is unlikely to end simply as a military conflict. The political instability in the Middle East will likely have a continuous impact on oil prices and exchange rates, which will burden both consumers and businesses. In this regard, both the government and businesses need to develop long-term economic plans.

Hidden Variables to Consider

An important variable not covered in this article is the changing dynamics of international politics. For instance, how other countries establish their relationships with Iran can affect oil prices and exchange rates. Additionally, if the war prolongs, new issues such as humanitarian problems may arise.

Current Checkpoints

  • Oil Price Trends: Continuously monitor how international oil prices are changing.
  • Exchange Rate Fluctuations: Pay close attention to changes in the won-dollar exchange rate.
  • Interest Rate Hike News: Watch for official announcements regarding the Bank of Korea's interest rate decisions.

FAQ

Q: What impact does rising oil prices have on consumers?
A: Rising oil prices lead to increased fuel costs and inflation, raising the cost of living for consumers.

Q: Why is an interest rate hike important?
A: An interest rate hike raises borrowing costs for consumers and businesses, potentially negatively impacting economic growth.

Q: How long will the war between Iran and Israel last?
A: It is currently difficult to predict, but the situation could change rapidly depending on international political dynamics.

Q: How should South Korea respond?
A: South Korea needs to develop long-term plans, such as diversifying its energy sources, to reduce its dependence on Middle Eastern oil.
