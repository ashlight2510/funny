Points to Note When Writing a Career Description

A career description is an important document that effectively conveys the applicant's experience and capabilities during the job application and transition process. A well-written career description can increase interview opportunities and serve as the first step towards successful employment. In this article, we will specifically look at the points to be aware of when writing a career description.

Basic Structure of a Career Description: What Elements Does It Include?

Understanding the basic structure of a career description allows for systematic organization of content. Generally, it can be divided into the following elements:

  • Personal Information: Name, contact details, email address, etc.
  • Career History: Starting from the most recent experience to past experiences
  • Education: List from the highest education attained
  • Certifications and Skills: List of relevant certifications and skills

Checkpoints

  • Exclude Unnecessary Information: Keep personal information to a minimum and list career history starting from the most recent experience.
  • Maintain a Clear Format: Separate each item to enhance readability.

Achievement-Centered Career Description: Utilizing the STAR Technique

Describing specific achievements can enhance credibility. It is important to systematically narrate the Situation, Task, Action, and Result (STAR) using the STAR technique.

How to Apply the STAR Technique

  1. Set the Situation and Task: Clearly explain what the assigned task was.
  2. Describe the Action: Specify the actions taken to solve the problem.
  3. Present the Result: Express achievements in numerical terms to enhance credibility.

Example

"In Project A, to achieve a 20% increase in sales, I collaborated with team members to develop and implement a new marketing strategy."

Execution Tips

  • If possible, use specific data or metrics to express achievements numerically.

Differences Between a Resume and a Career Description: What’s the Difference?

Resumes and career descriptions differ in purpose and format. A resume aims to provide simple information, while a career description requires detailed explanations of experiences and achievements.

Item Resume Career Description
Purpose Simple information provision Detailed explanation of experiences and achievements
Format Concise and summarized form Specific and narrative form
Content Basic information and career summary In-depth explanation of each experience

Checkpoints

  • Be careful not to confuse resumes and career descriptions. Each document should be written according to the required content and format.

Writing a Tailored Career Description: How to Approach It?

Writing a career description tailored to the job you are applying for can enhance your competitiveness. To do this, you first need to analyze the job you are applying for and emphasize relevant experiences.

Execution Tips

  • Job Analysis: After understanding the requirements of the job you are applying for, focus on describing experiences related to that job.
  • Use Keywords: Include keywords relevant to the job to enhance readability and attract the attention of recruiters.

Checklist After Writing a Career Description: What to Check?

Final checks can reduce errors and enhance completeness. Using a checklist while reviewing your written career description can be more effective.

Checklist

  1. Check grammar and spelling
  2. Verify consistency in career history
  3. Remove unnecessary information
  4. Confirm inclusion of job-relevant keywords

Execution Tips

  • Feedback from Others: Seek feedback from professionals or friends. You may discover overlooked aspects from a new perspective.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  1. How many pages should a career description be?
    Generally, 1 to 2 pages is appropriate. If it becomes too long, the key points may get lost.

  2. What content must be included in a career description?
    It should include career history, achievements, relevant skills, and certifications, and should be adjusted according to the job you are applying for.

  3. What are the most common mistakes made when writing a career description?
    The most common mistakes are exaggerated expressions or listing unnecessary information. It is important to write based on facts.

Conclusion: The First Step in Writing a Career Description

When writing a career description, remember and apply the points mentioned above. As a first step, it is recommended to organize your career and achievements and practice describing them specifically using the STAR technique. Start organizing your experiences right now!
