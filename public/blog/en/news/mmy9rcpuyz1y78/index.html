Worker falls to death during work... Supreme Court "Site manager responsible for neglecting danger"

Recently, the Supreme Court recognized the responsibility of the site manager in the case of a foreign worker falling to death at a construction site, drawing renewed attention to the scope of application of the Industrial Safety and Health Act and the importance of safety obligations. The Supreme Court ruled that even if the site manager did not give specific instructions, they could still be held legally responsible if they neglected foreseeable dangers. This is considered an important case that raises awareness in the construction industry and regarding worker safety.

The incident occurred in 2020 at an apartment exterior construction site, where a Russian national fell and died due to negligence while working. The Supreme Court's ruling holds the site manager accountable for neglecting safety measures, which is expected to significantly influence legal standards in similar cases in the future.

Key Summary

  • The Supreme Court ruled that site managers can be held legally responsible for neglecting dangers, even if they did not give specific work instructions.
  • This ruling emphasizes the safety obligations of site managers in relation to the incident where a foreign worker fell to death.
  • The court recognized the causal relationship between the violation of safety obligations and the worker's death, broadening the scope of the Industrial Safety and Health Act.

Key Highlights

  • Expansion of Legal Responsibility: Site managers can be held accountable for foreseeable dangers even if they did not give specific instructions.
  • Safety Obligation: Under the Industrial Safety and Health Act, all workers are required to implement safety measures.
  • Recognition of Causality: The court acknowledged the causal relationship between the violation of safety obligations and the death of the worker.
  • Protection of Foreign Workers: The court noted the issue of communication gaps regarding the safety of foreign workers.
  • Awareness in the Construction Industry: This ruling could serve as a catalyst for improving safety practices at construction sites.

Why Background Matters

This incident is one of the frequent safety accidents in the construction industry, particularly highlighting the safety issues faced by foreign workers. Many foreign workers struggle with the Korean language, leading to communication difficulties that can act as risk factors for safety accidents. The Supreme Court's ruling is interpreted as an effort to clarify legal standards to address these issues.

Why It Matters

This ruling conveys a message that goes beyond individual responsibility, emphasizing the need to strengthen safety measures across the construction industry. It is particularly significant in that it clearly delineates the legal responsibilities to ensure the safety of workers who may have communication barriers, such as foreign workers. This is likely to serve as a precedent in future similar cases, contributing to an overall improvement in safety culture within the construction industry.
