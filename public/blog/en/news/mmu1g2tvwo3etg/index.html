Ilgwang Polymer CEO Sentenced to 3 Years in Prison… Significance of the Serious Accident Punishment Act

In a case where a worker in their 20s died in an explosion at Ilgwang Polymer's factory in Seocheon, Chungcheongnam-do in 2022, the CEO of the company has received a confirmed sentence of 3 years in prison. This is the highest sentence applied under the Serious Accident Punishment Act, and the Supreme Court made it clear that this incident cannot be dismissed as a mere personal mistake. This ruling emphasizes corporate safety management responsibilities and is expected to have a significant impact on the future application of the Serious Accident Punishment Act.

This case holds significance beyond a simple legal ruling. It is one of the first cases to be applied since the introduction of the Serious Accident Punishment Act, conveying the message that companies can be held legally accountable if they fail to ensure worker safety. This will serve as an important safety net for workers and set a higher standard of responsibility for companies.

Key Summary

  • The CEO of Ilgwang Polymer has been sentenced to 3 years in prison for violating the Serious Accident Punishment Act.
  • The accident occurred due to the improper use of flammable material, ethanol.
  • The Supreme Court ruled that the Serious Accident Punishment Act should be applied considering the entire corporation.

Key Highlights

  • Application of Maximum Sentence under the Serious Accident Punishment Act: This ruling demonstrates that the Serious Accident Punishment Act can be applied strongly in practice.
  • Emphasis on Safety Management Obligations: A warning that companies will face legal responsibilities if they lack safety management and preventive measures.
  • Corporate Responsibility as a Whole: Even if the number of workers at an individual factory is small, the safety management responsibility of the entire corporation applies.
  • Awareness of Worker Safety: This ruling will act as a legal protective measure for worker safety.
  • Need for Change to Prevent Accidents: Companies must reassess their safety management systems to prevent recurrence.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The Serious Accident Punishment Act has been in effect since January 2021 and strengthens corporate safety management obligations. This law was introduced to prevent fatal accidents caused by industrial accidents and to encourage companies to ensure worker safety. However, there have been doubts about how the law would actually be applied. This incident serves as an important milestone in alleviating those doubts.

Ilgwang Polymer, where the accident occurred, did not comply with safety management standards and improperly used flammable materials, leading to the disaster. This makes it the first case to apply the Serious Accident Punishment Act, further emphasizing corporate safety management obligations.

Why It Matters

This ruling serves as a case demonstrating the enforceability of the Serious Accident Punishment Act, informing that companies can face serious legal consequences if they neglect safety. This will raise the voices demanding a safer working environment for workers and serve as a warning for companies to strengthen their safety management systems.

Points of Interest

In my view, this case is noteworthy not just for the high sentence but for the long-term impact of the application of the Serious Accident Punishment Act on corporate culture. There is a high likelihood that companies will increase their investments in safety management to avoid legal responsibilities.

Hidden Variables to Consider

In this ruling, the Supreme Court specified that the number of workers at individual factories does not affect the application of the Serious Accident Punishment Act. This will be an important criterion for how the Serious Accident Punishment Act is interpreted and applied in the future. Companies need to prepare for this legal interpretation in advance.

Current Checkpoints
