Year-End Tax Settlement Tips for Young Adults: What Refund Items Are Easily Overlooked?

The year-end tax settlement is an important time for young adults every year, as it offers various items to reduce tax burdens and receive refunds. In addition to basic deduction items, utilizing lesser-known refund items can lead to greater refunds. In this article, we will explore the refund items that young adults often overlook during the year-end tax settlement and how to make the most of them.

Basic Understanding of Year-End Tax Settlement

What is Year-End Tax Settlement?

Year-end tax settlement is the process by which wage earners reconcile the taxes they have paid over the year. Excess taxes paid can be refunded, while any shortfall must be paid additionally. The year-end tax settlement occurs every January, making it essential for wage earners to be informed.

Action Tip: Check your income and tax payment history before the year-end tax settlement period begins.

Key Terminology

Understanding the differences between deductions, tax credits, and refunds is crucial for grasping year-end tax settlement.

  • Deduction: Amount deducted from income
  • Tax Credit: Amount directly deducted from the tax owed
  • Refund: The return of excess taxes paid

Checkpoint: Clearly understand the definitions of each term and verify the deductions and tax credits applicable to your situation.

Who is Subject to Year-End Tax Settlement?

Year-end tax settlement primarily applies to wage earners, but self-employed individuals can also settle their taxes through it. Wage earners undergo the settlement process through their employers, while self-employed individuals must report directly to the tax office.

Key Refund Items for Young Adults

Wage Income Tax Deduction

The wage income tax deduction is a basic deduction applicable to young workers, with the deduction amount varying based on income. For example, if your annual income is below 30 million won, you can receive a maximum deduction of 700,000 won. Failing to report income can lead to disadvantages, so accurate income reporting is essential.

Action Tip: Accurately calculate your annual income and thoroughly report it.

Education Expense Tax Credit

The education expense tax credit allows you to reduce your tax liability through support for tuition, academy fees, and other educational expenses. You must keep receipts to claim this tax credit.

Checkpoint: Organize your education expense receipts and confirm which items are eligible.

Housing Fund Deduction

The housing fund deduction provides financial support for purchasing a home, allowing you to claim deductions on loan interest. If you have purchased a home using a mortgage, you can receive a tax credit on the interest paid.

Action Tip: Check the loan conditions and prepare relevant documents in advance.

Additional Overlooked Deduction Items

Charitable Contribution Tax Credit

The charitable contribution tax credit is a way to reduce your tax liability through social contributions. Donations to public interest corporations are recognized, and you must keep donation receipts.

Checkpoint: Organize your donation receipts and confirm which contributions are eligible.

Medical Expense Tax Credit

The medical expense tax credit is also an important item that can alleviate unexpected medical costs. It includes hospital bills, medication costs, etc., and requires proof of medical expenses, so it's crucial to keep receipts well-organized.

Action Tip: Organize your medical expense receipts and check which items are eligible for deductions.

Year-End Tax Settlement Checklist for Young Adults

Use the following checklist to prepare for your year-end tax settlement.

  1. Prepare Necessary Documents: Gather income certificates, receipts, donation receipts, etc., in advance.
  2. Verify Deduction Items: Check deduction items to ensure nothing is overlooked.
  3. Confirm Reporting Deadline: Check the deadline for year-end tax settlement reporting to ensure you complete it on time.

Year-End Tax Settlement FAQ

Q1: What happens if I don't do the year-end tax settlement?
A1: If you don't do the year-end tax settlement, you will miss the opportunity to receive a refund and may have to pay additional taxes.

Q2: What other types of income are included in the year-end tax settlement besides wage income?
A2: In addition to wage income, business income, interest income, and dividend income can also be included in the year-end tax settlement.

Q3: Why is my refund amount lower than expected?
A3: A lower refund amount may be due to unreported income, non-application of deduction items, or limits on tax credits.

The Future and Changes in Year-End Tax Settlement

Trends in Tax Law Amendments

Recent amendments to tax laws have a significant impact on young adults. There is an expansion of tax credits for the youth, and utilizing these effectively can lead to larger refunds.

Impact of Digitalization

The advantages of electronic reporting include convenience and speed. Electronic reporting makes document submission and inquiries easier, allowing for a more efficient year-end tax settlement process.
