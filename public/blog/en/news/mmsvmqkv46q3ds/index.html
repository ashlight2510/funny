Basic Pension Couple Reduction Reform Gains Momentum: What Questions Remain?

March 16, 2026 · 경향신문 · View source

Basic Pension Couple Reduction Reform Gains Momentum···President Lee Also Supports, But ‘Finances’ Are a Variable

There are signs that the basic pension couple reduction system is set to be improved. The Ministry of Health and Welfare has announced plans to gradually lower the reduction rate for low-income elderly couples, and President Lee Jae-myung has expressed support for this initiative. Currently, the basic pension system reduces the pension amount by 20% for couples receiving benefits simultaneously, which has faced criticism for not aligning with the realities of living conditions. In relation to this, a complete repeal bill is also being discussed in the National Assembly, with financial burdens acting as a variable.

This issue goes beyond a simple policy change; it is directly related to the living standards of elderly couples. The basic pension couple reduction system causes economic disadvantages and even leads to social side effects such as fake divorces. Considering these complex interests, it is essential to pay attention to the direction this change will take.

Key Points

  • The basic pension couple reduction system is set to be gradually improved.
  • The reduction rate is planned to decrease, focusing on low-income elderly couples.
  • President Lee Jae-myung has expressed support for the improvement of the system.
  • Financial burdens may act as a major variable for complete repeal.
  • Concerns about the ‘reversal phenomenon’ where basic pension amounts exceed national pension amounts.

Background and Context

The current basic pension couple reduction system is designed based on the logic of ‘economies of scale,’ where couples living together share living expenses. However, the reality is different. According to a survey by the National Pension Research Institute, the average monthly consumption expenditure of the bottom 20% of the poorest elderly couples is 1.74 times that of elderly individuals living alone, exceeding the criteria of the existing system. This situation reveals that the basic pension couple reduction system is an outdated policy.

Since last year, the Ministry of Health and Welfare has made improving the couple reduction system a national task and has expressed its commitment to strengthening support for vulnerable groups. However, discussions on how to address the financial burden are still ongoing. The National Assembly Budget Office estimates that abolishing the couple reduction would require an additional 16.7 trillion won in finances by 2030.

Why Is This Issue Important?

Improving the basic pension couple reduction system is not merely a policy change; it is a matter directly related to the stability of elderly couples' lives. If this system is maintained, economically disadvantaged elderly couples may find themselves in even more difficult situations. Furthermore, if improvements are made, it will serve as an important case showing how the direction of elderly welfare policies may change. Therefore, this issue provides significant implications for the overall elderly welfare policy.

Often Overlooked Aspects

One aspect not covered in this article is the potential social changes that may occur if the basic pension couple reduction system is abolished. For example, while beneficiaries of the basic pension may receive more benefits without the couple reduction, this could undermine trust in the national pension system. It is important to remember that changes in the system can have complex social impacts beyond mere figures.

What to Check Now

It is crucial to closely monitor the discussions on improving the basic pension couple reduction system in the Ministry of Health and Welfare and the National Assembly. In particular, whether specific support measures for low-income elderly couples will be established and how the financial burden issue will be resolved are important points.
