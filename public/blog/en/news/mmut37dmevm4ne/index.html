A frame that shows photos and videos as '3D holograms' has been introduced

Recently, the American startup Looking Glass Factory unveiled a new type of display device called 'Musubi.' This product features the ability to display photos and videos like a 3D hologram without the need for a separate headset or special glasses. Users can easily transfer images and videos stored on the device via USB-C, and it can store up to 1,000 photos and 30-second video clips. The price is approximately 220,000 KRW, and it is currently available on Kickstarter for a discounted price of 140,000 KRW.

This innovative product has the potential to redefine how we appreciate photos and videos, going beyond simple display technology. Let's examine why this news is important and what points we should pay attention to.

Key Summary

  • Musubi: A frame-like device that displays photos and videos like a 3D hologram.
  • No headset required: Experience 3D effects without additional equipment.
  • Storage capacity: Can store 1,000 photos and 30-second video clips.
  • Price: Regular price is about 220,000 KRW; available for about 140,000 KRW on Kickstarter.
  • User convenience: Images and videos can be transferred via USB-C after conversion using software on a PC or Mac.

Reasons to Consider the Background

In recent years, hologram technology has rapidly advanced. The development of VR (virtual reality) and AR (augmented reality) technologies has provided users with immersive experiences. However, these technologies typically require expensive equipment or complex installations. Looking Glass Factory's Musubi aims to break down these barriers and provide 3D holograms in a form that is easily accessible to the general consumer.

Why is it Important?

This device holds potential that goes beyond being a simple consumer product; it could impact various industries. For example, in education, 3D holograms could help visually simplify complex concepts. Additionally, it will offer artists and content creators a new way to exhibit their works.
