Minister of Climate: “If we do not expand two nuclear reactors, we fear ‘Nuclear Phase-Out Season 2’… We must escape the nuclear phase-out frame for renewable energy growth”

Recently, Minister of Climate, Energy and Environment Kim Seong-hwan emphasized the necessity of expanding two nuclear reactors and stated that renewable energy can only grow if we move away from the nuclear phase-out policy. He highlighted the importance of nuclear power as an essential energy source for stable electricity supply amid the prolonged energy crisis in the Middle East, asserting that nuclear power and renewable energy must coexist. Alongside this, he presented specific plans for expanding renewable energy and expressed his commitment to accelerating the transition to solar energy.

This news reflects the government's efforts to balance climate policy and energy supply, and it holds significant meaning as it seeks practical solutions to resolve the conflict between nuclear power and renewable energy.

Reasons to Consider the Background

These remarks come amid a complex situation where the energy crisis and climate change intersect. Recent political instability in the Middle East has significantly impacted energy supply, making stable electricity supply even more critical. South Korea has faced conflicts between nuclear power and renewable energy, leading to extreme polarization in energy policy. Minister Kim is reiterating the need for nuclear power to facilitate a smooth energy transition and resolve these conflicts.

This news illustrates how South Korea's energy policy is evolving. Balancing nuclear power and renewable energy goes beyond merely addressing energy supply issues; it also significantly impacts climate change response and maintaining industrial competitiveness. Additionally, the need for policy support for the growth of renewable energy is emphasized.
