Geumcheon-gu, 'Public First' in Waste Reduction···'Department Name System' for Trash Bags and Ban on Disposable Products

Seoul's Geumcheon-gu has announced a new policy aimed at waste reduction and recycling as it begins the 'Public Sector Resource Circulation Leading Practice Movement.' The core of this policy is to lead by example in the public sector to encourage voluntary participation from the private sector. Within the district office, measures such as the implementation of a 'Department Name System' that requires departments to label trash bags with their names and a ban on the use of disposable products will be enforced. Additionally, various support programs are planned to expand this initiative to the private sector.

These changes are expected to play a significant role not only in reducing waste but also in promoting resource circulation throughout Seoul and raising awareness about environmental protection.

Key Summary

  • Geumcheon-gu has initiated a practice movement to lead resource circulation in the public sector.
  • Introduction of the 'Department Name System' requiring departments to label trash bags.
  • Ban on the use of disposable products in public offices and encouragement of personal tumbler use.
  • Plans for expanding recycling items and support programs to promote private sector participation.
  • Emphasis on public sector responsibility due to the ban on direct landfill at metropolitan landfills.

Key Scenes

  • Introduction of the Department Name System: Enhances transparency in waste management by clearly defining responsibilities for waste separation.
  • Ban on Disposable Products: Strengthens environmental protection practices by prohibiting the use of disposable products in public offices.
  • Encouraging Private Participation: Promotes resource circulation in the private sector through resident support programs.
  • Expansion of Recycling Items: Changes items previously treated as general waste to be recyclable.
  • Raising Environmental Awareness: Public sector leadership creates opportunities to enhance local residents' awareness of environmental protection.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This policy was developed in response to the contemporary challenge of banning direct landfill at metropolitan landfills. Geumcheon-gu has made various efforts regarding environmental issues in the past, but this practice movement attempts a more systematic and comprehensive approach. The recognition that the public sector must take the lead for the private sector to follow has been the impetus for this policy, which will serve as an important milestone for sustainable development in Geumcheon-gu.

Why It Matters

This policy has the potential to contribute not only to solving environmental issues in Geumcheon-gu but also to improving the resource circulation system throughout Seoul. The proactive stance of the public sector can serve as a model for the private sector, which is essential for raising local residents' environmental consciousness and promoting sustainable lifestyles in the long term. Furthermore, if these changes spread to other regions, they could lead to even greater social transformations.

FAQ
