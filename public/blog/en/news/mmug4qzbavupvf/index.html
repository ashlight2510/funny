Prosecutorial Reform Agreement: A Turning Point in Alleviating Concerns of 'Prosecutorial Domination'

The recently announced bill for the establishment of the Public Prosecution Office and the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency (SIA) by the Democratic Party is emerging as a significant milestone in prosecutorial reform. This bill has substantially reduced concerns about the indirect investigative powers of prosecutors that have been raised until now, and it is scheduled to be presented at the National Assembly's plenary session on the 19th. The Democratic Party emphasized that this agreement reflects the public's concerns by removing toxic provisions and modifying the scope of prosecutors' duties to be defined only by law. This is seen as a measure that fundamentally blocks the possibility of restoring prosecutors' investigative powers through amendments to enforcement decrees when the regime changes in the future.

This agreement is an important step toward prosecutorial reform, as it removes the obligation for SIA investigators to notify Public Prosecution Office prosecutors of the initiation of investigations and the right for prosecutors to demand case registration when assessing the necessity of investigations, thereby blocking the potential for prosecutors' investigative involvement. This effort stands out as a means to prevent the abuse of prosecutorial power and to clarify the separation of investigation and prosecution. However, how these changes will actually play out depends on the future legislative process and public response.

Key Points

  • Limitation of Prosecutorial Investigative Powers: A bill is expected to pass that blocks the possibility of prosecutors' investigative involvement.
  • Definition of Duties by Law: The scope of prosecutors' duties will be defined by law rather than enforcement decrees to prevent abuse of power.
  • Independence of SIA and Public Prosecution Office: The influence of SIA investigators over Public Prosecution Office prosecutors will be eliminated.
  • Sustainability of Prosecutorial Reform: Discussions on amending the Criminal Procedure Act will continue to advance prosecutorial reform.
  • Resolution of Conflicts within the Ruling Party: Emphasis on the need for coordination and deliberation reflecting conflicts during the bill preparation process.

Why Background Matters

This prosecutorial reform agreement was reached amid deepening conflicts within the ruling party. President Lee Jae-myung pointed out the inadequacy of managing the legislative process for prosecutorial reform and emphasized the need for deliberation and coordination. Given that the past expansion of investigative powers through amendments by former Minister of Justice Han Dong-hoon sparked controversy, this bill can be seen as an effort to alleviate such concerns.

Why It Matters

Prosecutorial reform is essential not only for the passage of a single bill but also for preventing the abuse of power and ensuring the protection of citizens' rights. When prosecutorial authority is excessively concentrated, the risk of distorting justice increases, which can undermine trust across society. Therefore, this agreement is crucial as it aims to check prosecutorial power and lay the groundwork for democracy.

Personal Perspective

In my view, this bill represents significant progress toward prosecutorial reform, but its effectiveness will depend on the future legislative process and public reaction. Particularly, how the contents of the bill will be implemented and how the public will respond will be key points. If measures to limit prosecutorial power are effectively enforced, it will greatly help protect citizens' rights.

Hidden Variables to Consider

This agreement does not resolve all concerns. Some have raised worries that prosecutorial investigative capabilities may weaken. Additionally, variables or conflicts that may arise during the implementation of the bill still exist, necessitating ongoing monitoring and evaluation.

Points to Check Now

Attention should be paid to how this bill will be handled in the upcoming National Assembly plenary session. It is also important to observe how prosecutors' powers will change after the bill's passage and how the public will react.
