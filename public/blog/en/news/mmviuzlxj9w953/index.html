National Human Rights Commission “Excluding Oriental Medicine Doctors' Diagnosis from Transportation Vulnerable Assessment is Discrimination”

Recently, the National Human Rights Commission has issued a recommendation stating that excluding Oriental medicine doctors' diagnosis from the assessment of transportation vulnerable individuals for special transportation services is discriminatory. This issue arose from a case in Gumi City, where a diagnosis issued by an Oriental medicine doctor was excluded from the assessment, and the NHRC deemed this action as discrimination without reasonable grounds. Gumi City cited budget and personnel constraints, but the NHRC pointed out that such criteria cannot be legally justified.

This incident raises important questions beyond the mere recognition of Oriental medicine doctors' diagnoses; it prompts a reevaluation of the fairness and accessibility of policies concerning transportation vulnerable individuals. It is particularly crucial to consider how the rights of this socially vulnerable group can be guaranteed.

Key Summary

  • The National Human Rights Commission has determined that Gumi City's exclusion of Oriental medicine doctors' diagnoses from the assessment for special transportation services is discriminatory.
  • Gumi City cited budget and personnel constraints, but the NHRC did not accept this argument.
  • The differential treatment of Oriental medicine doctors' and medical doctors' diagnoses, despite both being subject to the same legal standards, has been highlighted as an issue.
  • The NHRC emphasized that Oriental medicine hospitals are recognized as medical institutions, and diagnoses issued by Oriental medicine doctors are valid.

Key Points

  • The NHRC's recommendation emphasizes the fairness of policies for transportation vulnerable individuals.
  • The discrimination between diagnoses from Oriental medicine doctors and medical doctors could exacerbate social inequality.
  • Gumi City's operational policies cannot be legally justified.
  • The need for policy improvements regarding transportation vulnerable individuals is underscored.

Why Background Matters

This incident began when Gumi City excluded Oriental medicine doctors' diagnoses from the assessment for special transportation services. Why It Matters

Transportation vulnerable individuals are among the socially disadvantaged, and their inability to access necessary services can lead to serious discrimination. The NHRC's recommendation can play a crucial role in enhancing the fairness of policies for transportation vulnerable individuals and, furthermore, in ensuring the rights of socially vulnerable groups. Additionally, the fact that both Oriental medicine doctors' and medical doctors' diagnoses are subject to the same legal standards suggests that broader discussions are needed within the medical community.

Points to Watch

Policies for transportation vulnerable individuals should not merely serve as a means for budget cuts but require a more comprehensive and equitable approach.
