Coal Power Cap Lifted, Nuclear Utilization Expanded to 80%: What Questions Remain?

March 16, 2026 · 경향신문 · View source

Government and Ruling Party Announce Lifting of Coal Power Cap and Expansion of Nuclear Power Utilization Rate

Recently, due to the situation in the Middle East causing disruptions in energy supply, the government and ruling party announced emergency measures. They decided to lift the cap on coal power generation and expand the utilization rate of nuclear power to 80%, while also releasing strategic oil reserves for three months. This is being implemented alongside policy funding to alleviate financial pressure on small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), aiming for a swift response to the energy crisis.

These measures are expected to significantly impact SMEs and general consumers, playing a crucial role in securing stability in the energy market. However, there are various variables and limitations to consider, necessitating careful observation of future developments.

Key Points

  • Lifting the cap on coal power generation to 80% and expanding the nuclear power utilization rate to 80%.
  • Gradual release of 22.46 million barrels of strategic oil over three months.
  • Provision of 670 billion won in emergency management stabilization funds for SMEs.
  • Extension of the maturity repayment period for policy funds for SMEs and non-application of additional interest rates.
  • Support for small exporting companies through emergency logistics support vouchers.

Background and Context

This measure was taken in response to a serious crisis in energy supply caused by the unstable situation in the Middle East. In particular, the political instability in oil-exporting countries is directly affecting South Korea's energy market, prompting the government to respond swiftly to secure energy resources and ensure stable supply. Against this backdrop, the increase in coal and nuclear power generation is intended to diversify energy supply while contributing to the management stability of SMEs.

Why This Issue is Important

This decision goes beyond simply addressing energy supply issues; it is directly related to the survival of SMEs. Rising energy costs immediately impact operational costs for businesses, which in turn affects consumer prices. Therefore, this measure has significant ripple effects on the overall economy, and the effectiveness of the government's response will be an important indicator of future economic stability.

Often Overlooked Aspects

While the article focuses on short-term measures like the release of strategic oil, it is also important not to overlook the lack of long-term policy direction for energy transition. If the current energy policy emphasizes short-term crisis response, there will also need to be considerations for the sustainability of energy resources and responses to climate change in the future.

What to Check Now

In the current situation where SMEs are under financial pressure, it is important to observe what actual effects the government's emergency management stabilization fund support will have. Additionally, continuously monitoring the impact of the strategic oil release on the energy market and whether the nuclear power maintenance schedule proceeds as planned is crucial.
