Ulsan Petroglyph Museum Creates Barrier-Free Viewing Environment

Recently, the Ulsan Petroglyph Museum announced plans to create a barrier-free exhibition environment so that everyone can view the UNESCO World Heritage Bangucheon Petroglyphs without discrimination. This project includes adjusting the flow within the exhibition hall for the convenience of various visitors, such as people with disabilities, the elderly, and pregnant women, as well as producing tactile exhibits, providing braille guidebooks, and offering online content through QR codes. The project is set to be completed in June this year with a budget of 200 million won and is being promoted after Ulsan was selected for the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism's project to enhance barrier-free tourism connectivity.

These changes will not only improve the physical accessibility of the museum but also serve as an important opportunity to socially expand access to cultural heritage. In particular, the Bangucheon Petroglyphs contain evidence of prehistoric whale hunting, which adds significant historical value.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This project is being promoted as a result of Ulsan being selected for the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism's project to enhance barrier-free tourism connectivity. Barrier-free tourism aims to enable people with disabilities and various social minorities to easily access cultural content, which can be interpreted as fulfilling social responsibilities related to the protection of cultural heritage. In particular, since the Bangucheon Petroglyphs were registered as a UNESCO World Heritage site last year, this effort can be seen as a way to share their value with a broader audience.

Why It Matters

This project holds significance beyond merely improving physical accessibility. Enhancing access to cultural heritage increases social inclusivity and provides opportunities for diverse audiences to experience cultural properties. This can also positively impact the protection and preservation of cultural heritage. Furthermore, the creation of a barrier-free viewing environment can serve as a good example for other museums and cultural heritage management organizations.

Points of Interest

In my view, a particularly noteworthy aspect of this project is the production of tactile exhibits. Moving away from exhibits that rely solely on visual information to convey information through touch can provide a new experience for all visitors, not just those with disabilities. This approach could serve as a catalyst for changing the paradigm of cultural property viewing, allowing visitors to experience cultural heritage in a way that stimulates various senses.
