Bithumb's Negligence in Customer Verification Leads to 36.8 Billion Won Fine and 6-Month Business Suspension

Recently, the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) imposed a fine of 36.8 billion won on Bithumb for violations of the Specific Financial Information Act and ordered a 6-month partial business suspension. This action follows the results of an anti-money laundering field inspection conducted between March and April of last year, which confirmed that Bithumb violated customer verification obligations approximately 3.55 million times. This incident marks the largest fine ever imposed on a virtual asset exchange, revealing issues such as inadequate verification of customer information and continued transactions with unreported overseas virtual asset operators.

This incident carries significant implications beyond just the imposition of a fine, particularly demonstrating the financial authorities' strong regulatory intent, increasing the likelihood of similar actions being taken against other exchanges in the future.

Key Summary

  • Bithumb fined 36.8 billion won and received a 6-month business suspension.
  • Approximately 3.55 million violations of customer verification obligations and violations of transaction restrictions.
  • Largest fine imposed on a virtual asset company.
  • Strong regulatory intent from financial authorities is evident.
  • Bithumb has expressed its intention to improve in the future.

Key Highlights

  • Fine Amount: 36.8 billion won is the largest fine imposed on a virtual asset company.
  • Business Suspension Measure: Restrictions on external virtual asset deposits and withdrawals for new customers.
  • Legal Obligation Violations: Large-scale violations of customer verification and transaction restriction obligations.
  • Strengthening of Financial Authorities' Regulations: Increased monitoring and sanctions against virtual asset exchanges.
  • Impact on the Industry: Other exchanges may face similar penalties.

Why Background Matters

This incident occurred amid a trend of increasing regulation on virtual asset exchanges. The FIU is strengthening on-site inspections of virtual asset exchanges to prevent illegal money laundering and crime, which is understood as a measure to combat these issues. Bithumb has suffered significant damage due to the problems revealed through these inspections, which could raise awareness across the industry.

Why It Matters

The virtual asset market is rapidly growing, but it also requires regulation and oversight. Bithumb's punishment clearly illustrates the reasons why virtual asset exchanges must comply with legal obligations. With strengthened regulations for customer protection and anti-money laundering, there is a higher likelihood of providing investors with a safer trading environment.
