US Defense Requests an Additional 300 Trillion Won for Iran War... Low Chances of Passage

Recently, the US Department of Defense requested an additional supplementary budget of $200 billion, equivalent to about 300 trillion won, for the war with Iran. This request aims to replenish funds depleted due to the prolonged war, and there is controversy within both the Democratic and Republican parties regarding the justification and necessity of this budget. In particular, concerns have been raised within the White House about the low likelihood of this budget passing, which could significantly impact US foreign policy.

This situation is not merely a budget issue but reveals the internal chaos and political conflicts within the US government. Senior officials in intelligence agencies have questioned the justification for the war, with some even resigning. The increasing opacity of information regarding Iran's nuclear threats is seriously undermining trust in US foreign policy.

Key Highlights

  • US Department of Defense requests $200 billion for Iran war funds.
  • Controversy over the justification and necessity of the budget within the Democratic and Republican parties.
  • Skepticism raised within the White House regarding the likelihood of the budget passing.
  • Senior intelligence officials questioning the justification for the war and resigning.
  • Deepening opacity of information regarding Iran's nuclear threats.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This request for war funds comes amid escalating tensions between the US and Iran. The Trump administration initiated the war citing Iran's nuclear program, but criticism has emerged regarding the unclear justification and objectives of the war within the US. As the war with Iran drags on, the costs are skyrocketing, leading to political conflicts. In particular, the Democratic Party is strongly opposing the war, questioning its legitimacy.

Why It Matters

This request transcends mere military funding issues and could have significant implications for US foreign policy and political stability. As war costs escalate, public backlash may grow, potentially influencing future elections. Additionally, the decline in the credibility of intelligence agencies is increasing concerns about national security. As uncertainty about the actual existence of Iran's nuclear threat grows, US foreign policy may become even more complicated.

Furthermore, if internal conflicts within intelligence agencies deepen, threats to national security could increase.
