St. Vincent Hospital Registers US Patent for Survival Prediction Technology for Lung Cancer Brain Metastasis Patients

Recently, it was reported that the survival prediction technology for lung cancer brain metastasis patients developed by Professor Kim Young-il's team at St. Vincent Hospital has completed patent registration in the United States. This technology analyzes changes in the thickness of the temporalis muscle in non-small cell lung cancer patients to predict survival duration, distinguishing itself from existing prognostic assessment methods. The research team expects that this technology will contribute to the establishment of personalized treatment strategies for patients.

The core of this technology is its significance as a non-invasive prognostic assessment method based on brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and it has the potential to become a new indicator that can be used independently of existing genetic analyses or AI-based image analyses.

Key Summary

  • The St. Vincent Hospital research team has registered a US patent for survival prediction technology for lung cancer brain metastasis patients.
  • Developed a method to predict patient survival duration through the change slope of temporalis muscle thickness.
  • Expected to be an important prognostic tool for non-small cell lung cancer patients.
  • Emphasizes differentiation from existing technologies through an MRI-based non-invasive method.

Key Points

  • Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: Accounts for 85-90% of all lung cancers, with brain metastasis being a major cause of poor prognosis.
  • Change Slope of Temporalis Muscle Thickness: A new indicator that has a significant correlation with patient survival duration.
  • Non-Invasive Assessment: A method utilizing MRI, which places less burden on patients.
  • Clinical Application Potential: Expected to contribute to the establishment of personalized treatment strategies.

Why Background Matters

Lung cancer is one of the most common cancers worldwide, and while there are various treatment methods for non-small cell lung cancer, treatment tends to become difficult when brain metastasis occurs. Existing prognostic prediction methods have relied on genetic analyses or AI-based image analyses, which often are not non-invasive or require complex procedures. Professor Kim Young-il's team aimed to overcome these limitations by developing a new predictive indicator.

Why It Matters

This technology represents a significant advancement in enhancing the objectivity of prognostic predictions for lung cancer patients. Having a tool to quantitatively assess the patient's overall condition and progression of muscle wasting will greatly assist medical professionals in determining treatment directions. This can provide personalized treatment for patients and contribute to extending survival duration.
