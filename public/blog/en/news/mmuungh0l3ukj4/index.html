Iran withstands US airstrikes with 'Hormuz'... Core power IRGC takes a hardline stance

Currently, tensions between the US and Iran in the Middle East are escalating. While the airstrikes by the US and Israel have successfully targeted Iran's military infrastructure and eliminated high-ranking officials, Iran is resisting by leveraging the Strait of Hormuz as a strategic lever. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) is adopting a hardline approach, appointing a new leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, and further strengthening internal control. This situation is becoming more complicated due to issues related to the costs of war and reparations.

As the Iranian regime becomes more powerful under the IRGC, the US is intensifying diplomatic efforts to isolate Iran. However, the internal chaos in Iran and the complex interests of the international community make it difficult to predict the outcome of this war.

Key Summary

  • Iran's military base is being targeted by US and Israeli airstrikes, but Iran is resisting through the Strait of Hormuz.
  • The IRGC is establishing a new leader and continuing its hardline stance while strengthening internal control.
  • Discussions of a ceasefire between the US and Iran are complicated by issues of war costs and reparations.
  • The US is urging countries to designate the IRGC as a terrorist organization, increasing diplomatic pressure.
  • The combination of internal chaos in Iran and external pressure creates uncertainty regarding the war's direction.

Key Scenes

  • Military confrontation between the US and Iran: Attacks on Iran's military facilities and the elimination of high-ranking officials are ongoing.
  • Hardline stance of the IRGC: The IRGC is strengthening internal control and appointing a new leader.
  • War cost issues: The US war costs are estimated to be at least $12 billion, complicating ceasefire discussions due to reparations.
  • Diplomatic pressure: The US is urging countries to designate the IRGC as a terrorist organization.
  • Internal chaos: Political turmoil in Iran is impacting the direction of the war.

Why Background Matters

This conflict began with Iran's military provocations and the US's hardline responses. The US is conducting military operations to counter Iran's military threats, while Iran is trying to strengthen its position through the Strait of Hormuz. The IRGC has established itself as the military and political axis of Iran, adopting a more hardline stance internally while responding to external pressures.

Why It Matters

Iran's political changes and military responses could significantly impact stability in the Middle East. The Strait of Hormuz is a major route for global oil transportation, and Iran's military actions could have a substantial effect on international oil prices and the global economy. Additionally, if tensions between the US and Iran escalate, other countries in the region are likely to become involved.

Points to Watch

As the IRGC becomes more hardline internally, it is crucial to observe how Iran's diplomatic stance and military responses will change. In particular, analysis is needed on how US diplomatic pressure will affect Iran's policies and whether Iran's internal politics can remain stable.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The issues of war costs and reparations extend beyond mere financial interests; they could significantly impact trust and diplomatic relations between the two countries. There is a possibility that the current war will not only result in short-term military victories but also lead to long-term complexities in diplomatic relations.

Current Points to Check

It is important to continuously monitor Iran's military responses, the internal situation of the IRGC, and the unfolding of US diplomatic pressures. In particular, checking the situation in the Strait of Hormuz and its impact on international oil prices is necessary.
