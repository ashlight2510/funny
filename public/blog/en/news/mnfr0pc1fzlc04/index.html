Labor Day, which regained its name after 62 years, is now "Every Worker’s Day"

On May 1, 2023, Labor Day will be designated as a legal holiday, marking a historic change. Labor Day, which had previously applied only to certain workers, will now be established as a day that guarantees the universal right to rest for all working individuals. The Korean Confederation of Trade Unions hopes that this amendment will serve as a starting point for resolving institutional discrimination, while the Public Service Federation evaluates it as an important achievement in addressing the discrimination faced by approximately 1.2 million workers.

Now, Labor Day has taken on the significance of a day to restore the rights and dignity of all workers, rather than just being a simple holiday. However, there are voices calling for continuous efforts from both the government and workers to enhance the effectiveness of the law even after its implementation.

Key Summary

  • May 1 is designated as Labor Day, a legal holiday.
  • It transforms into a day that guarantees the universal right to rest for all workers.
  • The Korean Confederation of Trade Unions, the Public Service Federation, and the Korean Trade Union Confederation emphasize the importance of this amendment.
  • Workers who were discriminated against based on their employment type will now benefit from paid holidays.
  • Continuous efforts are needed to enhance effectiveness after the law's implementation.

Key Scenes

  • Designated as a legal holiday: May 1 will be implemented as a legal holiday for the first time.
  • Inclusion of all workers: Public servants, teachers, and special employment workers, who were previously excluded, are now included.
  • Starting point for resolving discrimination: It is seen as the first step in eliminating institutional discrimination.
  • Restoration of workers' rights: A significant turning point in restoring the rights and dignity of workers.
  • Need for continuous efforts: There are calls for ongoing efforts from both the government and workers even after the law's implementation.

Why Background Matters

The designation of Labor Day as a legal holiday is a historic event that regains its original name after 62 years. Until now, Labor Day was guaranteed as a paid holiday only for private sector workers covered by the Labor Standards Act, while public servants and special employment workers were excluded. Discrimination among workers based on their legal status and employment type has occurred in this context, and this amendment can be seen as an important measure to address such inequalities.

Why It Matters

The designation of Labor Day as a legal holiday is not just about adding another day off; it represents a significant turning point in changing the perspective on labor. It will contribute to ensuring equal rights for all workers and restoring their rights and dignity. In particular, substantial changes are expected for many workers who have faced discrimination based on their legal status.

Personal Comment

In my view, this amendment is a very meaningful change that reflects the voices of previously marginalized workers. However, many challenges still remain in the actual field. It is important to note that the implementation of the law does not solve all problems, so both the government and workers must work together. Especially, it is essential for workers to raise their awareness of their rights.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Even though this amendment guarantees equal rights for all workers, there are still many cases where the law is not properly enforced in the field. For example, while paid holidays for Labor Day apply to businesses with fewer than five employees, there are many instances where this is not adhered to. Therefore, efforts to inspect and improve the actual conditions on the ground are necessary in addition to legal measures.

Points to Check Now

  • Changes after the implementation of Labor Day law: It is important to closely observe how workers' rights and rest rights change after May 1.
  • Reality of law enforcement: The government needs to monitor how the law regarding Labor Day is enforced, especially in small businesses.
