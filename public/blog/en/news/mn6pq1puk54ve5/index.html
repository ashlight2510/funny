US jury: "YouTube and Instagram are responsible for causing social media addiction in teenagers... Pay 60 million dollars in damages"

Recently, a jury in a US court ruled that Meta and Google, specifically Instagram and YouTube, are responsible for causing social media addiction in teenagers, issuing a damages verdict of 6 million dollars, or about 9 billion won. The plaintiff, a woman in her 20s named Kaylee G M, filed a lawsuit citing depression and physical disabilities she experienced from using these platforms since childhood, claiming that the social media companies intentionally designed their platforms to increase addiction. This case is expected to spark social discussions about the design of social media platforms and mental health issues among teenagers, going beyond a simple personal lawsuit.

This ruling directly addresses the mental harm to teenagers and the accountability of social media, and it is anticipated to have a significant impact on similar lawsuits in the future. The court decided that Meta would bear 70% and Google 30% of the damages, which can be interpreted as a signal that social media companies must take user safety and mental health more seriously in the future.

Why Background Matters

This case arises amid growing controversy over the mental health issues of teenagers and the addictive nature of social media. In recent years, studies have shown a correlation between social media use and psychological problems among teenagers, leading to an increase in lawsuits from parents and educational authorities holding companies accountable. Notably, there are currently over 2,000 similar lawsuits ongoing across the United States, indicating that this issue is not merely an individual problem.

Why It Matters

This ruling reflects a societal demand to clarify the responsibilities of social media companies regarding their impact on teenage users, going beyond just one individual's case. This could lead companies to consider user safety and mental health more seriously, potentially resulting in policy changes aimed at protecting teenagers.

Responsibility of Social Media Operators

It is noteworthy that social media companies have been accused of designing their platforms to increase addiction in order to optimize user experience. This goes beyond a simple business strategy and raises ethical responsibilities regarding users' mental health. If such responsibilities are legally recognized, companies may face the need to reevaluate their existing profit models.

Hidden Variables to Consider

A point to note in this ruling is the counterargument from Meta and Google that the plaintiff's mental health issues are unrelated to social media. Companies may present various arguments to evade legal responsibility, which could become significant variables in future similar lawsuits.

Current Points to Check

It is important to pay attention to ongoing similar lawsuits. Particularly, the fact that hundreds of lawsuits are pending across the United States, including California, foreshadows how legal precedents may be established in the future. This trend is likely to pressure social media companies to strengthen their policies for user protection.
