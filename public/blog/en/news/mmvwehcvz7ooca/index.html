‘Juvenile Delinquent’ Age Reduction Issue, First Public Discussion at Government Level

Recently, the government held its first public discussion on the issue of lowering the age of juvenile delinquents. During this meeting, opinions were exchanged regarding the increase in the crime rate among juvenile delinquents and the limitations of the protective measures under the juvenile law. There were divided opinions on the proposal to lower the age standard for juvenile delinquents from 14 to 13, which has been maintained since 1953, and discussions on future policy directions are expected to become more active.

This issue carries significance beyond a simple age adjustment. In-depth discussions are needed on how to harmonize the two pillars of crime prevention and youth protection. This forum revealed the conflict between social demands for lowering the age of juvenile delinquents and the legal effectiveness of such measures, and it is expected to have a significant impact on future policy decision-making processes.

  • The government held a public discussion on the issue of lowering the age of juvenile delinquents.

  • Supporters of lowering the age argue for crime prevention and responsiveness to public sentiment.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The juvenile delinquent system applies to minors aged 10 to 14 who commit crimes, as stipulated in the Criminal Act enacted in 1953. In recent years, the crime rate among juvenile delinquents has surged, leading to active discussions about the necessity and effectiveness of this system. Notably, the number of juvenile delinquents committing crimes increased from 9,606 in 2020 to 20,814 in 2024, which is a reason for the government and society to take this issue seriously.

Against this backdrop, the government held a public discussion to gather citizens' opinions and seek comprehensive solutions to the issue of juvenile crime. However, questions have been raised about whether simply lowering the age will effectively prevent crime.

Why It Matters

This issue goes beyond merely adjusting legal age standards; it encompasses the prevention of youth crime and social responsibility. In relation to the increasing crime rate, society is posing fundamental questions about what protection and guidance should be provided to youth. This discussion will have significant implications for future juvenile crime policies, and a social consensus on how to establish standards of legal responsibility is needed.

Personal Thoughts

In my view, the discussion on lowering the age for juvenile delinquents is not a problem that can be solved merely by lowering the age standard. To prevent crime, education and social support for youth are essential. A system is needed to help juvenile offenders reintegrate into society, which can actually lower crime rates.

Additionally, while discussions on lowering the age are ongoing, there should be in-depth discussions on what punishments and protections are appropriate for juvenile offenders. It is crucial to remember that this requires an approach that considers the human rights and rehabilitation of youth, rather than just criminal punishment.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect that may be overlooked in this discussion is the importance of educational and therapeutic programs for crime prevention. Even if the age is lowered, if fundamental measures to prevent youth from committing crimes are not established, simple legal adjustments may be meaningless. In this regard, policymakers should put more effort into establishing practical programs and support systems for crime prevention.

Points to Check Now

  • Follow-up actions and policy directions regarding the government's discussion on lowering the age of juvenile delinquents.
  • Current status of educational and social support programs for preventing juvenile delinquent crime.
  • Various opinions raised during the public opinion gathering process and responses to them.

FAQ

Q1: What changes will occur if the age for juvenile delinquents is lowered?
A1: If the age is lowered, more youth will be classified as juvenile delinquents, and the legal responsibilities applied to them may be strengthened. However, the effectiveness in preventing crime is not clear.

Q2: What are the reasons for the increase in juvenile delinquent crime rates?
A2: Various factors contribute, including the social environment of youth, lack of education, and changes in perceptions of crime.

Q3: What measures are needed besides lowering the age?
A3: Educational programs for crime prevention, support from families and communities, and rehabilitation programs for youth are necessary.

Q4: How long will this discussion continue?
A4: The Ministry of Gender Equality and Family plans to hold the second public forum in mid-April, and subsequent policy decisions will be made through a process of gathering public opinions.
