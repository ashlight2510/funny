Increased Transportation Revenue and Financial Support? Key Issues for Seoul's Buses

March 17, 2026 · 한겨레 · View source

Original Article at a Glance Title: Transportation revenue increased, but so did financial support?…“Seoul City, no measures for bus semi-public operation” Source: Hankyoreh After the introduction of the bus semi-public operation system in Seoul, financial input has significantly increased, but the service has actually regressed, according to criticism. The Citizens' Coalition for Economic Justice (CCEJ) held a press conference on the 17th at the CCEJ auditorium in Dongsoong-dong, Jongno-gu, Seoul, to report on the operational status of the bus semi-public operation system and pointed out the issues with the system. Read the original article

Issues with Seoul's Bus Semi-Public Operation Despite Increased Transportation Revenue

Criticism of the bus semi-public operation system implemented by Seoul City is growing. The Citizens' Coalition for Economic Justice (CCEJ) claims that while financial support is increasing, the actual service is regressing. The semi-public operation system allows private companies to operate buses, but if a deficit occurs, local governments compensate for it. However, CCEJ pointed out that contrary to the intention of securing publicness, this system has led to lax financial management and a decline in citizen services.

Key Summary

  • Seoul's bus semi-public operation has seen increased financial support, but service quality has declined.
  • Transportation revenue has increased, but financial support remains at a high level.
  • CCEJ analyzes that delays in deficit settlement and lending practices are problematic.
  • While routes and stops have increased, the number of operations has decreased, potentially increasing citizen wait times.

Key Scenes

  • Increase in Financial Support: A sharp rise from 291.5 billion won in 2019 to 891.5 billion won in 2023.
  • Increase in Transportation Revenue: Growth from 1.3 trillion won in 2019 to 1.53 trillion won in 2024.
  • Decline in Service: Although the number of routes and stops has increased, the distance and frequency of operations have decreased.
  • Opaque Financial Management: Practices of unpaid bills lead to financial distortions and increased burden on citizens.
  • Concerns Over Increased Citizen Wait Times: The increase in dispatch intervals may lead to longer wait times.

Why Background Matters

The bus semi-public operation system was introduced in 2004, allowing private companies to operate buses while local governments compensate for deficits to secure publicness. However, in recent years, the involvement of private equity funds and lax financial management have become problematic, and CCEJ criticizes Seoul City for not presenting improvement measures. While Seoul City has announced plans to promote financial and public service innovations starting in 2024, CCEJ argues that there has been no substantial change.

Why It Matters

This issue is not just about Seoul's bus operation methods. It directly relates to citizens' everyday means of transportation, and the increase in wait times and decline in service quality directly affect citizens' quality of life. Furthermore, if the finances supported by taxes are not managed transparently, it could lead to a loss of citizen trust. Ultimately, this matter transcends mere transportation issues and leads to discussions about social trust and the quality of public services.

Points of Interest

In my view, the most critical point raised by CCEJ is the 'practice of unpaid bills.' This not only distorts the flow of financial support but can also undermine financial soundness in the long run. If deficit settlements are delayed, bus companies may resort to loans to address urgent needs, which ultimately comes back to citizens in the form of taxes. Unless this structural problem is resolved, the quality of bus services in Seoul is likely to continue to deteriorate.

Hidden Variables to Consider

As mentioned in the article, while the number of routes and stops has increased, the actual wait times for citizens may also increase, which can be easily overlooked. Many people may superficially be satisfied with the increase in routes, but it is important to recognize that service quality is declining. Additionally, it should be noted that the increase in financial support is not solely due to the deficits of bus companies. This prompts a reconsideration of how citizens' taxes are utilized.

Points to Check Now

We should pay attention to what specific measures Seoul City will take to address the issues with the bus semi-public operation system. It is particularly important to examine the specifics and feasibility of the financial, public service, and service innovations that will be implemented starting in 2024. Citizens should remain interested in this issue and continuously monitor whether real changes are being made by Seoul City.
