5 Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Credit Cards

Credit cards are a convenient payment method, but if used incorrectly, they can lead to significant financial burdens. It is important to avoid common mistakes that many people encounter while using credit cards. This article presents frequent mistakes made when using credit cards and their solutions to help readers use their cards wisely.

1. The Risks of Exceeding Your Credit Limit

Exceeding your credit limit is a common mistake that can lead to additional costs and negatively impact your credit score. For example, if you have a credit limit of 1 million won and you spend 1.1 million won, you will incur fees for exceeding the limit, and your credit score may drop.

Action Tips

  • Know Your Limit: Regularly check your card limit and plan your spending each month.
  • Budget Management: Set a monthly spending budget and be careful not to exceed it.

Checkpoint

  • Since interest rates for exceeding limits vary by card issuer, make it a habit to check these rates and your limits in advance.

2. The Trap of Paying Only the Minimum Amount

Paying only the minimum amount due is a common mistake that increases debt. For instance, if you have a balance of 500,000 won and pay only the minimum of 100,000 won, interest will accrue on the remaining 400,000 won.

Action Tips

  • Pay Off the Full Balance: Make it a habit to pay off the full balance each month. It’s advisable to settle your balance before the due date to reduce interest burdens.

Checkpoint

  • Use the interest calculator provided by your card issuer to estimate the interest you may incur.

3. The Importance of Utilizing Card Points and Benefits

Many people overlook the proper use of card points and benefits. Effectively utilizing points can provide additional advantages.

Action Tips

  • Understand Benefits: Familiarize yourself with the benefits offered by your card issuer and earn extra points when making purchases at specific merchants.

Checkpoint

  • Check the expiration dates of your points and regularly plan how to use them.

4. Neglecting Credit Card Transaction Management

Failing to manage credit card transactions can lead to unexpected expenses. Many people overlook their transaction history, resulting in exceeding their budget.

Action Tips

  • Analyze Transactions: Develop a habit of organizing and analyzing your monthly transaction history. It is important to identify where your spending is high.

Checkpoint

  • Regularly check your card statements and contact your card issuer immediately if you notice any unusual transactions.

5. Precautions When Cancelling a Credit Card

Cancelling a credit card can affect your credit score. It is important to be cautious as your credit score may drop after cancellation.

Action Tips

  • Check Your Credit Score: Before cancelling, check your credit score to understand the potential impact of the cancellation.

Checkpoint

  • Ensure that there are no remaining balances after cancellation, and if there are, it is advisable to pay them off before cancelling.

Checklist: Things to Check When Using a Credit Card

  1. Check your credit limit
  2. Pay off the full balance each month
  3. Understand card points and benefits
  4. Organize transaction history
  5. Check your credit score before cancelling a card

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  1. What problems arise if I exceed my credit limit?

    • Exceeding your limit can lead to additional fees and a drop in your credit score.

  2. How can I efficiently use card points?

    • Understand the benefits provided by your card issuer and check expiration dates to use them at the right time.

  3. How does cancelling a credit card affect my credit score?

    • Cancelling a card can negatively impact your credit score, so it is important to check your score before cancelling.

To use credit cards wisely, it is crucial to avoid the mistakes mentioned above and conduct regular checks. Start by checking your credit limit now and develop the habit of paying off your full balance each month. This will help you reduce unnecessary costs and manage your finances better.
