Old Gwangjin District Office Transformed into Youth Dance Class and Intangible Heritage Transmission Center

The old office space of Gwangjin District in Seoul is being transformed into a youth dance classroom and an intangible heritage transmission center, reborn as a new cultural space for local residents. This remodeling reflects the district office's commitment to utilizing vacant spaces before the development of the office site begins in earnest. Various programs for youth and seniors are expected to contribute to meeting the cultural needs of the community.

This change will not only recycle the space but also serve as an opportunity to enhance communication and cultural exchange among local residents. In particular, youth will have the chance to learn dance under the guidance of professional instructors, while seniors will enjoy leisure activities in a Go (baduk) resting area, strengthening intergenerational connections.

Key Summary

  • The old Gwangjin District Office is being transformed into a youth dance classroom and an intangible heritage transmission center.
  • Various programs will be provided for youth and seniors.
  • Remodeling cost: 114.55 million KRW.
  • The youth dance classroom will be guided by professional instructors starting in April.
  • A space for the inheritance of traditional culture will also be established.

Key Highlights

  • Youth Cultural Space: Providing a space for youth to learn and engage in dance.
  • Intergenerational Communication: Creating a Go resting area for seniors and a study room for youth.
  • Inheritance of Traditional Culture: Strengthening traditional culture education through the intangible heritage transmission center.
  • Community Participation: Operating cultural programs that residents can directly participate in.
  • Utilization of Vacant Space: Effectively using vacant space before the development of the office site.

Why Background Matters

This project is in line with the district office's policy to utilize its vacant space as a cultural facility for local residents. Given that the existing office space had been vacant for a long time, discussions on how to utilize this space were necessary. The establishment of programs for youth and seniors can be seen as a reflection of the cultural demands of local residents.

Why It Matters

This change can serve as an important catalyst for promoting cultural revitalization in the community, going beyond mere space recycling. By providing opportunities for youth to engage in cultural and artistic activities, it will positively impact their emotional and social development. Additionally, communication between seniors and youth is expected to enhance understanding and respect across generations.

Personal Comment

In my view, this remodeling presents a great opportunity to provide tangible benefits to local residents. The youth dance classroom is expected to attract many young participants, and the establishment of a stage for them to showcase their talents is a positive aspect. However, for these changes to be sustained and developed, active participation and interest from the residents are essential.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One limitation of this change is that it represents a temporary utilization of space until the development of the office site begins in earnest. There needs to be a plan for how this space will be operated in the long term, and continuous improvements reflecting residents' opinions should be made.

Points to Check Now

  • Specific details and schedule of the youth dance classroom program.
  • Educational programs offered at the intangible heritage transmission center.
  • Various cultural programs that residents can participate in.

FAQ

Q1: When does the youth dance classroom start?
A1: The dance classroom program guided by professional instructors will begin in April.

Q2: What kind of education is provided at the intangible heritage transmission center?
A2: Training for the national intangible heritage ‘Seodo Sori Baebangi Gut’ will be conducted.

Q3: Until when will this space be operational?
A3: It will be operational until the office site is fully developed.

Q4: How can residents utilize this space?
A4: Residents can participate in various programs such as the youth dance classroom and the seniors' Go resting area.

It is now important to observe how this space will positively impact residents and the future changes and developments that will occur.
