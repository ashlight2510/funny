The Complex Relationship Between the Iran War and the Request to Postpone the U.S.-China Summit

Recently, President Donald Trump requested a one-month postponement of the summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping due to the war with Iran. This request seems to stem from the burdens associated with the prolonged U.S.-Iran war, and President Trump expressed his intention to fulfill his responsibilities as Commander-in-Chief by postponing the meeting. This situation is expected to have significant economic and diplomatic impacts on U.S.-China relations.

This incident carries implications beyond merely postponing a summit between the two countries. Adjusting the U.S. diplomatic schedule amid the prolonged U.S.-Iran war could have major repercussions on the future international situation. In particular, the intertwined complexities of trade conflicts and Taiwan issues between the U.S. and China could amplify the fallout.

Reasons to Consider the Background

As tensions escalate between the U.S. and Iran, President Trump feels the need to emphasize his role as Commander-in-Chief. In this context, postponing the U.S.-China summit can be viewed as a significant adjustment to U.S. foreign policy. The summit was supposed to finalize the "one-year tariff truce" agreed upon last year in Busan, but the uncertainties surrounding the U.S.-Iran war are shaking this plan.

Moreover, China is closely monitoring how this summit will impact its strategy towards the U.S. If the summit is postponed, there may be more room for discussions on sensitive issues like Taiwan.
