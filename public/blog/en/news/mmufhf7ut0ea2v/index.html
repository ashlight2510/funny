Remembering socialist Kim Cheol-soo, who spent 13 years in prison for the anti-Japanese movement

Kim Cheol-soo was a socialist who dedicated his life to the independence movement during the Japanese colonial period and endured 13 years in prison. Even after liberation, he contributed to the national movement. To honor his life and achievements, a memorial exhibition of his calligraphy works is being held at the Jeonil Building in Gwangju, reflecting on his philosophy and the meaning of his life. Kim Cheol-soo carried out the independence movement based on socialist ideology, and his last writing, 'Daeeungyeonghwa', well illustrates the principles of his life.

This exhibition is not merely an homage to an individual but an opportunity to explore the legacy he left behind and the questions it poses to contemporary society. Kim Cheol-soo's story transcends a simple historical account, prompting a reevaluation of current social values and the significance of the independence movement.
