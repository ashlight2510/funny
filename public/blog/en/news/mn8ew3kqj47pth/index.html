Director Jeon Seung-il's Retrial and the Contradictions of the National Security Law

Director Jeon Seung-il has filed for a retrial 37 years after being convicted of violating the National Security Law. He pointed out the contradiction of still having a guilty verdict despite being recognized by the state as related to the democratization movement, asserting that the state must acknowledge its own mistakes. This retrial is seen as an important process for judging the illegal exercise of state power in the past and healing the wounds of modern history.

This case goes beyond an individual legal issue, shedding light on the relationship between the state and individuals, as well as the historical context of the democratization process. Director Jeon's retrial raises important questions about how society remembers and heals from the history of the democratization movement and the damages it caused.

Why Background Matters

Director Jeon Seung-il produced a large banner as part of the national liberation movement in the 1980s, which was deemed an anti-state act by the National Security Planning Agency. At that time, he was illegally detained and subsequently convicted of violating the National Security Law. However, over time, his work has been reevaluated as part of the people's art movement, and he has been recognized as related to the democratization movement under the Democratization Compensation Act. Against this historical backdrop, Jeon asserts his innocence through the retrial, which is viewed as a process of correcting the state's historical wrongs rather than just a legal procedure.

Why It Matters

This retrial goes beyond an individual legal issue, reframing the relationship between the state and individuals. The effort to clarify the individual's suffering during the democratization process and the state's responsibilities holds significant implications for future social justice and historical healing. Additionally, the fact that past wrongs continue to impact the present conveys an important message to society as a whole.
