Iran only opens Hormuz to yuan trading countries... "Negotiating with 8 countries"

Iran has proposed to guarantee safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz only for ships carrying oil traded in Chinese yuan. Iran is currently in negotiations with eight countries regarding this condition, and it appears that some countries have responded positively to this condition. This move is interpreted as part of Iran's strategy to weaken the dominance of the US dollar in international oil trading.

Iran's recent action is a significant issue that could impact not only maritime passage but also the currency structure of international energy trading. The Strait of Hormuz is strategically located, with about 20% of the world's oil passing through it, making Iran's decision likely to have a major ripple effect on the global energy market.

Why Background Matters

Iran's recent actions have emerged amid heightened tensions with the United States. The US has been intensifying sanctions related to Iran's nuclear program, and Iran has been increasing military pressure in the Strait of Hormuz in response. In this context, Iran seems to be strategizing to enhance its economic autonomy through yuan trading and to elevate its position in the international community. Notably, the cases of India and Pakistan purchasing Russian oil in yuan suggest a positive reaction to Iran's proposal.

Why It Matters

Iran's actions could influence not just maritime passage but also the currency structure of international oil trading. Currently, over 80% of global oil transactions are conducted in US dollars, but Iran's yuan trading proposal has the potential to disrupt this pattern. This could weaken US economic influence and provide an opportunity to strengthen China's economic dominance.

Points to Note

In my view, Iran's yuan trading proposal carries significance beyond mere passage permissions. It could represent a crucial turning point capable of changing the paradigm of the international economy. If Iran's actions proceed successfully, other countries may also consider yuan trading. Therefore, it is essential to closely observe the impact of Iran's moves on the future of the international economy.

Hidden Variables to Consider

While there is a positive response to Iran's actions, there are also skeptical views within China regarding Iran's moves. This skepticism stems from concerns that Iran could draw China into broader geopolitical tensions. As a result, there is uncertainty about how Iran's proposal will actually unfold.

Current Points to Check

It is important to examine the identities of the eight countries currently negotiating with Iran and their reasons for responding to yuan trading. This information could provide insights into the future structure of international energy trading.
