From 190,000 won to 10,000 won... 'Battery Uncle' has pushed 'Geumyang' to effectively face delisting

Recently, it has been reported that Geumyang's stock price has plummeted, effectively entering the delisting process. Geumyang, which was at the center of the 'secondary battery' investment boom from 2022 to 2023, is facing a serious financial crisis after receiving disclaimed opinions from auditors for two consecutive years. As a result, 230,000 small shareholders are at risk of incurring significant losses. The stock price, which once soared to 190,000 won, has now fallen to the 10,000 won range.

Geumyang's stock price surged due to expectations for battery development, but those hopes have collapsed due to production failures and financial difficulties. In particular, the external auditor raised 'significant doubts,' accelerating the delisting process. This situation has particularly shocked small shareholders, and their losses are expected to be substantial.

Key Summary

  • Geumyang has effectively entered the delisting process after receiving disclaimed opinions from auditors for two consecutive years.
  • The stock price has plummeted from a peak of 190,000 won to the current 10,000 won range, putting small shareholders at risk of significant losses.
  • The external auditor's report raised questions about the company's sustainability and revealed serious financial issues related to debt.

Key Scenes

  • Disclaimed Opinions: Facing delisting risk due to disclaimed opinions for two consecutive years.
  • Stock Price Plunge: Dropped from 190,000 won to 10,000 won, leading to significant losses for small shareholders.
  • Deteriorating Financial Condition: Current liabilities exceed current assets, raising questions about the company's viability.
  • Battery Production Failure: Actual sales have been poor, contrary to expectations.
  • Legal Disputes: Lawsuit for loan repayment against Busan Bank and notice of forced auction.

Reasons to Look at the Background

Geumyang was once a company that garnered great expectations in the secondary battery market. The recruitment of Park Soon-hyuk, known as 'Battery Uncle,' as the promotional director attracted investor interest, leading to a surge in stock prices. However, in reality, the company failed in battery production and faced serious financial crises due to debt issues. This situation is a major reason for Geumyang's entry into the delisting process.

Why It Matters

This incident is not just a problem for Geumyang alone. It could significantly impact the overall credibility of the secondary battery industry and investor sentiment. Especially, small shareholders are likely to suffer considerable losses, making it important to observe how they respond. Additionally, legal disputes or financial solutions during the delisting process could set important precedents for similar companies in the future.

Points to Pay Attention To

Geumyang's delisting process is directly affecting small shareholders. These individuals invested in the belief that Geumyang's stock price would rise, but they now face significant losses. Furthermore, Geumyang's debt issues could raise awareness among other companies. It is essential to consider how excessive expectations for the secondary battery industry might impact corporate sustainability.

Hidden Variables to Watch

Geumyang's appeal against delisting is scheduled to proceed until the 13th of next month. The outcome of this process is uncertain, but if the appeal is accepted, it could provide an opportunity for improving the company's financial situation. However, given the current serious debt issues and financial state, expecting a positive outcome seems difficult.

Points to Check Now
