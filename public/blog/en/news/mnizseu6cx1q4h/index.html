Japanese Ship Passes Through Hormuz for the First Time After the Iran War

Recently, a Japanese liquefied natural gas (LNG) ship has passed through the Strait of Hormuz for the first time since the war with Iran. The vessel in question is the 'Soha LNG', operated by the Japanese shipping company Mitsui O.S.K. Lines, and this occurred despite Iran's ban on vessels passing through the strait. The Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs secured the safety of the ship through consultations with Iran, and the passage of the Japanese vessel is seen as an important case indicating a potential change in Iran's management policy of the strait.

This incident occurred amid diplomatic tensions between Japan and Iran, and the fact that the Japanese ship was able to pass safely can be interpreted as a deliberate action by Iran. Iran has recently strengthened its management policies regarding vessels passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and it is noteworthy whether the passage of the Japanese ship reflects a new maritime policy from Iran.

  • The Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs secured the safety of the vessel through consultations with Iran.
  • There is a possibility of a change in Iran's management policy of the strait.

Why Background Matters

As the war between Iran, the U.S., and Israel intensified, the Strait of Hormuz was effectively blocked. Iran has strictly managed the passage of vessels from allied countries of the U.S. and Israel. However, the passage of Japanese and French vessels indicates that Iran is showing a more flexible attitude towards ships from friendly nations. This is also noteworthy as Iran appears to be pursuing new policies, such as imposing transit fees.

Why It Matters

This incident serves as an important indicator of how diplomatic relations between Japan and Iran are changing. Additionally, the Strait of Hormuz is a crucial global energy transport route, so any changes in Iran's maritime policy could significantly impact international economics and security. The passage of the Japanese vessel raises the possibility that other countries may also benefit from freer passage in the future.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The change in Iran's management policy of the strait may not be limited to just the passage of Japanese vessels. Iran is exploring various options to strengthen its diplomatic standing even amid conflicts with the U.S., which could affect its relations with other countries in the future. Furthermore, even though the passage of the Japanese vessel was secured, it should not be overlooked that the Strait of Hormuz remains a region with inherent risks.
