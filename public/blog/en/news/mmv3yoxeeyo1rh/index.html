Judicial Review, Constitutional Values, and the Future of the System

With the recent implementation of the constitutional complaint system, requests for judicial review have surged. As of the 16th, 68 cases have been received since its implementation on the 12th. While some are considered legitimate requests for fundamental rights remedies, there is growing concern over the number of requests from sex offenders and threats. The Constitutional Court emphasizes the need for a pre-review system to prevent the abuse of lawsuits, which will be a crucial factor in determining the nature of future judicial reviews.

The content discussed in this article goes beyond mere changes in legal procedures. It presents an important opportunity to assess how the protection of fundamental rights and constitutional values can be practically implemented. Notably, through the examples of Germany and Spain, we learn that the citation rates for judicial reviews are only around 1%, suggesting that a similar situation could arise in Korea.

Key Summary

  • Following the implementation of the judicial review system, 68 requests have been received, with some related to fundamental rights remedies, but many are also from non-urgent requests.
  • The Constitutional Court emphasizes the importance of a pre-review system to prevent the abuse of lawsuits.
  • The low citation rates of judicial reviews in Germany and Spain highlight a potential similar situation in Korea.

Key Scenes

  • Surge in judicial review requests: 68 cases received in the first week of implementation.
  • Mix of fundamental rights remedies and non-urgent requests: includes claims from sex offenders and threats.
  • Need for a pre-review system: Essential for the Constitutional Court to establish criteria for case selection.
  • Comparison of citation rates in Germany and Spain: 1.18% and 1.4% respectively, raising concerns about a similar situation in Korea.
  • Discussion on constitutional values and the identity of the system: A need for discourse on the essence of judicial reviews.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The judicial review system was introduced as a legal mechanism to remedy violations of fundamental rights. However, the cases in Germany and Spain illustrate how this system can be abused. Germany has improved the quality of cases through a pre-review system, while Spain has amended laws to address case backlog issues. Such trends are likely to be repeated in Korea.

Why It Matters

This news raises important questions about how to protect citizens' fundamental rights, not just a change in legal procedures. Depending on how the Constitutional Court selects and processes cases, fundamental rights may be adequately protected, or legal confusion may deepen. This is a matter that directly affects all citizens.

Personal Comment

In my view, the introduction of the judicial review system could be a positive change for the protection of fundamental rights, but if abused, it could lead to serious side effects. Particularly concerning is the use of this system by sex offenders seeking legal remedies, which could undermine the purpose of protecting fundamental rights. Therefore, it seems crucial for the Constitutional Court to establish pre-review criteria.

Hidden Variables to Watch

There is concern that discussions regarding the essence and identity of judicial reviews are not sufficiently taking place. Although the system has been introduced, a lack of clear standards and direction could ultimately lead to situations where citizens do not receive legal protection.

Points to Check Now

  • Discussions on how Korea's judicial review system may develop through the examples of Germany and Spain.
  • Legal mechanisms and policy proposals to prevent the abuse of the judicial review system.

FAQ

Q: What is the judicial review system?
A: The judicial review system is a legal procedure for requesting remedies from the Constitutional Court when fundamental rights guaranteed by the constitution are violated.

Q: How do Germany and Spain operate their judicial review systems?
A: Both countries have implemented pre-review systems to enhance the quality of cases and strictly set criteria to prevent abuse.

Q: What are examples of abuse of the judicial review system?
A: Cases where sex offenders or threats file judicial reviews against their sentences fall into this category.
