During the period of conflict between doctors and the government, the number of cancer surgeries in large hospitals decreased by up to 32%... “The dependence on residents is revealed”

Recently, as the conflict between doctors and the government intensified, the number of cancer surgeries in large hospitals has drastically decreased. Following the mass resignation of residents in 2024, the number of major cancer surgeries in tertiary hospitals decreased by up to 32%, leading to patients moving to other medical institutions or delays in surgeries. This change clearly reveals how much the South Korean healthcare system relies on residents.

This article goes beyond the mere reduction in numbers and highlights significant issues regarding the structural problems of the South Korean healthcare system. The consequences of the mass resignation of residents are having a tangible impact on patients, which will pose significant challenges to the continuity of healthcare services in the future.

Key Summary

  • After the mass resignation of residents in 2024, the number of major cancer surgeries in large hospitals decreased from an average of 22% to 32%.
  • A "balloon effect" where patients move to other hospitals was observed, but it did not sufficiently compensate for the decrease in surgeries.
  • The high dependence on residents emphasizes the need to maintain service continuity even in a healthcare workforce crisis.

Key Scenes

  • Decrease in cancer surgeries in large hospitals: colorectal cancer 22%, stomach cancer 23%, lung cancer 32%, breast cancer 20%, thyroid cancer 32%.
  • Although the number of surgeries in general hospitals increased, it did not sufficiently absorb the decrease in tertiary hospitals.
  • Many patients are unable to receive surgeries or face delays, raising concerns about the continuity of healthcare services.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This issue arose as the conflict between doctors and the government intensified alongside the mass resignation of residents. The shortage of residents has led to a sharp decline in cancer surgeries in tertiary hospitals, revealing how much the South Korean healthcare system relies on residents. The research team analyzed data from 2019 to 2024 to confirm these changes.

Why Is This Important?

This situation reveals fundamental problems within the South Korean healthcare system beyond just the decrease in the number of surgeries. The shortage of residents directly affects patients and can significantly impact the quality and continuity of healthcare services. Therefore, urgent fundamental measures are needed to address this issue.

Points of Attention

In my view, the most critical aspect of this incident is the issue of dependence on residents. The shortage of healthcare personnel poses serious concerns that this short-term crisis could lead to long-term deterioration in the quality of healthcare services. If patients continue to be unable to receive surgeries, it will severely undermine the trust in the healthcare system.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The study found no statistically significant changes in liver cancer surgeries. This is likely due to the high complexity of liver cancer surgeries, making it difficult for patients to move to other medical institutions. Therefore, this aspect is something that general patients may overlook.

Current Checkpoints

  • There is a need to continuously monitor the impact of the mass resignation of residents on healthcare services.
  • If the phenomenon of patients being unable to receive surgeries persists, changes in healthcare policy may be necessary.

FAQ

Q: What is the mass resignation of residents?
A: The mass resignation of residents refers to the phenomenon where a large number of residents resign, which significantly impacts the continuity of healthcare services.

Q: How are patients affected?
A: Due to the decrease in the number of surgeries, many patients are unable to receive surgeries or face delays, which can directly affect their health.

Q: What measures are needed to address this issue?
A: Structural changes are needed to secure healthcare personnel and reduce dependence on residents.

Through these issues, I want to emphasize the need to understand the structural problems of the South Korean healthcare system and the necessity for discussions on future measures. It is time for active discussions on this issue to take place.
