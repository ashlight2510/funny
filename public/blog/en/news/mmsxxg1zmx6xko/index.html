Exchange rate exceeds 1500 won in weekly trading… “If high oil prices persist, the 1500 won level will be established”

Recently, the won-dollar exchange rate has surpassed 1500 won. This is the first occurrence in 17 years since the 2009 global financial crisis, primarily due to the Middle East situation and rising international oil prices. While foreign exchange authorities are striving to keep the exchange rate below the psychological barrier of 1500 won, the likelihood of the exchange rate becoming entrenched in the 1500 won range increases if high oil prices persist. Experts warn that this situation could also impact the interest rate policy of the U.S. Federal Reserve.

The recent rise in the exchange rate is noteworthy not only for its implications for currency fluctuations but also for its significant ripple effects on the overall economy. In particular, if high oil prices continue, inflationary pressures will increase, affecting both consumers and businesses.

Key Points

  • The won-dollar exchange rate exceeding 1500 won during trading is a first in 17 years.
  • The Middle East situation and high oil prices are the main causes of the exchange rate increase.
  • Despite interventions by foreign exchange authorities, it is difficult to curb the rising trend of the exchange rate.
  • If high oil prices persist, inflationary pressures may increase, potentially affecting the Fed's interest rate policy.
  • The intensity of government intervention will be a crucial variable in the future trend of the exchange rate.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The recent rise in the exchange rate is attributed to the instability in the Middle East, particularly the heightened tensions between Iran and the U.S. Following airstrikes in Iran, international oil prices surged, increasing demand for the dollar as a safe asset. This situation raises exchange rate volatility and directly impacts the South Korean economy. Unlike last year, the reasons for the rising exchange rate differ, making it challenging for foreign exchange authorities to intervene effectively.

Why It Matters

The rise in the exchange rate affects not only the foreign exchange market but also significantly impacts our daily lives. An increase in import prices is likely to raise consumer prices as well. Additionally, it can affect corporate profitability, ultimately having a negative impact on employment and economic growth. Therefore, this rise in the exchange rate should be viewed as a warning not just for short-term economic issues but for long-term economic stability.

My Perspective

If the exchange rate remains in the 1500 won range, it will impose a real burden on both consumers and businesses. In particular, companies that are highly dependent on imports may face increased production costs due to rising raw material prices. In this situation, the government's policy response will become crucial. However, it is concerning that the effectiveness of foreign exchange authorities' interventions may be limited. If external factors like high oil prices influence the exchange rate, government interventions may not be effective.

Hidden Variables to Consider

While high oil prices and the Middle East situation are identified as the main causes of the recent rise in the exchange rate, other economic indicators should also be examined. For example, the possibility of U.S. interest rate hikes, domestic economic growth rates, and companies' overseas investment situations are all factors that can influence the exchange rate. Therefore, the exchange rate should not be viewed solely as a change in the foreign exchange market but should be analyzed comprehensively alongside various economic indicators.

Points to Check Now

  • We need to closely monitor how the exchange rate will fluctuate if high oil prices persist.
  • Changes in foreign exchange authorities' policies or interventions are also key points to watch.
  • Continuous monitoring of consumer prices and changes in corporate production costs is necessary.
