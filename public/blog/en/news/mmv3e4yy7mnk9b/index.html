Hyundai Motor Union, Stephen Hawking, President Lee's Shared Thoughts

In the context of the Hyundai Motor Union's opposition to the introduction of robots, the statements made by President Lee Jae-myung and Dr. Stephen Hawking are drawing attention. The Hyundai Motor Union has expressed a strong stance that "not a single robot can enter the workplace without an agreement between labor and management," demanding measures to minimize potential side effects from the introduction of robots. At the same time, Dr. Stephen Hawking pointed out the issue of wealth imbalance caused by automation, and President Lee Jae-myung also warned about the imbalance between owners of production means and ordinary workers. These three parties ultimately reveal a common concern about the social changes brought about by technological advancement.

This issue goes beyond merely discussing the introduction of robots; it deeply addresses the inequality structure and job issues in modern society. This situation serves as a reminder of the impact of technological advancement on the labor market and the need for social consensus regarding it.

This situation began with Hyundai's move to introduce robot technology. Historically, the impact of technological advancement on the labor market has been significant, and this case is no exception. Therefore, social consensus and policy responses are necessary to address this issue.
