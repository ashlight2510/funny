‘Pioneer of Biotechnology’ Dr. Han Moon-hee Passes Away

Dr. Han Moon-hee, who significantly contributed to the advancement of biotechnology in Korea, passed away on the 30th. At the age of 91, he played a crucial role in establishing the technological development and industrial foundation of biotechnology in South Korea through the development of enzyme processes for isomerized sugar and the localization of raw materials for anti-tuberculosis drugs. His research achievements also had a substantial impact on the formulation of government biotechnology policies, and his career as the first director of the Genetic Engineering Center is noteworthy.

The passing of Dr. Han Moon-hee serves as an opportunity to reflect on the footprints he left in the history of Korean biotechnology, going beyond the loss of an individual. Through his achievements, we are reminded of how biotechnology in Korea has evolved, and it is a time to contemplate the future direction of the field.

Why Background Matters

After returning to Korea in 1974, Dr. Han developed an enzyme process for producing isomerized sugar, which could replace the raw sugar that was entirely imported at the time. This was a significant milestone in laying the foundation for Korean biotechnology, leading to the localization of the anti-tuberculosis drug rifampicin. These achievements not only advanced the field of biotechnology but also had a substantial impact on national health and industrial policies.
