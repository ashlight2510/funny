‘Daejeon·Chungnam’ to include Chungbuk·Sejong?…‘Chungcheong Region Integration Theory’ in a complex situation

Following the failure of the administrative integration of Daejeon and Chungnam, the integration proposals for the Chungcheong region have resurfaced. Jang Cheol-min, a member of the National Assembly running for the Daejeon mayoral election, has proposed the creation of a new administrative district called ‘New Capital Special City’ by integrating Daejeon, Sejong, and Cheongju, along with a plan to create ‘Chungcheong Special Self-Governing Province’ by combining Chungbuk and Chungnam. Additionally, President Lee Jae-myung has emphasized the need for regional integration, suggesting that discussions on integration in the Chungcheong region will intensify. However, Sejong City opposes integration, claiming the need to complete its status as the administrative capital, and there is backlash from Chungbuk against the Democratic Party's integration proposal, indicating that this process may not be smooth.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The failure of the Daejeon and Chungnam integration was influenced by various political interests and the backlash from local residents. In particular, Sejong City emphasizes its identity as the administrative capital and takes a negative stance toward integration. In this context, Jang Cheol-min's proposal for a new integration plan appears to be driven by political pressure recognizing the need for regional integration. Such discussions on integration are likely to emerge as significant issues in the local elections as well.

Why It Matters

This integration discussion is not merely about changes in administrative boundaries; it has significant implications for the local economy and residents' lives. If integration occurs, a new administrative district with a population of 2.7 million will be formed, which could lead to substantial changes in regional development and resource allocation. Conversely, if integration fails, the political instability in the region may persist, potentially negatively impacting residents' lives.

Personal Perspective

In my view, this integration discussion transcends mere political games and is a matter that directly affects residents' lives. Given the strong backlash from Sejong City and Chungbuk, it is essential to develop an integration plan that adequately reflects their opinions. If integration takes place, discussions on what tangible benefits local residents can experience are crucial.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect that may be overlooked in this integration discussion is how well political interests can reflect the voices of residents. If local residents' opinions are disregarded, even if integration occurs, the practical effects may be minimal. Additionally, the impact of integration on the local economy and how the identities of each region will harmonize are also important variables.
