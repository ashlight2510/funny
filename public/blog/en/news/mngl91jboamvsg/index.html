U.S. Department of State Expresses Concerns About Korea's Information and Communications Network Act

Recently, the U.S. Under Secretary of State for Public Diplomacy officially conveyed concerns regarding the amendment to Korea's Information and Communications Network Act. This issue was discussed during the 2nd Korea-U.S. Public Diplomacy Consultation held on the 1st in Seoul, where both countries exchanged views on public diplomacy cooperation and digital regulation. The U.S. side expressed concerns that the Korean law could excessively restrict freedom of expression, and a session was arranged to hear the Korean government's stance on this matter.

This issue goes beyond a simple amendment of a law and could lead to significant discussions that may influence the direction of the digital environment and public diplomacy between Korea and the U.S. In particular, the impact of the Information and Communications Network Act on global technological cooperation and freedom of expression could bring about significant changes in the bilateral relationship in the future.

Key Summary

  • U.S. Under Secretary of State for Public Diplomacy conveyed concerns about the amendment to Korea's Information and Communications Network Act.
  • Both countries exchanged views on public diplomacy cooperation and digital regulation.
  • The law imposes obligations on businesses like Google and Meta to delete false information.
  • The Korean government emphasized that the purpose of the law is to hold creators of false information accountable.
  • The U.S. reaffirmed its commitment to freedom of expression and privacy.

Key Scenes

  • Expression of Concern: The U.S. warned that the Information and Communications Network Act could limit freedom of expression.
  • Contents of the Law: Includes provisions imposing obligations to delete and block false manipulated information.
  • Public Diplomacy Cooperation: Both countries aim to enhance mutual understanding and trust through public diplomacy.
  • Digital Ecosystem: Emphasized the importance of a safe and reliable digital environment.
  • Upcoming Implementation of the Law: The amendment to the Information and Communications Network Act is set to be implemented in July.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The amendment to Korea's Information and Communications Network Act was enacted to address issues of false information and deepfakes, passing through the National Assembly last year. However, this law has raised concerns in several countries, including the U.S., about potentially limiting freedom of expression. In particular, the U.S. Department of State has warned that such laws could negatively impact technological cooperation. This consultation served to officially convey these concerns and foster a consensus on the digital environment between the two countries.

Why It Matters

This issue transcends mere concerns about a single country's legislation and could serve as an opportunity to re-examine regulations, freedom of expression, and the direction of international cooperation in the global digital environment. For Korea to establish itself as a global technological power, it is essential that such laws align with international standards. Additionally, the possibility that U.S. concerns may actually influence Korea's digital policies should also be considered.

Personal Perspective

In my view, the most noteworthy aspect of this discussion is how the Korean government will accommodate and adjust to U.S. concerns. Even if the law's purpose is to hold creators of false information accountable, it is crucial to ensure that the execution process does not excessively restrict freedom of expression. Building trust between the two countries requires substantial dialogue based on mutual understanding.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect that may be overlooked in this issue is the impact of Korea's Information and Communications Network Act on businesses, particularly global IT companies. If this law is implemented, these companies may experience significant changes in their operational methods in the Korean market, which could ultimately affect consumers as well. Therefore, it is important to closely monitor corporate reactions and subsequent policy adjustments.

Points to Check Now

  • Implementation Schedule of the Law: The amendment to the Information and Communications Network Act is scheduled to be implemented in July, so it is important to closely observe any additional measures or amendments from the Korean government before and after this date.
  • U.S. Response: It is essential to monitor how the U.S. government continues to respond to Korea's legislation and whether there will be any diplomatic changes as a result.
