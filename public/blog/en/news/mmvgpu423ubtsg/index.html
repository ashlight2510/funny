Decrease in Student Numbers and Seoul's Strategy for Utilizing Closed Schools

The educational environment in Seoul is undergoing rapid changes. According to the recently announced 'Five-Year Strategic Plan for the Utilization of School Relocation Sites and Closed Schools' by the Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education, it is expected that the number of students will decrease by 28% over the next six years due to a decline in the school-age population. In response to the forecast of increased school closures, the Office of Education has devised a plan to utilize closed school sites as multi-purpose facilities, with a particular focus on expanding the infrastructure for special schools.

This change represents an important opportunity not only to address the issue of a declining number of schools but also to reflect the restructuring of educational infrastructure and the educational needs of the local community. How to utilize these sites when schools close will be a significant issue in future educational policies.

Key Summary

  • The Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education has announced a five-year plan to utilize closed school sites as multi-purpose facilities in response to the declining school-age population.
  • From 2026 to 2030, 273.2 billion won will be invested to utilize closed schools as public education assets.
  • The number of students is projected to decrease from 745,815 in 2025 to 538,558 in 2031.
  • The plan prioritizes the expansion of special school infrastructure, focusing on autonomous districts that currently lack special schools.
  • The strategy for utilizing closed schools will be implemented in phases across five regions.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The school-age population in Seoul is continuously declining. The number of small schools, which was 36 in 2015, is expected to increase to 183 by 2025, signaling a need for significant changes in educational policy. The Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education is exploring ways to utilize closed schools not merely as abandoned spaces but as assets that contribute to education and the local community.

Why It Matters

This news represents a critical turning point that goes beyond the mere decrease in student numbers; it addresses the restructuring of educational infrastructure and the fulfillment of special educational needs in the local community. When schools close, how those spaces are utilized will directly impact local residents and students. In particular, the expansion of special schools can enhance educational equity and strengthen support for socially vulnerable groups.

Changes in the Educational Environment

Currently, the autonomous districts in Seoul without special schools include Dongdaemun-gu, Yangcheon-gu, Geumcheon-gu, Yeongdeungpo-gu, and Yongsan-gu. Residents in these areas are facing a lack of educational support due to the absence of special schools. Therefore, if the strategy for utilizing closed schools contributes to establishing special schools in these regions, it would greatly benefit the local residents.

Hidden Variables to Consider

A part not mentioned in the article is the specific execution plan for utilizing closed schools. For instance, the local residents' reactions to the development of closed school sites or operational issues when special schools are actually established could become significant issues in the future. Additionally, there is a need for transparency regarding how the budget, which corresponds to about 2.5% of the education budget, will be executed.

Points to Check Now

  • It is important to pay attention to how the Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education's plan for utilizing closed schools will be specifically implemented.
  • Checking the progress of special school establishment in each autonomous district is crucial.
  • It is also necessary to observe how local residents' opinions are collected and responded to.

FAQ

Q: When will the plan for utilizing closed schools be implemented?
A: It is scheduled to be implemented in phases from 2026 to 2030.

Q: What criteria will be used to establish special schools?
A: Special schools will be planned primarily in autonomous districts that currently lack special schools.

Q: How will the budget needed for utilizing closed schools be secured?
A: The plan is under consideration to create a joint fund with the Seoul City and the central government.

Q: How will local residents' opinions be reflected if schools close?
A: A process for gathering local residents' opinions will be necessary, and this will help formulate practical implementation plans.

This plan presents an opportunity for significant changes in Seoul's educational environment. It is important to closely monitor the progress moving forward.
