Yangcheon-gu Receives Minister of Health and Welfare Award: A New Model for Age-Friendly Cities

Yangcheon-gu in Seoul has received the Minister of Health and Welfare Award at the '4th Korea Healthy Age-Friendly City Policy Awards.' This recognition, awarded to only six out of 226 local governments nationwide, signals that Yangcheon-gu's age-friendly policies are gaining attention. Notably, Yangcheon-gu has received certification as an age-friendly city from the World Health Organization (WHO) and is contributing to improving the quality of life for seniors through innovative approaches such as the smart senior center system.

These achievements go beyond mere awards; they play a crucial role in presenting a new model for community welfare that aligns with the aging society. The example of Yangcheon-gu can serve as a reference for other local governments and is expected to significantly influence future policy directions.

Key Summary

  • Yangcheon-gu receives the Minister of Health and Welfare Award: Selected as one of six excellent institutions among 226 local governments nationwide.
  • Obtained WHO age-friendly city certification and established a 'Comprehensive Plan for Age-Friendly Cities.'
  • Introduced the mobile senior center system 'AI Village Living' for digital innovation in senior welfare.
  • Expanded smart senior centers and cultural arts programs.

Key Highlights

  • Selection as an Excellent Institution: Yangcheon-gu was selected as one of six excellent institutions among 226 local governments nationwide.
  • Digital Innovation: The QR code-based mobile senior center system is gaining attention as a new model for senior welfare.
  • Cultural Programs: Cultural and arts programs for vulnerable seniors enhance their quality of life.
  • Smart Senior Centers: Establishment of health management and educational environments utilizing information and communication technology.

Why Background Matters

Yangcheon-gu's age-friendly city policy was initiated as a response to the aging society. Since receiving WHO certification in 2018, the district has continuously pursued various policies for senior welfare. In particular, the operation system of senior centers utilizing digital technology encourages seniors' participation and enhances accessibility to welfare services.

Why It Matters

This news reexamines the role and responsibility of communities in an aging society. The case of Yangcheon-gu can serve as a good example for other local governments on how to innovate senior welfare policies. Additionally, the significant positive impact of such policies on seniors' lives underscores their social value.

Points to Note

In my view, Yangcheon-gu's mobile senior center system is not just a technological innovation but an important case that changes the paradigm of senior welfare. This change provides seniors with opportunities to connect more actively with society, contributing to their self-esteem and quality of life.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One point not covered in the article is that such policies do not provide the same benefits to all seniors. In particular, seniors who are not familiar with digital devices may still be at risk of exclusion. Therefore, it will be necessary to provide non-face-to-face services in addition to technological approaches.

Current Points to Check

It is important to closely observe how innovative approaches like Yangcheon-gu's smart senior center system can be applied in other regions and what results emerge. In particular, discussions on what lessons other local governments can learn from successful cases are needed.
