North Korea to Discuss Possible Constitutional Amendment Regarding Hostile Two States at First Supreme People's Assembly Meeting

North Korea will hold the first meeting of its newly formed Supreme People's Assembly on the 22nd. This meeting will primarily address the 'issue of amending and supplementing the socialist constitution', with particular attention on whether to specify the 'hostile two states' relationship between North and South Korea in the constitution. Chairman Kim Jong-un is likely to be re-elected as the Chairman of the State Affairs Commission, and there is also a possibility of delivering messages regarding South Korea and the United States during this meeting.

This meeting will be an important opportunity to gauge North Korea's political direction and changes in foreign policy. In particular, if a constitutional amendment occurs, it could significantly impact inter-Korean relations.

Key Summary

  • The first meeting of the North Korean Supreme People's Assembly will be held on the 22nd.
  • The meeting will discuss the possibility of constitutional amendments and the election of the Chairman of the State Affairs Commission.
  • There is speculation about reflecting the concept of 'hostile two states' in the constitution.
  • Kim Jong-un is expected to be re-elected.
  • There is a possibility of announcing messages directed at South Korea and the United States.

Key Highlights

  • Discussion on Constitutional Amendment: Potential to specify 'hostile two states' in the constitution.
  • Political Changes: Anticipation of Kim Jong-un's re-election and changes in the national leadership.
  • Foreign Policy Direction: Increased tension due to the possibility of announcing messages directed at South Korea and the United States.
  • Changes in Delegate Composition: Increased ratio of Central Committee members reflecting changes in the power structure.
  • Economic Issues: Discussion on the national economic development five-year plan and budget issues.

Reasons to Consider the Background

Since declaring the 'hostile two states' at the end of 2023, North Korea has hinted multiple times at the possibility of enshrining this in the constitution. This meeting is seen as a continuation of that trend and is a crucial opportunity to clarify North Korea's political stance and diplomatic attitude. The Supreme People's Assembly functions similarly to South Korea's National Assembly, determining major policies and laws of the state.

Why It Matters

If a constitutional amendment occurs during this meeting, it could represent a significant event that changes the legal foundation of inter-Korean relations. In particular, North Korea's policy towards South Korea may become more hardline, which could directly impact relations with South Korea. Additionally, if Kim Jong-un is re-elected, it suggests that his policy direction will continue, making predictions about North Korea's future foreign strategy even more challenging.

Personal Commentary

In my view, the decision on whether to amend the constitution in this meeting is unlikely to be merely a change in legal wording. Given the potential for Kim Jong-un to deliver strong messages towards South Korea and the United States for the sake of political stability, the international community's response should also be closely monitored. Notably, if 'hostile two states' is specified in the constitution, North Korea's foreign policy could become even more isolated.

Hidden Variables to Watch

Changes in delegate composition during this meeting could be overlooked. The increased ratio of Central Committee members could signal changes in North Korea's internal power structure. Moreover, since economic issues will also be a major agenda item, discussions on economic policy should not be underestimated in terms of their impact on North Korea's foreign relations.

Current Points to Check

  • Impact of changes in delegate composition on North Korean politics.
