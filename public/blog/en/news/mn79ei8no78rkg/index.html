Impact of Generic Drug Price Reduction on the Pharmaceutical Industry

Recently, the government announced a plan to gradually reduce the prices of generics, or generic drugs, by 2029. Currently, the price of generics stands at 53.55% of original drugs, and it is set to be lowered to 45%. This is the first revision of the drug pricing system in 14 years. While this measure is expected to yield financial savings for health insurance, the pharmaceutical industry has expressed concerns about the potential for excessive benefits. In particular, small and medium-sized pharmaceutical companies, which are highly dependent on generics, are voicing worries about management impacts due to declining sales.

Now, let's examine what this change means and how it will affect various stakeholders.

Key Summary

  • The government has decided to gradually reduce generic drug prices from 53.55% to 45% by 2029.
  • The health insurance registration period for rare disease treatments will be shortened from a maximum of 240 days to 100 days.
  • The pharmaceutical industry is protesting, claiming that the measures do not consider the industry's specificities.
  • The government expects approximately 2.4 trillion won in financial savings from this reform.

Key Highlights

  • Gradual Price Reduction: The price of generics will start at 51% in the second half of this year and will be reduced by 2 percentage points each year until it reaches 45% by 2029.
  • Shortened Review Period for Rare Disease Treatments: The review period will be reduced from a maximum of 240 days to 100 days to enhance patient accessibility.
  • Pharmaceutical Industry Protests: Pharmaceutical companies are concerned that the price reduction will stifle their research and development capabilities.
  • Financial Savings: The government anticipates around 2.4 trillion won in financial savings by 2037 due to the price reduction.
  • Special Provisions: The pace of price reduction for innovative pharmaceutical companies will be slowed.

Why Background Matters

This reform is the first in 14 years since 2012 when generic drug prices were set at 53.55% of original prices. However, due to the high dependence on generics in the domestic pharmaceutical industry structure and competitive environment, the effects of price reduction have been limited. Amid continuous increases in health insurance drug costs, the clash between the pharmaceutical industry's protests and the government's need for financial savings has led to this reform proposal.

Why It Matters

This drug pricing reform signals a structural change in the pharmaceutical industry, beyond mere price reductions. While lower prices may reduce the burden of drug costs for consumers, the pharmaceutical industry may face decreased investment capacity for research and development. This could negatively impact the long-term development of innovative new drugs.

Personal Comment

In my view, this measure aims for short-term financial savings, but the long-term impact on the research and development ecosystem of the pharmaceutical industry is more significant. Small and medium-sized pharmaceutical companies, which are highly dependent on generics, are particularly at risk of management crises due to declining sales. Therefore, the government must fully understand the specificities of the pharmaceutical industry and create an environment conducive to ongoing research and development.

Hidden Variables to Consider

This reform proposal includes special provisions for pharmaceutical companies. The pace of price reduction for innovative pharmaceutical companies will be slowed, which could actually hinder competition in the market. Additionally, there needs to be a discussion on whether the shortened review period for rare disease treatments will genuinely enhance patient access to treatment.

Points to Check Now

  • Attention should be paid to how the government's drug price reduction policy will be implemented in practice.
  • The pharmaceutical industry's protests and the potential for policy changes in response should also be monitored.

FAQ

  1. How will the reduction in generic drug prices affect patients?

    • It is expected that patients' out-of-pocket costs will decrease as drug prices are lowered.

  2. Why is the pharmaceutical industry protesting?

    • The price reduction could diminish their investment capacity for research and development, which may negatively impact the development of innovative new drugs.

  3. When will this reform proposal be implemented?

    • The price will be reduced to 51% starting in the second half of this year, with subsequent annual reductions of 2 percentage points.
