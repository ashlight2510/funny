Bank of Korea begins Phase 2 of digital currency experiment... Exploring various use cases including small businesses

The Bank of Korea has embarked on the second phase of its experiment for the formal introduction of a digital currency system. This project is part of "Project Han River," and two additional banks will join the seven banks that previously participated in real transactions. The Bank of Korea plans to support a pilot project for the execution of national treasury funds in the first half of this year and will conduct follow-up real transactions in the second half. This experiment focuses on discovering various use cases, including small businesses and large enterprises, and is also developing functionalities to enhance the usability of digital currencies and deposit tokens.

The background for this digital currency introduction is the Bank of Korea's intention to support the digital transformation and innovation of the financial system. This is expected to have a significant impact not just as a technical change, but across the entire financial ecosystem.

Key Highlights

  • Launch of Phase 2 of the digital currency experiment: The Bank of Korea has started the second experiment for the introduction of digital currency.
  • Diversification of use cases: Exploring the potential for digital currency use in various fields, including small businesses and large enterprises.
  • Functionality development: Enhancing user convenience by developing new features such as biometric authentication and peer-to-peer transfers.
  • Pilot project for national treasury fund execution: Supporting a pilot project related to the establishment of electric vehicle charging facilities by the Ministry of Climate, Energy, and Environment in the first half of this year.
  • Development of the digital asset ecosystem: Providing innovative financial services through digital currencies and deposit tokens, and supporting the domestic digital asset ecosystem.

Why It's Important to Understand the Background

The Bank of Korea has been conducting "Project Han River" since last year to introduce digital currency. In Phase 1, it checked whether the processes of manufacturing, issuing, distributing, redeeming, and destroying digital currency operated smoothly. In this Phase 2, the aim is to discover more banks and various use cases based on this foundation to build a practical digital currency ecosystem. Targeting small businesses and large enterprises seems to be an intention to reduce their financial burdens and enhance the efficiency of payment systems.

Why It Matters

The introduction of digital currency could bring about fundamental changes in the financial system, beyond merely providing a new payment method. If vulnerable economic agents, such as small businesses, can enhance their financial accessibility through digital currency, it could have a positive impact on the economy as a whole. Additionally, if digital currency becomes more prevalent, it could reduce the burden of existing credit card fees, contributing to the management stability of small businesses.

Points to Watch

The most noteworthy aspect of this experiment is the exploration of various use cases targeting small businesses and large enterprises. This will allow for the measurement of the actual impact of digital currency on the economy. Additionally, how new features like biometric authentication are implemented and what convenience they provide to actual users will also be important checkpoints.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One should be cautious of excessive expectations that the introduction of digital currency will solve all problems. Various variables, such as technical limitations, security issues, and user acceptance, still exist. Furthermore, discussions on how digital currency will integrate with the existing financial system are necessary. If limitations or issues arise in these areas, the introduction of digital currency could lead to confusion instead.
