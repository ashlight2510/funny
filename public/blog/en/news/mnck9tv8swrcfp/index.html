“Thoroughly investigate and punish”···Last funeral of the victims of the Safety Industry fire disaster

The last funeral service for the late Oh Sang-yeol, who died in the Daejeon Safety Industry fire disaster, was held on the 30th. Mr. Oh was a mold technician who worked at the Safety Industry for 43 years and was the last victim to have a funeral service among the 14 victims of this fire. The bereaved families are demanding a thorough investigation into the facts and punishment for those responsible, highlighting the importance of safety management once again.

This incident has sparked serious discussions about safety issues in industrial sites and corporate responsibility, rather than being just a simple fire accident. The bereaved families pointed out that there had been several fires before the incident, suggesting that these previous occurrences may have delayed evacuation.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The fire disaster occurred at the Daejeon Safety Industry factory and was extinguished approximately 10 hours and 30 minutes after the fire broke out. It is known that there had been several fires at the factory prior to this incident, indicating a lack of safety management. The bereaved families argue that these previous occurrences may have delayed evacuation during the fire. In addition, labor authorities are investigating company officials for violations of the Serious Accidents Punishment Act and the Occupational Safety and Health Act.

Why It Matters

This incident is not just an industrial accident but has sparked discussions about safety management and corporate social responsibility across Korean society. The demands for fact-finding and punishment from the bereaved families will play a crucial role in preventing similar incidents in the future. It serves as a reminder of the importance of safety management systems in industrial sites where large fires frequently occur.

Points of Interest

In my view, the most noteworthy aspect of this incident is the bereaved families' persistent demand for a "thorough investigation." This should not only aim to identify the cause of the incident but also lead to institutional improvements to prevent similar incidents in the future. Safety training and improvements in firefighting facilities are both corporate responsibilities and issues directly related to the lives of workers.

Hidden Variables to Consider

A variable that cannot be overlooked in this incident is the internal culture of the company. Statements have emerged indicating that safety training was conducted only in a formal manner, which reflects a broader issue regarding the overall safety culture of the company. It is essential to recognize that safety management must go beyond mere compliance with regulations and be rooted in the consciousness and actions of all employees.
