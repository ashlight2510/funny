Increase in National Park Visitors and Positive Impact on Local Economy

Recently, the number of visitors to national parks has surged, drawing attention to their positive impact on local economies. Last year, approximately 43 million people visited national parks, spending a total of 3.5564 trillion won in nearby areas. The economic revitalization effect is particularly pronounced in regions facing severe population decline, indicating that visits to national parks contribute to reducing the risk of regional extinction.

This analysis is based on a survey conducted by the Korea National Park Service, which assessed the impact on the local economy through the duration of stay and consumption types of visitors. The results showed that overnight visitors have a greater impact on local consumption than day visitors. This suggests that national parks play a role beyond being mere natural tourist destinations.

Key Summary

  • Last year, the number of national park visitors was 43 million, and their spending in the region amounted to approximately 3.5564 trillion won.
  • Consumption generated in national parks located in areas with declining populations accounted for about 1.68 trillion won, or 47% of the total.
  • Overnight visitors have a greater impact on local consumption compared to day visitors.
  • As the duration of stay increases, the per capita spending tends to rise.
  • The contribution of national park visits to local economic revitalization holds significant policy value.

Why Is It Important?

This data clearly demonstrates the positive impact of national parks on local economies. The fact that national parks play a crucial role in economic revitalization in areas with declining populations is significant for policymakers. This trend could help mitigate the risk of regional extinction and will be an important factor in enhancing the sustainability of the tourism industry.

Relationship Between Local Economy and National Parks

The impact of national park visits on the local economy goes beyond mere consumption amounts. For instance, Bukhansan National Park recorded 623.5 billion won in consumption, showing the most significant economic effect, which directly benefits local restaurants, accommodation providers, and tourism service businesses. In reality, local merchants are experiencing increased sales due to the rise in visitors, which can lead to job creation.

Points of Interest

I believe it is important to note that visits to national parks have the potential to expand beyond simple nature tourism to include local cultural experiences. The plan by the service to expand stay-oriented visiting programs provides opportunities to experience both local culture and nature, which can increase the duration of visitors' stays and boost consumption. This will have a positive long-term impact on the local economy.

Hidden Variables to Consider

An important variable not addressed in this article is the changing consumption patterns of national park visitors. For example, travel trends have shifted post-COVID-19, with eco-friendly travel gaining prominence. This could influence the types of consumption among national park visitors, and new consumption trends may emerge based on future survey results.

Points to Check Now

  • The specific impact of national park visits on the economy in regions at risk of extinction.
  • The long-term effects of expanding overnight visiting programs on the local economy.
  • Response strategies to changes in visitors' consumption patterns.
