Integrated Care Faces Concerns of Being 'Just a Facade' Due to Budget Shortages

The nationwide implementation of the integrated care system is just around the corner, but concerns are growing that practical service provision may be impossible due to budget shortages. With only 290 million won allocated per local government, it seems likely that the operation will remain formal rather than substantive, contrary to the intent of integrated care. Consequently, there is a growing call for discussions on funding solutions for community integrated care.

This issue goes beyond just the scale of the budget; it poses a serious concern that could lead to qualitative disparities in care services across regions. Integrated care is a system designed to provide necessary medical, nursing, and caregiving services to those in need, such as the elderly and disabled, in their communities. If this system does not function properly, beneficiaries could suffer significant harm.

Key Summary

  • Concerns have been raised that the integrated care system may be poorly executed due to budget shortages.
  • The budget allocated per local government this year is 290 million won, limiting practical service provision.
  • There are worries about qualitative disparities in care services between regions, and a stable financial structure, including the establishment of funds, is needed.
  • The government's passive financial responsibility has been criticized, and a lack of legal basis has been pointed out as a problem.

Key Scenes

  • Budget Shortage: The integrated care budget per local government is 290 million won, making it difficult to even hire the necessary personnel for service provision.
  • Service Disparity: Regions with lower financial independence are at greater risk of declining quality in care services.
  • Lack of Legal Basis: Current legislation lacks specific legal grounds for funding procurement, leading to annual budget allocations determining the scale of the project.
  • Concerns of Administrative Showmanship: There is a high likelihood that the budget will be spent on promotions and events rather than actual care services.
  • Need for Funds: Experts argue for the establishment of a national care fund, citing examples from Japan.

Why Background Matters

The integrated care system was introduced with the intent of providing comprehensive services to those in need, such as the elderly and disabled, within their communities. However, the budget necessary to ensure the effectiveness of this system is woefully inadequate. Particularly, since local governments must fill in the gaps with their own budgets, regions with low financial capacity are likely to struggle in providing proper services. Such structural issues could undermine the very purpose of the integrated care system.

Why It Matters

This issue transcends mere budget size or distribution; it directly impacts the quality of life for those in need of care. If the quality of services varies by region, it could have serious implications for social equity. Especially for vulnerable groups like the elderly and disabled, a lack of adequate support can severely affect their lives.

Points of Interest

In my view, the success of integrated care hinges more on how the budget is utilized rather than its size. Even with a small budget, if used effectively, there is potential to enhance service quality. However, given the current budget structure, such potential appears low. Particularly, there is a need to consider how to provide practical services in a situation where hiring personnel is challenging.

Hidden Variables to Consider

While the article points out the lack of legal basis for integrated care, issues may also arise in the budget allocation process reflecting government policy decisions. In other words, how the government prioritizes budget allocation in a situation of scarcity could serve as a more significant variable.

Points to Check Now

  • Budget Allocation Plans: It is essential to examine the budget allocation plans for integrated care in each local government and pay attention to which services will be provided and how.
  • Policy Changes: It is crucial to observe what measures the government will take to improve the financial structure of the integrated care system.
