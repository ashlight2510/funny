30% of high school students say “It’s hard to concentrate and read for more than 10 minutes”···more than half engage in ‘habitual shorts viewing’

Recent research indicates that 3 out of 10 high school students find it difficult to concentrate on reading long texts for more than 10 minutes. This finding comes from a survey conducted by JinHakSa, where 30.6% of 3,525 students reported experiencing such difficulties. Moreover, more than half of the students habitually watch short-form content like YouTube Shorts or Reels, raising concerns about declining concentration. Experts have pointed out that the consumption of short-form content is making students' brains accustomed to short and intense stimuli.

This phenomenon is emerging as a serious issue that could impact not only individuals but also the education sector and society as a whole. The decline in students' concentration can affect their academic performance and, in the long run, their professional achievements when they enter the workforce. Therefore, discussions on how to address this issue are necessary.

Key Summary

  • 30.6% of high school students reported difficulty concentrating on reading long texts for more than 10 minutes.
  • Over 60% habitually watch short-form content, with a tendency for increased viewing time.
  • Experts warn that the rise of short-form content may lead to decreased concentration.
  • It is suggested that students need training in reading long texts to regain their concentration.

Key Scenes

  • Declining Concentration: Many students feel difficulty reading long texts for more than 10 minutes.
  • Impact of Short-form Content: More than half of the students habitually watch short-form content.
  • Learning Effects: Concerns about the negative impact of declining concentration on academic performance.
  • Expert Advice: There is a need to reduce smartphone use and train in reading long texts.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This survey is related to the recent surge in the consumption of short-form content. As students are exposed to short-form content through the internet and mobile messengers, it is analyzed that their concentration on long texts naturally declines. According to a survey by the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family, short-form content is the most used medium among adolescents, making this phenomenon even more pronounced.

Why It Matters

This issue goes beyond individual learning styles and could affect the cultivation of talent in society in the long term. There are significant concerns that students lacking concentration may struggle with performance and social responsibilities when they become adults. Therefore, collaborative efforts from the education sector, parents, and society are needed to address this issue.

Points to Note

In my view, solving this problem cannot rely solely on individual efforts. Cooperation between schools and families is necessary, and it is important to create an environment where students can develop the habit of reading long texts. For example, setting designated reading times or engaging in discussions after reading can help students find enjoyment in reading long texts.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Students who consume a lot of short-form content may actually possess the skills needed to read long texts. In other words, the reason they do not read long texts may not simply be a lack of ability, but rather a lack of interest. Therefore, it is essential to approach this issue by enhancing the quality and appeal of the content.

Points to Check Now

  • Look for programs or materials that can help students read long texts.
  • Discuss with students ways to reduce the consumption of short-form content.

FAQ

Q: What is the impact of short-form content on concentration?
A: Short-form content provides short and intense stimuli, making it difficult for the brain to develop the concentration needed for reading long texts.

Q: What kind of training should students undergo to read long texts?
A: It is important to distance themselves from smartphones and cultivate the habit of reading long texts for a set amount of time.

Q: How can educational institutions address this issue?
A: It is necessary to implement reading programs or provide various reading materials that can engage students' interests.

Addressing these issues requires collective efforts. It is crucial to find ways for students to enjoy reading long texts right now.
