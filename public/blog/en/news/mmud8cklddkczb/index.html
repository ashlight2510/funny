“Do you really believe that all lives are equal?”… The provocative question of the movie ‘Bomb’

The film ‘Bomb’ is based on the original work of third-generation Zainichi Korean writer Oh Seung-ho and depicts the psychological warfare surrounding a bomb terror incident in Tokyo. This film goes beyond being a simple thriller, posing challenging questions about the equal value of life and human biases. The protagonist, Suzuki, predicts that a bomb will explode, maximizing tension through psychological warfare with the police, and in the process, raises questions that reveal biases regarding life.

Key Summary

  • The film ‘Bomb’ is a thriller based on the original work of a third-generation Zainichi Korean author, centered around a bomb terror incident in Tokyo.
  • The protagonist, Suzuki, poses provocative questions about the equality of life during psychological warfare with the police.
  • The film is recognized as a work signaling the revival of Japanese commercial cinema, achieving notable box office success.
  • It has a strong thematic consciousness, exploring ethical questions beyond mere genre enjoyment.

Key Scenes

  • Thematic Consciousness: Reveals human biases through questions about life equality.
  • Box Office Success: Leading the revival of commercial cinema with box office records in Japan.
  • Tension of Psychological Warfare: The fierce psychological battle between the protagonist and the police serves as a major source of entertainment.
  • Strong Characters: Jiro Sato's performance as Suzuki enhances the film's immersion.
  • Social Message: A thriller based on bomb terror that addresses social issues.

Reasons to Consider the Background

‘Bomb’ was released at a time when the voices of third-generation Zainichi Koreans are gaining more attention in Japan. Recently, there has been a shift in societal perceptions of Zainichi Koreans, leading to more stories and experiences being depicted in film and literature. ‘Bomb’ showcases new possibilities for Japanese commercial cinema within this trend, aiming to convey social messages beyond mere entertainment.

Why It Matters

This film transcends the simple thriller genre, posing profound questions about life and value. In particular, the questions posed by the protagonist to the police provide an opportunity for reflection on societal biases and discrimination. The theme of life equality remains a significant issue in modern society, prompting deep thoughts among viewers. Additionally, the film's commercial success in Japan demonstrates that works with social messages can also achieve financial success.

Personal Perspective

In my view, the most noteworthy aspect of ‘Bomb’ is how the questions related to life equality connect to the biases that need to be overcome. The questions posed by Suzuki are not just a scene in a thriller but demand deep reflection from the audience. Viewers are prompted to reconsider their own biases, and these questions resonate more intensely alongside the film's tension.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The themes addressed in the film are not limited to incidents like bomb terror. Rather, they pose questions about structural issues in society and the essence of humanity through this incident. If the audience focuses solely on the thrills of the thriller, they may miss these profound messages. Moreover, failing to understand what the character Suzuki symbolizes could lead to missing the film's true meaning.

Points to Check Now

It is important to examine how the voices of Zainichi Korean writers are being reflected in the current Japanese film market and how ‘Bomb’ approaches social issues. Discussions on the potential impact of the questions raised by this film on our society are also necessary.
