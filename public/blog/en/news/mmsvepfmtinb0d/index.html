"Why the Memorial Ceremony for the Sewol Ferry Tragedy is Important for Families Ahead of the 12th Anniversary"

March 16, 2026 · 경향신문 · View source

One Month Before the 12th Anniversary of the Sewol Ferry Disaster... Families Demand President Lee to Attend Memorial Ceremony

As the 12th anniversary of the Sewol ferry disaster approaches, the bereaved families are urging President Lee Jae-myung to attend the memorial ceremony and to disclose records related to the tragedy. The April 16 Sewol Ferry Disaster Family Council and the April 16th Promise National Solidarity held a press conference at the Sewol Ferry Memorial Space in the Seoul City Council, emphasizing the state's responsibility for safety and requesting the president's attendance. They are particularly demanding the release of presidential records from the day of the disaster and the establishment of laws for disaster prevention.

This news goes beyond a mere request for attendance at a memorial ceremony; it demonstrates the bereaved families' strong will to uncover the truth about the Sewol ferry disaster and clarify the state's responsibilities. They are demanding legal and institutional changes for a safer society in the present and future, alongside their commitment to never forget the pain of the past.

Key Points

  • Bereaved families are urging President Lee Jae-myung to attend the 12th anniversary memorial ceremony.
  • They are demanding the release of the 'Seven Hours Document,' which is an important record that can confirm the state's response during the disaster.
  • There is a strong emphasis on the need for the establishment of the 'Basic Law on Life Safety' to strengthen disaster prevention and response systems.
  • The memorial ceremony is scheduled to be held on April 16 at the Hwaryong Recreational Area in Ansan, Gyeonggi Province.
  • The bereaved families have declared April as the 'Month of Memory and Promise.'

Background and Context

The Sewol ferry disaster was one of the largest maritime disasters in South Korean history, occurring on April 16, 2014, resulting in the tragic loss of 304 lives. Since then, the bereaved families have continuously demanded the clarification of the facts and improvements to legal systems for a safer society. This press conference was held in the context of reaffirming these ongoing demands and questioning the state's responsibilities once again as the 12th anniversary approaches.

In particular, the 'Seven Hours Document' requested by the bereaved families is a key piece of evidence that can confirm the actions of former President Park Geun-hye and the state's response on the day of the disaster, and its release remains controversial. The disclosure of this document would be an important opportunity for the bereaved families to uncover the truth.

Why This Issue is Important

The Sewol ferry disaster is not merely a maritime disaster; it is an event that prompts a re-examination of the state's safety systems and social responsibilities. The acknowledgment of state responsibility and the establishment of laws demanded by the bereaved families are essential measures for building a safe society in the present and future, going beyond a mere commitment to remember the pain of the past. This issue highlights the need for institutional changes for the safety of our society, extending beyond just a memorial ceremony.

Often Overlooked Aspects

The need for the establishment of the 'Basic Law on Life Safety,' emphasized during the press conference, is an important element that many may overlook. This law is a legal mechanism to ensure that all citizens have the right to live safely, going beyond just the memory of the Sewol ferry disaster. It would facilitate the protection of disaster and accident victims and vulnerable groups in terms of safety.

What to Check Now

Attention should be paid to whether the president will attend the Sewol ferry 12th anniversary memorial ceremony, the official message that will follow, and the legal judgment on the release of the 'Seven Hours Document.' This will be an important decision for our safety and future, extending beyond just the memory of a single event.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: When and where will the Sewol ferry 12th anniversary memorial ceremony be held?
A: It will be held on April 16 at 3 PM at the Hwaryong Recreational Area in Ansan, Gyeonggi Province.

Q: What is the 'Seven Hours Document' that the bereaved families are demanding?
A: It is a document produced by the Blue House on the day of the disaster, which contains important records that can confirm the actions of former President Park Geun-hye and the state's response.

Q: What does the 'Basic Law on Life Safety' stipulate?
A: It specifies the right of all citizens to live safely as a 'right to safety' and outlines the responsibilities of the state and local governments.
