February Car Export Amount This Year Decreased by 20.8% Compared to Last February “Influence of the Lunar New Year Holiday”

In February 2026, Korea's car export amount was recorded at $4.806 billion, a decrease of approximately 20.8% compared to the same month last year. According to the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy, this decrease is primarily attributed to the reduction in working days due to the Lunar New Year holiday. The export volume also decreased to 189,885 units, down 18.5% from 232,977 units in February of last year, with declines observed in all specific regions including the United States, European Union, Asia, and the Middle East. In contrast, domestic sales of eco-friendly vehicles showed an upward trend, indicating a positive impact from government subsidy policies.

This decline in exports carries significance beyond mere numbers. The automotive industry is one of the key pillars of the Korean economy, and a decrease in exports can have substantial effects on the overall economy. In particular, discussions are needed on how temporary factors like the Lunar New Year holiday may impact long-term industry outlooks.

Key Points

  • February 2026 car export amount decreased by 20.8% compared to last year.
  • Reduction in working days due to the Lunar New Year holiday led to a decrease in export volume.
  • Declines were observed across regions including the U.S., Europe, Asia, and the Middle East.
  • Domestic sales of eco-friendly vehicles increased, influenced by government subsidy policies.
  • Exports of electric vehicles decreased, while hybrid vehicle exports showed an upward trend.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The recent decline in car exports is mainly attributed to the reduction in working days due to the Lunar New Year holiday. While the Lunar New Year is an annual event, the resulting reduction in working days can have an immediate impact on exports. This phenomenon is closely related to the characteristics of the Korean automotive industry. Given Korea's economy relies heavily on car exports, a reduction in working days can lead to short-term declines in exports.

Why It Matters

The automotive industry is a significant part of the Korean economy, and a decrease in exports can negatively impact the domestic economy. In particular, it could lead to job insecurity for those working in the automotive sector and related industries. Furthermore, as new trends like eco-friendly vehicles emerge, the decline in traditional car exports may raise awareness of the need for structural changes in the industry.

Points of Interest

The increase in domestic sales and sales of eco-friendly vehicles can be interpreted as positive signals. The effectiveness of government subsidy policies suggests that they could be a crucial factor in determining the future direction of the automotive industry. However, the decline in exports of electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles continues to raise concerns, indicating areas where policy improvements may be necessary.

My Observations

I believe that the increase in domestic sales of eco-friendly vehicles will play a crucial role in the recovery of the automotive industry in the future. Specifically, the rise in sales of electric and hybrid vehicles signals a growing consumer interest in environmental issues. However, if the decline in exports of traditional internal combustion engine vehicles continues, a structural change in the entire industry may be required.

Hidden Variables to Consider

While the article emphasizes the impact of the Lunar New Year holiday, external factors such as the global economic situation and fluctuations in raw material prices can also significantly affect car exports. Additionally, changes in trade policies in various countries should not be overlooked as they can impact the Korean automotive industry.

Current Points to Check

It is essential to closely monitor whether this export decline will persist in the long term or remain a short-term phenomenon. In particular, it is important to observe how the government's eco-friendly vehicle subsidy policies will continue and how consumer responses will change accordingly.
