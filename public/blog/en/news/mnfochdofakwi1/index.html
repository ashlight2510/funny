President Yoon hints at tax reduction benefits for non-resident homeowners

Recently, President Lee Jae-myung expressed his intention to recognize tax reduction benefits for homeowners who have temporarily become non-resident due to unavoidable reasons such as work or children's education in relation to the long-term holding special deduction (LTHSD). He indicated that he would design policies to continuously provide LTHSD benefits to non-resident homeowners through these criteria. This approach differs from the existing LTHSD, which applies only to homes used for investment or speculation, and aims to alleviate the tax burden on non-resident homeowners.

These remarks can be interpreted as measures to resolve social controversies surrounding real estate policies and to enhance residential stability. In particular, it is expected to have a positive impact on many citizens who must leave their residences due to work or children's education.

Key Summary

  • President Lee Jae-myung hints at recognizing LTHSD benefits for non-resident homeowners.
  • Raises the possibility of tax reductions for those temporarily non-resident due to work and children's education.
  • Proposes a differentiated approach from existing investment and speculation homes.

Key Scenes

  • Tax Reduction Benefits: Possibility of recognizing LTHSD benefits for non-resident homeowners.
  • Distinction between Speculation and Residence: Clarifying the criteria for LTHSD application to protect non-residents.
  • Social Response: Building consensus on the issue of non-residency due to work and children's education.
  • Policy Direction: Commitment to eradicating speculation in real estate policies and efforts to secure residential stability.

Why Background Matters

This issue is part of the real estate policy and is an extension of President Lee Jae-myung's earlier emphasis on "eradicating real estate speculation" and "securing residential stability." The president intends to enhance the residential stability of citizens in unavoidable situations through tax reductions for non-resident homeowners. These remarks can be seen as measures to dispel concerns that non-residents would not receive LTHSD benefits.

Why It Matters

Long-term holding privileges in the real estate market can contribute to stabilizing housing prices, but if tax reductions for non-residents are implemented, they could play a positive role in enhancing residential stability. In particular, providing tangible benefits to households leaving their residences due to work or children's education offers an opportunity to address social discontent.
