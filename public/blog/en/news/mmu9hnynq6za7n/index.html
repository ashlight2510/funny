More than half of the public believes that taxes on the high-income class are low, while those on the middle and low-income classes are appropriate... Clear temperature difference by class

According to a recent survey released by the Korea Institute for Health and Social Affairs, more than half of the public perceives the tax burden on the high-income class as low. In contrast, many respondents evaluate the tax levels for the middle and low-income classes as appropriate. This difference in perception is distinctly evident based on income levels, and opinions for and against tax increases to expand welfare budgets are evenly matched.

This survey was conducted as an additional study of the Korean Welfare Panel, targeting 2,942 individuals, and it demonstrates that perceptions of taxes reflect inequalities between classes. Notably, there is a pronounced tendency for the low-income class to demand greater tax contributions from the high-income class.

Key Summary

  • The response indicating that taxes on the high-income class are low is 56.84%, exceeding half.
  • A majority believes that taxes on the middle and low-income classes are appropriate.
  • The low-income class tends to demand more tax contributions from the high-income class.
  • Opinions for and against tax increases for welfare budgets are evenly matched.
  • Perceptions of the tax burden on the high-income class vary by income level.

Key Scenes

  • Percentage of respondents perceiving high-income taxes as low: 56.84%
  • Percentage viewing middle and low-income taxes as appropriate: 54.69% (middle class), 51.26% (low-income class)
  • Percentage of low-income respondents who believe high-income taxes are excessively low: 19.10%
  • Percentage in favor of tax increases for welfare budgets: 37.72%
  • Dissatisfaction with high-income taxes varies by class.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This survey was conducted to examine perceptions of the tax system in Korean society. The perception that the tax burden on the high-income class is low emerges amid deepening economic inequality, with the low-income class tending to demand relatively more tax contributions. This perception will play a significant role in discussions about welfare policies and tax systems.

Why It Matters

Perceptions of taxes are crucial as they directly influence policy decisions. If the perception that the tax burden on the high-income class is low spreads, the government will have no choice but to adjust tax policies, which will ultimately affect welfare policies aimed at addressing social inequality. Additionally, the tendency of the low-income class to support tax increases to expand welfare budgets is directly linked to their living standards, making it impossible for policymakers to ignore.

Points of Attention

In my view, the perception of the tax burden on the high-income class signals a growing social distrust and dissatisfaction that goes beyond just tax policy. These differences in perception could lead to social conflict, and the government needs to make efforts to address this.

Hidden Variables to Consider

It is noteworthy that while dissatisfaction with the tax burden on the high-income class is increasing, the low-income class also has a heightened awareness of their own tax burden. This indicates that the low-income class feels their tax burden more acutely, which could lead to increased demands for welfare policies.

Current Points to Check

The results of this survey should be closely monitored for their potential impact on future government tax and welfare policies. In particular, how tax policies for the high-income class will be adjusted and how the welfare demands of the low-income class will be reflected are critical points.
