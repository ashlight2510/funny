“If supply disruptions continue until the end of April, oil prices may exceed $180” Internal Saudi forecast

Recently, Saudi Arabian oil officials have expressed that if energy supply disruptions due to the Iran war continue, oil prices could exceed $180 per barrel. They predicted that if the crude oil stocks exported from the Gulf region before the outbreak of the war are depleted, oil prices could rise to $138-$140 next week. If supply disruptions persist, oil prices could reach $150 by the second week of April, with a high likelihood of rising to $165 and $180 in the following weeks.

This forecast indicates a situation where prices could surpass the all-time high of $146.08 recorded in July 2008. Analysts from energy consulting firms are not ruling out the possibility of oil prices reaching $200 this year. Saudi Aramco is currently analyzing market trends, which are expected to have a significant impact on future price determinations.

Key Summary

  • Saudi oil authorities forecast oil prices of $180 due to the Iran war.
  • If supply disruptions continue, oil prices are likely to rise weekly.
  • The possibility of surpassing the 2008 record and reaching $200 is raised.
  • Saudi Aramco is analyzing the market for price determination.

Key Scenes

  • Impact of War: The effect of conflict with Iran on energy supply chains.

Why Background Matters

This issue has arisen as military tensions with Iran have intensified. In particular, after the outbreak of war due to attacks by the U.S. and Israel, the Strait of Hormuz has been blocked, severely impacting crude oil supply. This situation has already resulted in a roughly 50% increase in oil prices, which could have serious implications for the global energy market. Saudi Arabia, as an oil-producing country, is highly sensitive to the economic impacts of its conflict with Iran.

Why It’s Important

The surge in oil prices is not limited to the energy market; it can have various effects across the global economy. In particular, rising energy prices directly affect consumer prices, and if inflation rates increase, central banks in various countries may need to adjust monetary policies. Increased energy costs can also raise operational expenses for businesses, negatively impacting employment and investment.

Points to Note

It is important to recognize that the rise in oil prices is not a short-term phenomenon. If the Iran war continues, the recovery of supply chains may be delayed, which could significantly impact energy prices in the long run. Additionally, Saudi Aramco's price determination will not only affect their profits but will also have a considerable impact on the stability of the global energy market.

Personal Interpretation

In my view, the conflict with Iran is not merely a regional issue but a factor that could lead to instability in the global energy market. Particularly, if oil prices reach $180, it would bring about economic shocks that go beyond mere price increases. Therefore, this issue prompts a reconsideration of the complex relationships between international politics and economics beyond simple market predictions.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect that may be overlooked regarding Saudi oil price forecasts is the low likelihood of a quick resolution to supply disruptions. Additionally, as the uncertainty of war persists, prices of alternative energy sources may also be affected. For example, the demand for renewable energy sources like solar and wind may increase, but this also comes with technological and economic constraints.

Current Points to Check

It is crucial to closely monitor the current trends in oil prices and the official announcements from Saudi Aramco. In particular, the price determination to be announced in early April will be an important indicator of the future direction of oil prices. Furthermore, analyzing the impacts on the global economy is essential.
