Common Mistakes and Solutions When Writing Short Commentaries

Short commentaries play an important role in capturing the reader's interest and conveying information concisely. An effective commentary enhances communication with the reader and helps clarify the topic. However, many people often fail to deliver their message effectively due to common mistakes made when writing short commentaries. It is important to recognize these mistakes and seek solutions.

This article will address common mistakes made when writing short commentaries and their solutions, helping you write more effective commentaries.

Common Mistakes When Writing Short Commentaries

1. Including Unnecessary Information

Including unnecessary information in a short commentary can obscure the core message. Readers may find it difficult to grasp the topic, and the purpose of the commentary may diminish.

Action Tip: Focus on the core topic and boldly delete irrelevant information.

Example: In a commentary on politics, starting with "This election is..." may include unnecessary information such as personal anecdotes about candidates or the historical background of the election.

2. Using Complex Sentence Structures

Complex sentence structures can make it difficult for readers to understand. Especially in short commentaries, concise sentences are essential.

Action Tip: Avoid complex sentences and break them into shorter sentences to make it easier for readers to understand.

Example: Change “This policy is expected to have a positive impact on the economy, particularly contributing to the growth of small and medium-sized enterprises” to “This policy is positive for the economy. It will contribute to the growth of small businesses.”

3. Using Excessive Emotional Expressions

Excessive emotional expressions can undermine the objectivity of the commentary. It is important to write fact-based commentaries and minimize personal emotions.

Action Tip: Avoid overly emotional expressions and use fact-based language.

Example: Instead of saying “This policy is terrible,” express it as “This policy may cause several problems.”

How to Write Effective Commentaries

1. Set a Clear Topic

Setting a clear topic is an important factor in capturing the reader's interest. Clearly define the topic and be careful not to stray from it.

Action Tip: The clearer the topic, the easier it is for readers to understand and relate to the commentary.

Example: If you choose the topic “Measures for Environmental Issues,” clearly specify what measures you will address.

2. Use Concise Sentences

Concise sentences help readers understand easily. It is important to remove unnecessary words while maintaining a balance so that sentences do not become too short.

Action Tip: Keep sentences concise but be careful not to lose meaning.

Example: Change “The new policy that will be implemented next week will affect all citizens” to “The policy implemented next week will affect citizens.”

Checklist for Resolving Common Mistakes

  • Did I include only information relevant to the topic?
  • Are the sentences concise and clear?
  • Is the emotional expression not excessive?
  • Are there examples included that help the reader's understanding?

Examples to Reference When Writing Short Commentaries

  • Example 1: Commentary with Unnecessary Information

    • "This election is complicated with various candidates and issues intertwined. However, this commentary will provide information about the candidates' personal backgrounds and past political actions."

  • Example 2: Concise and Clear Commentary

    • "The important issue in this election is economic policy. Voters prioritize economic growth and job creation."

  • Example 3: Commentary Excluding Emotional Expressions

    • "This policy may cause several problems. Experts are concerned about the effects of the policy."

Considerations When Writing Short Commentaries

When writing short commentaries, it is important to consider the reader's background knowledge. Using language and expressions that the reader can understand is crucial. Additionally, maintaining a respectful attitude towards diverse opinions and continuously improving through feedback is necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  1. What is the most important factor when writing a short commentary?

    • The most important factor is setting a clear topic. Clearly defining the topic allows readers to understand easily.

  2. How can I reduce emotional expressions?

    • It is advisable to focus on fact-based information, minimize personal emotions, and use neutral language.

  3. What should I check after writing a commentary?

    • It is important to check whether only relevant information is included, whether the sentences are concise and clear, and whether emotional expressions are not excessive.

Recognize the common mistakes made when writing short commentaries and apply methods to resolve them. Start taking the first step towards writing concise and clear commentaries today.
