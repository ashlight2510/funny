The Aftermath of the Iran War: The Crisis of Japanese Public Baths 'Sentō'

Recently, the Iran war has caused international oil prices to surge, putting traditional public baths known as 'Sentō' in Japan in serious financial distress. In particular, the long-standing bathhouse 'Fujimiyu' near Mount Fuji has faced an additional annual cost burden of about 600,000 yen due to the price of heavy oil skyrocketing from 100 yen to 130 yen per liter. As a result, many bathhouses that have been in operation for decades are now considering closure.

This phenomenon extends beyond individual business issues and significantly impacts the viability of Sentō, a cultural symbol of Japanese society. While Sentō has been an important place of relaxation for the common people in Japan, its operation is now threatened by economic factors.

Key Summary

  • The Iran war has caused a surge in international oil prices, leading to financial difficulties for Japanese public baths 'Sentō'.
  • The rise in heavy oil prices has drastically increased operating costs, prompting some long-standing establishments to decide to close.
  • Due to local government price caps, raising prices is not possible, resulting in accumulated losses.

Key Scenes

  • Rising International Oil Prices: Fuel costs have surged due to the impact of the Iran war.
  • Economic Hardships for Sentō: Increased operating costs due to rising heavy oil prices.
  • Price Cap Regulations: Local government regulations prevent price increases.
  • Increasing Closure Cases: Traditional Sentō are shutting down.
  • Social Impact: The cultural spaces for the common people in Japan are at risk of disappearing.

Why Background Matters

This issue is directly caused by the outbreak of the Iran war and the resulting rise in international oil prices. Historically, Japanese public baths have served as important refuges for the common people, but the recent years of sustained economic difficulties, combined with this rise in oil prices, have delivered a decisive blow. In particular, Sentō is in an even more serious situation due to local government price caps that prevent them from freely adjusting prices.

Why It Matters

This situation is not just about the closure of a few businesses. A part of Japan's traditional culture and the everyday lives of the common people is at risk of disappearing, which could have psychological and cultural impacts across Japanese society. Furthermore, these changes will significantly affect consumers who frequent public baths.

Personal Perspective

What is noteworthy in this situation is that the closure of Sentō could lead to social isolation and cultural loss beyond mere economic issues. For instance, if a bathhouse that someone has frequented for over 40 years closes, the experiences and memories associated with it will vanish, negatively affecting the cohesion of the local community.

Hidden Variables to Consider

While the article mentions the geopolitical crisis of the Iran war impacting Japanese public baths, the underlying economic structure and policy issues within Japan are overlooked. For example, if discussions do not take place regarding easing the price caps on public baths, these problems will only worsen.

Current Points to Check

  • Response from the Japanese Government: It is necessary to check whether policies to support public baths are being discussed.
  • Community Reactions: Observing the responses and coping strategies of local residents regarding the closure of Sentō is important.

FAQ

Q: When did the closures of Sentō begin?
A: Following the recent surge in international oil prices due to the Iran war, particularly from early 2023, cases of closure have been increasing.

Q: How are price caps determined?
A: Price caps are set according to the policies of each local government and often do not reflect the operating costs of public baths.

Q: What measures is the Japanese government taking in response to this situation?
A: Currently, no specific measures have been announced, but if the voices from the industry grow louder, policy changes may be anticipated.

In conclusion, the crisis facing Japan's public baths 'Sentō' is emerging as an important issue that extends beyond mere business problems, touching on social and cultural aspects as well. It is crucial to closely observe how the Japanese government and local communities respond at this moment.
