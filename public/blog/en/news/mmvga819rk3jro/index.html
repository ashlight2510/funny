Gyeonggi Province Climate Action Opportunity Income Listed in OECD Public Sector Innovation Casebook

News has emerged that Gyeonggi Province's ‘Climate Action Opportunity Income’ policy has been included in the OECD public sector innovation casebook. This policy provides local currency rewards to residents who participate in activities that reduce carbon emissions in their daily lives, showcasing a different approach from the existing regulation-centered carbon reduction policies. So far, over 1.84 million residents have participated, and additional benefits are expected to be provided starting in April. Gyeonggi Province stated that this listing is significant as it recognizes the policy innovation in the international community.

This policy focuses on encouraging resident participation beyond simple carbon reduction and presenting practical responses to the climate crisis. As a result, Gyeonggi Province's climate policy is gaining attention both domestically and internationally, showing potential to establish a new model for climate change response.

Key Highlights

  • Policy Innovation: Climate Action Opportunity Income is an innovative policy that reduces carbon emissions through residents' daily participation.
  • International Recognition: Listed in the OECD public sector innovation casebook, Gyeonggi Province's policy is recognized in the international community.
  • Expanded Participation: Currently, 1.84 million residents are participating, with additional benefits expected from April.
  • Reward System: Supports up to 60,000 won in local currency for various climate actions such as installing home solar panels and using public transportation.
  • Role of Residents: Responding to the climate crisis requires more than just administrative efforts; active participation from residents is crucial.

Why Background Matters

This issue can be seen as an attempt by Gyeonggi Province to draw resident participation through a new approach as the response to climate change becomes increasingly important. While existing carbon reduction policies have been limited in inducing substantial resident participation due to their regulatory focus, the Climate Action Opportunity Income is emerging as an innovative solution to overcome these limitations.

Why It Matters

The climate crisis is no longer a distant issue. To create tangible changes in each community, voluntary participation from residents is essential. Gyeonggi Province's Climate Action Opportunity Income policy is gaining attention as a model that can encourage such participation and enhance the community's climate response capabilities. Additionally, OECD recognition opens up the possibility for Gyeonggi Province's policy to be established as a best practice internationally.

My Thoughts

The greatest advantage of this policy is its structure that allows residents to directly participate and receive rewards. In reality, it can contribute to instilling the perception that small actions can lead to significant changes. However, the success of the policy depends on how actively residents participate. Therefore, Gyeonggi Province should implement various promotions and educational efforts to make this policy more appealing.

Hidden Variables to Consider

This policy does not provide equal benefits to all residents. The available climate actions for participation may be limited, and there may be differences in benefits depending on the region. Additionally, if the usage of local currency is restricted, the practical benefits may decrease, which should also be taken into account.

Current Checkpoints

  • Specific details and expected effects of the ‘City-County Reward’ system to be implemented from April.
  • Reactions and benchmarking cases from other regions or countries following the OECD casebook listing.

FAQ

A1: You can participate in various climate actions through a dedicated application and receive rewards based on your performance.

Q2: Where can I use the local currency received as a reward?
A2: The local currency can be used at shops or services within the region. However, it is necessary to check as usage may be limited.

Q3: How long will the effects of this policy last?
A3: The effects of the policy will depend on residents' participation levels and the sustainability of climate actions. Therefore, continuous interest and participation are important.

Q4: Are there plans for similar policies in other regions?
A4: Currently, various policies encouraging climate action are being discussed in other regions, and it is likely they will refer to Gyeonggi Province's case.

The Climate Action Opportunity Income policy in Gyeonggi Province holds significance beyond being just a policy. It presents an opportunity to enhance residents' awareness and participation in the climate crisis, and attention should be paid to its future development direction.
