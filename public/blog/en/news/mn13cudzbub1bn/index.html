Is Coupang the Key to Solving Local Extinction?

Coupang has recently claimed through press releases that it is contributing to solving local extinction and revitalizing the local economy. However, Professor Choi Han-byeol from Jeonbuk National University points out that Coupang's role is exaggerated and that more factors need to be considered to truly address the issue of local extinction. He emphasized that the jobs provided by Coupang are mostly non-regular positions and that accessibility for the elderly population is low, resulting in minimal impact.

This article sheds light on the impact of Coupang on the local economy and the underlying issues, showing that merely expecting the attraction of Coupang will not resolve the problem of local extinction. Local governments are actively working to attract Coupang, but questions are being raised about the labor environment issues that arise and the actual benefits to local residents.

Why Background Matters

Coupang has announced plans to contribute to the local economy by building large-scale logistics centers in cooperation with local governments in recent years. However, discussions are needed regarding what actual benefits this attraction brings to local residents. In particular, there are growing concerns that the non-regular jobs provided by Coupang could undermine the stability of the local economy.

Why It Matters

This issue is not limited to Coupang's business model. The problem of local extinction is a structural issue in Korean society, where population decline and aging are intensifying, necessitating consideration of the sustainability of local economies. While Coupang appears to contribute to the local economy, if workers' rights and quality of life are not guaranteed, it will not provide real help to local residents.

Points to Note

In my view, while Coupang's logistics centers undoubtedly bring convenience to local residents, we must not overlook the labor conditions and social costs that arise from this. What local residents truly desire are stable and sustainable jobs and services. There is a need for discussions on whether Coupang's approach meets their expectations and what policy efforts are required to achieve this.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Questions remain about whether Coupang's successful delivery service can contribute to solving the problem of local extinction. In particular, the fact that the elderly population cannot utilize online shopping suggests a need for policy approaches. Alternatives like mobile markets have been proposed, but there is a lack of infrastructure and policies to support them.

Points to Check Now

  • What alternative services or policies exist for the elderly population?
