Demolition of Weirs on the Han River and Nakdong River: A New Turning Point in Economic Viability

According to a recent report by Hankyoreh, the project to demolish and re-naturalize 11 weirs on the Nakdong River and Han River has been analyzed as economically viable. This study is based on a socio-economic analysis report commissioned by the Ministry of Environment in December 2021, estimating the total cost of weir demolition at approximately 1.6967 trillion won, while the total benefits are expected to reach about 2.8722 trillion won. The benefit-cost (B/C) ratio is reported to be 1.69, suggesting that this project will have a positive economic impact.

Such a decision allows for various positive effects, including improved water quality, ecosystem restoration, and enhanced flood control capacity. In particular, it emphasizes the positive impact of weir demolition on improving water quality in relation to the serious algal bloom problem in the Nakdong River.

Key Highlights

  • Economic Viability: The benefit-cost ratio of demolishing 11 weirs is 1.69, indicating economic feasibility.
  • Environmental Effects: Anticipated benefits include improved water quality, ecosystem restoration, and enhanced flood control capacity.
  • Beneficiaries: Residents of the Han River and Nakdong River basins are likely to directly benefit from improved water quality.
  • Cost Structure: The total cost associated with weir demolition is 1.6967 trillion won, with water utilization measures accounting for the largest share.
  • Stakeholder Opinions: Environmental groups and researchers emphasize the necessity of weir demolition and raise the need for further studies.

Why Background Context Matters

This study represents an active measure to address water quality and ecosystem issues following the Four Major Rivers Project, indicating a shift in the government's environmental policy direction. Particularly, as the adverse effects of the past Four Major Rivers Project have become evident, the need for weir demolition and re-naturalization has grown. The Ministry of Environment has previously conducted economic evaluations regarding the handling of weirs on the Geum River and Yeongsan River, and the analysis of the Han River and Nakdong River follows in continuity with this.

Why It Matters

This news is an important issue that could spark social discussions about environmental protection and sustainable development, going beyond mere economic viability. In particular, improvements in water quality and ecosystem restoration directly impact the quality of life for local residents, suggesting that these factors will play a significant role in future policy decision-making processes.

My Perspective

This aspect needs to be addressed in future research, and a more cautious approach is required, especially considering the health and safety of residents in the Nakdong River basin.

  • Gathering Residents' Opinions: There needs to be discussion on how local residents' voices will be reflected and what roles they can play in this process.
