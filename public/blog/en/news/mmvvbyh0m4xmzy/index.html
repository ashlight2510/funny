Caught, crushed, and hit... The deaths of migrant workers do not stop

The recent death of a migrant worker at a foundry manufacturing company in Gyeongbuk's Goryeong County has once again brought the issue of migrant worker safety in South Korea to the forefront. The incident, where a Thai worker in his 30s was crushed by a 2.5-ton casting while working, reveals not just a simple accident but the structural problems within South Korea's industrial sites. The poor working conditions faced by migrant workers and the resulting accidents are increasing every year, and there are criticisms that the government's and companies' measures have lost their effectiveness.

This incident cannot be dismissed as merely an individual misfortune; it highlights the serious risks and unstable working conditions that migrant workers face. So far this year, at least nine migrant workers have died, reflecting how indifferent South Korean society has been to this issue.

Key Summary

  • A Thai migrant worker was crushed by a casting in Gyeongbuk's Goryeong County.
  • There have been at least nine deaths of migrant workers this year, primarily in typical industrial accident forms.
  • Migrant workers are engaged in dangerous jobs and are in a vulnerable situation as they cannot freely change workplaces.
  • The industrial accident death rate for migrant workers is three times higher than that of South Koreans.
  • The government's measures and employers' indifference are pointed out as the root causes of the problem.

Why It Matters

This incident is not merely a personal tragedy. It is an important issue that reflects the industrial structure of South Korean society and attitudes towards migrant workers. The reality that migrant workers are often assigned to dangerous jobs without guaranteed safety shows how lacking our society is in human rights awareness. Particularly, migrant workers are in situations where it is difficult for them to assert their rights, making it crucial for society as a whole to ensure their safety and lives.

Why We Need to Look at the Background

The issue of industrial accidents among migrant workers is a long-standing structural problem in South Korean society. There is a tendency for migrant workers to be assigned to dangerous jobs that locals avoid, and they have limited legal rights to change workplaces. This environment places migrant workers in an even more vulnerable position, and the resulting accidents do not end as mere individual misfortunes but become societal problems.

Personal Comment

In my view, the safety issue of migrant workers goes beyond mere improvements in working conditions; it requires a change in social awareness. They play a crucial role in the South Korean economy, yet their lives and safety are still pushed to the background. Employers and the government must take more responsibility in creating an environment where migrant workers can work safely.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The deaths of migrant workers mentioned in the article are just reported cases, and it is highly likely that more accidents are occurring. They often find themselves in situations where it is difficult to voice their concerns even after an accident, which may lead to hidden victims.

Points to Check Now

  • It is time for a policy change from the government to ensure the rights and safety of migrant workers.
  • We should examine what efforts companies are making to create a safe working environment for migrant workers.

FAQ

Q: Why is the industrial accident death rate for migrant workers so high?
A: Migrant workers are primarily assigned to dangerous jobs that locals avoid, and they are in a more vulnerable situation due to limited freedom to change workplaces.

Q: How is the government addressing the safety issues of migrant workers?
A: The government has implemented various policies to reduce industrial accidents, but there are still criticisms regarding their effectiveness.

Q: Is there no way for migrant workers to easily change workplaces?
A: Currently, legal restrictions limit the ability to change workplaces, preventing an environment where migrant workers can work freely.

This issue is not merely an individual misfortune but a structural problem within our society, and it is time to pay attention to the safety and rights of migrant workers.
