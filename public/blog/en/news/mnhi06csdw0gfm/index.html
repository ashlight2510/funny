Iranian authorities execute death penalty for anti-government protest participant···Amnesty: “Extremely unfair trial relying on forced confessions”

Recently, a death sentence was executed for a man who participated in anti-government protests in Iran. Iranian judicial authorities announced that Amirhossein Hatami was executed after being convicted of attacking a military secret facility. Hatami was charged with trespassing into a military facility during the protests, attempting arson, damaging property, and attempting to seize weapons. However, the international human rights organization Amnesty criticized that this case was based on forced confessions and an unfair trial.

This incident is intertwined with the violent suppression of anti-government protests in Iran, and the Iranian government's harsh response has sparked significant controversy in the international community. The Iranian authorities regard these protests as a coup and justify their suppression through severe punishments.

This incident is closely related to the anti-government protests that occurred in Iran. Following large-scale protests in January, the Iranian government responded severely, resulting in numerous casualties among protesters. The Iranian government views these protests as acts threatening national security and has taken strong legal measures. The execution of Hatami can be seen as part of these legal actions.
