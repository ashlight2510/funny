Even after the war ends, high oil prices continue... “Stuck at $90 per barrel until the end of next year”

According to a recent report, even if the war between the U.S. and Iran ends, international oil prices are expected to remain over 40% higher than pre-war levels until next year. The Korea Institute for International Economic Policy warned that due to delays in the recovery of energy facilities, oil prices could reach $90 per barrel by the fourth quarter of next year. If the war escalates, there is also a possibility that prices could soar to $174 per barrel. This has implications beyond mere numbers and is expected to have a significant impact on the global economy and energy market.

The rise in oil prices goes beyond just energy costs and could have ripple effects across the global economy. In particular, industries dependent on energy supply, as well as general consumers, could feel direct impacts. This article will summarize the key points of this issue and explore why it is important to pay attention to it at this time.

Key Summary

  • International oil prices are expected to remain above $90 per barrel until next year.
  • If the war escalates, oil prices could rise to $174 per barrel.
  • Delays in the recovery of energy facilities are a major contributing factor.
  • Navigation restrictions in the Strait of Hormuz are expected to impact global crude oil production.
  • The International Energy Agency (IEA) has also warned that supply disruptions will worsen.

Why It Matters

This news is not just about the numbers related to oil prices. Oil prices serve as a crucial indicator of the global economy, and for countries and companies highly dependent on energy, it is a matter of survival. For example, a sharp rise in oil prices increases transportation and production costs, which in turn affects final consumer prices. This can lead to inflation, negatively impacting the economy as a whole.

Why We Need to Look at the Background

This issue has arisen as tensions between the U.S. and Iran have escalated. With the outbreak of war and damage to energy facilities, concerns have grown about disruptions to the global energy supply chain. In particular, the attack on Qatar's Ras Laffan gas complex has caused a spike in naphtha prices, severely impacting the supply of petrochemical raw materials. In this context, the forecasts from the Korea Institute for International Economic Policy further highlight the instability in the overall energy market.

Personal Interpretation

In my view, the most critical aspect of this issue is not just the numerical rise in oil prices but the economic imbalances that may arise from it. The increase in energy prices can place a greater burden on low-income countries and households, potentially leading to social discontent. Additionally, it could create job instability for those working in energy-related industries.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect that this article does not focus on is the response of various governments. How governments will respond to inflationary pressures due to rising oil prices and the actual impact of those policies are very important variables. For instance, some countries may provide energy subsidies or adjust taxes, but this could increase fiscal burdens.

Current Points to Monitor

It is crucial to continuously monitor key indicators related to oil prices and policy changes in various countries. In particular, we should observe what measures are taken to ensure the stability of energy supply and whether these measures will be effective.

FAQ

Q: What impact will rising international oil prices have on consumers?
A: The rise in oil prices increases transportation and production costs, which affects final consumer prices. This can lead to inflation.

Q: Will oil prices stabilize after the war ends?
A: Even if the war ends, delays in the recovery of energy facilities are expected to keep oil prices at a high level.

Q: How will governments respond to rising oil prices?
A: Governments may respond to rising oil prices through various policies such as providing energy subsidies or adjusting taxes, but this could increase fiscal burdens.

Q: What is the situation in the Strait of Hormuz?
A: If navigation restrictions in the Strait of Hormuz continue, global crude oil production is expected to decrease, which will directly impact oil prices.

Considering all these factors, the current oil price issue is an important matter that could have deep impacts beyond just economic concerns, affecting society as a whole. Therefore, it is essential for readers to closely monitor news related to oil prices and policy changes in various countries at this time.
