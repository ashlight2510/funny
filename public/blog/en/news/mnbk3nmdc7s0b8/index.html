“Unsinkable Aircraft Carrier”... Seven Islands in the Strait of Hormuz Rise as Iran's Key Defense Line

Amid rising military tensions in the Middle East, the seven islands located in the Strait of Hormuz are gaining attention as Iran's defense line. According to CNN's report, there is an analysis that these islands could serve as strategic points in Iran's military operations, closely related to the U.S. deployment of additional naval and Marine Corps forces. In particular, it is emphasized that the Iranian Revolutionary Guard can control the strait and monitor external warships and vessels from these islands.

These islands are emerging as key elements that could determine the success or failure of military operations, going beyond mere geographical locations. Specifically, Lesser Tunb, Greater Tunb, and Abu Musa Island serve as major bases for controlling the strait and play an essential role in Iran's military strategy. Iranian military experts have likened these islands to “unsinkable aircraft carriers,” warning of the potential for attacks on vessels passing through the strait.

Key Points

  • They could function as maritime routes vulnerable to fast attack boats and drone strikes from the Iranian Revolutionary Guard.
  • There are opinions that occupying these islands would be strategically important for U.S. military operations.
  • Territorial disputes between the islands could act as potential factors for diplomatic repercussions.
  • Concerns are growing about the impact of U.S. military approaches on the relationship between Iran and the United Arab Emirates.
