Time Travel to Incheon’s Dal-dong Neighborhood 55 Years Ago, Reopening of Sudokuksan Museum

Recently, the Sudokuksan Dal-dong Neighborhood Museum in Songhyeon Neighborhood Park, Dong-gu, Incheon, has reopened. This museum is a space that realistically recreates life in the Dal-dong neighborhood of 1971, showcasing how ordinary citizens in Incheon lived in the past. The exhibition hall displays various items used by people at that time, providing visitors with an opportunity to emotionally connect with memories of that era.

The reopening, which involved an investment of 15 billion won to expand the exhibition space, has attracted many visitors who come to experience life from the past. In particular, it has become a space for family visitors to share intergenerational empathy.

Key Summary

  • Sudokuksan Dal-dong Neighborhood Museum reopened with an investment of 15 billion won.
  • Exhibition space realistically recreates life in the Dal-dong neighborhood of 1971.
  • Visitors revive memories of the past through historical items and share intergenerational empathy.
  • During the trial operation period, over 15,000 visitors came, with more than 800 visiting on weekends.
  • The museum plays an important role in showcasing the evolution of ordinary people's lives in the past.

  • Diversity of Exhibits: Engaging visitors' interest through a variety of items and recreated spaces.

Reasons to Consider the Background

Sudokuksan is a place name that originated during a time when water was scarce in Incheon, leading to the establishment of a reservoir for water storage. In the early 20th century, Koreans who lost their homes to the Japanese military settled here, forming the Dal-dong neighborhood. The area has since undergone significant changes due to the Korean War and urban development. The museum first opened in 2005, and this reopening is part of an effort to shed light on those changes and remember the lives of forgotten ordinary people.

Why It Matters

This museum goes beyond merely recreating the past; it offers important lessons for current and future generations. By sharing the stories of those who endured difficult times, it provides an opportunity to connect with today’s social issues. Additionally, it contributes to strengthening community identity and preserving cultural heritage.

FAQ

Q: When did the Sudokuksan Dal-dong Neighborhood Museum reopen?
A: The museum reopened on March 1, 2023.

Q: How many visitors does it receive?
A: Approximately 15,000 visitors came during the trial operation period, with about 600-700 on weekdays and over 800 on weekends.

Q: What exhibits are available at the museum?
A: The museum features old electronic products such as black-and-white TVs, telephones, radios, and a space that recreates life in the Dal-dong neighborhood of 1971.

Q: What should visitors be aware of when visiting the museum?
A: It is recommended to check the exhibition schedule and special programs before visiting, and to come with family to facilitate intergenerational conversations.
