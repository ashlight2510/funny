87% of Affected Residents at 'High Risk' for PTSD… Psychological Aftermath of Wildfire Damage

A survey has shown that 87% of residents affected by the large-scale wildfires in the Yeongnam region belong to the high-risk category for post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). This survey, conducted by Greenpeace East Asia's Seoul office and several research institutions, revealed that the elderly, women, and long-term residents are particularly vulnerable to PTSD. Notably, higher trust in administration tends to correlate with lower PTSD risk, and the majority of residents reported a lack of information regarding wildfire damage and recovery.

These findings highlight a significant issue that transcends mere statistical figures, potentially having profound implications for the psychological stability of affected residents and the resilience of the community. It is crucial to note that the impact of wildfire damage extends beyond material losses, leaving serious psychological aftereffects on residents.

Key Summary

  • 87% of residents affected by the Yeongnam wildfires are classified as high-risk for PTSD.
  • The elderly, women, and long-term residents are particularly vulnerable, and trust in administration affects PTSD risk.
  • Residents report a lack of information about damage and recovery, leading to psychological distress.
  • The percentage of those in serious PTSD risk reaches 67%, while the normal range is only 8%.

Key Highlights

  • PTSD Risk Group Ratio: 87% classified as high-risk.
  • Trust in Administration: Trust in damage assessment and recovery support critically influences PTSD risk.
  • Lack of Information: 75% of residents are unaware of the details of recovery support funds.
  • Characteristics of High-Risk Group: The elderly and long-term residents are particularly vulnerable.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The large-scale wildfires in the Yeongnam region caused significant material damage to many residents. However, the survey results clearly reveal psychological aftereffects that cannot be explained solely by material losses. Particularly, the loss of trust in administration among affected residents can exacerbate psychological distress during the recovery process, providing important insights. Such a lack of information can further increase residents' anxiety and adversely affect long-term recovery.

Why It Matters

This news carries significance beyond mere statistics. PTSD is a mental disorder that can severely impact an individual's life, and neglecting it can lead to increased social costs. The psychological suffering experienced by affected residents also impacts the resilience of the community, ultimately affecting local economies and social stability. Moreover, the response from the government and local authorities plays a crucial role in the psychological stability of residents, indicating an urgent need for policy improvements.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The characteristics of the high-risk group mentioned in the survey should not only be understood statistically but also in terms of the actual psychological suffering they endure. For example, residents who experienced temporary shelter living are 2.3 times more likely to belong to the PTSD high-risk group, illustrating the impact of shelter environments on individual psychological stability. Overlooking such detailed contexts could hinder the effectiveness of policy implementation.

Points to Check Now

  • Information Dissemination System: The system for communicating information about recovery support and damage assessment needs improvement.
  • Incorporating Resident Feedback: Policies that actively reflect the opinions of affected residents are necessary.
