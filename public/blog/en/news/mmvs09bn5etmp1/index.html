Mandatory Facial Recognition for Mobile Phone Activation: Key Points to Consider

March 18, 2026 · 한겨레 · View source

Original article at a glance Title: Civic groups including the Participation Solidarity say, “The facial recognition policy for mobile phone activation should be abolished.” Source: Hankyoreh Starting from the 24th, a policy mandating facial recognition for activating new mobile phones is set to be fully implemented. Civic groups are urging for the complete abolition of the policy, citing significant risks of personal data infringement. Civil society organizations, including the Participation Solidarity and the Digital Justice Network, held a press conference in front of the Seoul Government Complex in Jongno-gu on the afternoon of the 18th. Read the original article

Civic groups including the Participation Solidarity say, “The facial recognition policy for mobile phone activation should be abolished.”

Civic groups are strongly opposing the government's upcoming policy that mandates facial recognition for mobile phone activation. They argue that the policy poses a significant risk of personal data infringement and infringes on citizens' rights without addressing fundamental issues, calling for its immediate abolition. The Participation Solidarity and the Digital Justice Network pointed out in a press conference that while the government's policy aims to prevent the use of burner phones and voice phishing crimes, it could actually threaten individual data protection.

This controversy has intensified as the government announced that it will fully implement a policy that has already been piloted since last year starting from the 24th. Civic groups warn that if facial recognition data is leaked or misused, there would be no way to recover from the damage, which could impact individuals for a lifetime.

Key Summary

  • Civic groups demand the immediate abolition of the facial recognition policy for mobile phone activation.
  • The policy aims to prevent burner phones and voice phishing crimes and is set for full implementation after a pilot run.
  • Concerns about irreversible damage if facial recognition data is leaked.

Key Scenes

  • Concerns about personal data infringement: If facial recognition data is leaked, irreversible damage could occur.
  • Fundamental issues with the policy: There is a lack of fundamental measures to address the voice phishing problem.
  • Opposition from civic groups: Civic groups, including the Participation Solidarity, are strongly opposing the government and calling for the policy's abolition.
  • Trust issues: Telecom companies have lost trust due to past personal data leaks.
  • Feasibility of the policy: Concerns about side effects and citizen dissatisfaction if the policy is implemented.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This issue arose when the government decided to mandate facial recognition to prevent burner phones and voice phishing crimes. After instances of identity verification through ID cards being misused with counterfeit IDs, the government sought safer verification methods. However, civic groups argue that such measures could infringe on basic rights and raise significant concerns about data protection.

Why It Matters

This policy is not just a change in technical verification methods; it encompasses important issues related to personal data protection. Facial recognition, as biometric information, is a unique characteristic of individuals, and if this information is leaked, irreversible harm can occur. Additionally, it serves as a case showing how much citizen voices are reflected in government policy-making processes. This could set a significant precedent for other personal data-related policies in the future.

Personal Interpretation

In my view, this issue transcends the mere introduction of facial recognition; it represents a conflict in the process of building trust regarding how the government and corporations manage and protect personal data. The opposition from civic groups is not just a complaint about the policy but raises more fundamental issues related to data protection, which deserves attention.

Hidden Variables to Consider

If this policy is implemented, there is a lack of specific examples of how many citizens will be affected. For instance, awareness is needed regarding the potential harm that could arise from data leaks after activating a mobile phone through facial recognition. Furthermore, the state of personal data management by telecom companies and past leak incidents should not be overlooked in relation to this policy.

Points to Check Now

  • Policy implementation schedule: Specific details and procedures of the facial recognition mandate policy starting from the 24th.
  • Ongoing activities of civic groups: Future plans and government responses from the Participation Solidarity and the Digital Justice Network.
  • Laws related to personal data protection: How current laws and systems regarding personal data protection are being operated.

FAQ

Q: What changes will occur if the facial recognition mandate policy is implemented? A: When activating a new mobile phone or transferring a number, facial recognition will be mandatory, and the previous ID card presentation method will be eliminated.

Q: How will personal data be protected if this policy is implemented? A: The government has stated it will establish various measures for personal data protection, but civic groups question whether actual protection will be achieved.

Q: Why do civic groups oppose this policy? A: Civic groups argue that if facial recognition data is leaked, there would be no way to recover from the damage, which could affect individuals for a lifetime.

Q: How should we address this issue in the future? A: Citizens should raise awareness about personal data protection and continue to monitor and demand accountability from the government and corporations.
