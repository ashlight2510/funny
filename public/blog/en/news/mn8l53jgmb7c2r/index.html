Busan Transportation Corporation Starts Primary and Subcontract Negotiations as the First Public Enterprise

Busan Transportation Corporation has embarked on primary and subcontract negotiations as the first public enterprise. On the 27th, negotiations aimed at concluding a collective agreement began through a labor-management-government meeting. In this process, Busan Transportation Corporation is conducting joint negotiations with workers from both the parent company and its subsidiaries. Since the implementation of the Yellow Envelope Law, Busan Transportation Corporation is the only public enterprise to respond to negotiations, and the labor sector emphasizes that these negotiations should lead to a collective agreement.

This negotiation holds significant meaning not only for Busan Transportation Corporation but also for the public sector as a whole. The union is demanding improvements in wages and working conditions, and the Ministry of Employment and Labor expects that the actions of Busan Transportation Corporation will have a positive impact on other public institutions.

Key Highlights

  • Busan Transportation Corporation has initiated primary and subcontract negotiations, marking a first for public enterprises.
  • It is the only public enterprise to respond to negotiations since the implementation of the Yellow Envelope Law.
  • The labor sector is strongly demanding the conclusion of a collective agreement.
  • Improvements in wages and working conditions are key agenda items during the negotiations.
  • There is a possibility that the case of Busan Transportation Corporation will spread to other public institutions.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The implementation of the Yellow Envelope Law has brought significant changes to the labor sector. This law stipulates that the primary contractor must also be responsible for subcontract workers, and despite at least 298 unions in the public sector requesting negotiations, only a few, including Busan Transportation Corporation, Hwaseong City, and Incheon Medical Center, have responded. By starting negotiations in this context, Busan Transportation Corporation has the potential to spark a wave of change.

Why It Matters

The primary and subcontract negotiations at Busan Transportation Corporation are not just an isolated case of one public enterprise. If this process proceeds successfully, it could lay the groundwork for similar negotiations in other public institutions and private enterprises. This could be a crucial turning point in improving workers' rights and addressing issues related to non-regular workers.

Points to Watch

If the negotiations at Busan Transportation Corporation lead to a collective agreement, it will have legal effects on all matters effectively controlled by the primary contractor. This provides an opportunity for substantial improvements in the working conditions of workers. In particular, subcontract workers affiliated with Busan Transportation Corporation will be direct beneficiaries of this agreement.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Busan Transportation Corporation has also expressed concerns about difficulties in securing funding and issues related to illegal dispatch. These variables could impact the outcomes and sustainability of the negotiations. In other words, even if the negotiations proceed successfully, there is a possibility that the demands may not be fully reflected due to funding issues.

Current Points to Check

It is essential to continuously monitor how the negotiations at Busan Transportation Corporation progress and what impact the results will have on other public institutions. In particular, information on how the negotiation outcomes will affect wages and working conditions will be an important indicator for understanding the future direction of the labor sector.
