Gulf States Urge Neutralization of Iran, Yet Face Dilemma of War Participation

Recently, it has been reported that Gulf states in the Middle East have urged the U.S. to continue the war to neutralize Iran's military power, but at the same time, they are hesitant to participate in the war due to concerns of Iranian retaliation and escalation. Given that Iran's military threat could seriously impact these countries' oil-dependent economies, this issue reveals a situation that is more complex than simple diplomatic discussions.

Since the outbreak of the war with Iran, Gulf states have shifted to a position supporting U.S. attacks, but they are also exercising restraint in direct military intervention due to fears of Iranian retaliatory attacks. The potential threat of Iranian military attacks to the energy supply chains in the Gulf region significantly influences the strategic choices of these countries.

Key Summary

  • Gulf states are urging the U.S. to continue the war to neutralize Iran's military power.
  • However, they are refraining from direct military involvement due to concerns of Iranian retaliation and escalation.
  • Iranian military attacks threaten the oil and gas-based economies of Gulf states.
  • Gulf states are worried that without U.S. support, they could be isolated in a full-scale war with Iran.
  • Tensions among Gulf states are escalating due to the threat of Iranian attacks.

Key Scenes

  • The Gulf states' call for the neutralization of Iran is part of protecting their oil economies.
  • Iran's military threat directly impacts the security of Gulf states.
  • The presence of U.S. military support is a crucial variable in the strategic decisions of Gulf states.
  • The possibility of Iranian retaliatory attacks is a factor hindering military intervention by Gulf states.
  • Gulf states are raising their alertness to Iranian military attacks.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The conflict with Iran has a long historical background, and Gulf states have opposed U.S. attacks on Iran in the past. However, following the recent outbreak of war, as Iranian attacks pose a direct threat to the oil and gas-based economies of Gulf states, they are strongly urging the U.S. to neutralize Iran. Iranian attacks have the potential to hold the energy supply chains of Gulf states hostage, significantly influencing their strategic choices.

Why It Matters

This issue transcends mere military conflict and could have significant implications for energy security and economic stability in the Middle East. Gulf states are increasing their alertness to Iranian military attacks, which could also have ripple effects on the international energy market. If the Iranian military threat continues, Gulf states may have no choice but to consider stronger military responses, which could escalate regional conflicts.

Personal Perspective

In my view, while it is understandable that Gulf states show a willingness to neutralize Iran, the strategic choices made in the process are very important. If a full-scale war with Iran occurs, the damage will not be limited to military losses but could also lead to economic impacts and regional instability. Therefore, while the military threat from Iran cannot be ignored, it is crucial to avoid escalation into full-scale war.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Many may think that Gulf states can completely neutralize the Iranian threat simply by receiving U.S. support, but it is essential not to overlook the impact of Iranian military retaliation on Gulf states. Additionally, if Gulf states become isolated in their conflict with Iran, their strategic choices could become even more challenging.

Current Points to Check

  • The possibility of Iranian military retaliation and the corresponding military responses from Gulf states.
  • The implications of U.S. military support and the resulting political changes in the Middle East.
