The Extreme Heat Warning, introduced for the first time this year, is the highest level of warning issued when extreme high temperatures that pose a significant risk of serious harm, including death, are expected for all citizens, including healthy individuals. Establishment of Extreme Heat Warning, a Signal of Heat

To prepare for extreme heat this summer, a new 'Extreme Heat Warning' will be introduced. This warning will be issued when the maximum perceived temperature exceeds 38 degrees or the actual temperature exceeds 39 degrees, setting it as the highest level of warning above the existing heat warning. The Korea Meteorological Administration emphasizes that this change is aimed at more effectively protecting the health and safety of the public, with the new warning system scheduled to be implemented from June 1.

The introduction of the Extreme Heat Warning is understood as a measure to address the limitations of the existing warning system and to raise public awareness. As the frequency of heatwaves increases due to climate change, the accumulation of 'warning fatigue' regarding heat warnings has made such measures necessary.

Key Summary

  • Extreme Heat Warning: The highest level of warning issued when the perceived temperature exceeds 38 degrees or the actual temperature exceeds 39 degrees.
  • Limitations of Existing Warnings: The existing heat warning has been criticized for not adequately conveying the extreme heat that threatens lives, leading to decreased awareness.
  • Establishment of Tropical Night Advisory: Issued when the minimum nighttime temperature remains above 25 degrees for two consecutive days.
  • Background for Introduction: Increasing frequency of heatwaves due to climate change and accumulated warning fatigue.
  • Expected Frequency: The Extreme Heat Warning is estimated to be issued about once every 10 years.

Why Is It Important?

The establishment of the Extreme Heat Warning is not merely a change in weather information. It is an important safety measure that can directly impact the health and lives of citizens. Particularly, as climate change leads to more frequent heatwaves, a more sophisticated and systematic warning system has become necessary. This warning will significantly affect vulnerable groups, such as the elderly and those at high risk. Since these individuals are more susceptible to extreme heat, timely issuance of such warnings can be crucial in saving lives.

Even if a warning is issued, it is meaningless if the public does not recognize it and respond appropriately. Therefore, the government and local authorities must prepare swift and effective response measures when a warning is issued, and continuously educate the public about the importance of the warnings.
