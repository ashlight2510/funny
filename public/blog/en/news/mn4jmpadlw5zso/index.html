Differentiation is now an outdated topic... The key issue is how far to expand the minimum wage

Amid heated discussions on the minimum wage, the conflict between labor and management is becoming more pronounced. The labor sector advocates for the expansion of minimum wage application to special employment and platform workers, while the management sector demands differentiated application by industry and region. Particularly, with the number of non-wage workers reaching 8.7 million, there is a growing call for urgent measures to protect their rights.

In this context, it is noteworthy that the direction of the minimum wage system is shifting towards 'how far it will be expanded.' This goes beyond simply raising the minimum wage and relates to significant issues concerning structural changes in the labor market.

Key Summary

  • In the discussions on the minimum wage, the positions of labor and management are opposing, with the main issues being the expansion of minimum wage application to special employment and platform workers and differentiated application.
  • There are demands for differentiated application referencing Japan's minimum wage system, but Japan is moving towards reducing regional disparities.
  • The discussions on the minimum wage are at a crucial stage, highlighting the need for new systems related to the protection of non-wage workers.
  • New ideas such as minimum wage for minimum compensation and subcontracting minimum wage are being proposed, and discussions on these are expected to be significant issues this year.

Key Scenes

  • Conflict between labor and management: The labor sector emphasizes the protection of non-wage workers, while the management sector seeks to reduce wage burdens through differentiation.
  • Non-wage worker issues: Approximately 8.7 million non-wage workers are in a blind spot regarding minimum wage protection.
  • Japanese case: Japan's minimum wage system is changing to reduce regional disparities, which is somewhat distant from the demands for differentiation in Korea.
  • Need for new systems: Proposals for minimum compensation systems and subcontracting minimum wage are being discussed, which could contribute to the protection of workers' rights and income security.

Background and Context of the Issue

Discussions on the minimum wage have been ongoing for several years. In particular, the number of non-wage workers has surged in the 2020s, and these individuals are not protected by labor standards. This change is intertwined with structural changes in the labor market, and there is a growing need to adjust the direction of the minimum wage system accordingly.

Following President Lee Jae-myung's remarks on the 'minimum compensation system,' discussions on the expansion of minimum wage application have intensified, but specific discussions have been postponed due to the need for fact-finding investigations. As deliberations begin this year in the Minimum Wage Commission, it is expected that specific directions on these issues will be determined.

Why It Matters

The minimum wage is an important social issue that goes beyond mere wage concerns, directly affecting workers' livelihoods. Particularly, as the number of non-wage workers increases, if systems to protect them are not established, social inequality could deepen. Therefore, this discussion is an essential process for ensuring fairness in the labor market and strengthening the social safety net.

Personal Observations

In my view, the most important aspect of the minimum wage discussion is the protection measures for non-wage workers. In a situation where they are not protected by the minimum wage system, simply raising the minimum wage will not resolve the issue. Rather, I believe it is more urgent to establish appropriate income security measures for them.

Additionally, when considering the Japanese case, it is interesting to note that they are moving towards reducing regional disparities through differentiated application. In Korea, however, there is a significant possibility that differentiation could work against workers, necessitating a more cautious approach.

Hidden Variables to Consider

While this discussion appears to present a dichotomy between the expansion of minimum wage application and differentiation, it is crucial to fully consider the purposes and effects of each system. For instance, Japan's industry-specific minimum wage system is designed to create higher wages, whereas in Korea, there are arguments for applying lower wages. Overlooking this could lead to missing the essence of the minimum wage discussion.

Points to Check Now

  • Minimum Wage Commission deliberation schedule: Attention should be paid to how the deliberations in the Minimum Wage Commission will proceed this year.
  • Protection measures for non-wage workers: It is necessary to check whether specific protection measures for these workers are being discussed.
  • Analysis of the Japanese case: It is important to examine what lessons Japan's minimum wage system can provide for Korea.

FAQ

Q: What is the difference between expanding minimum wage application and differentiated application?
A: Expanding minimum wage application means applying the same minimum wage to all workers, while differentiated application means setting different minimum wages based on industry or region.

Q: What are non-wage workers?
A: Non-wage workers are those not recognized as workers under labor standards law, including platform workers and those in special employment.

Q: What is the minimum compensation system?
A: The minimum compensation system is a system that guarantees a minimum income for workers not recognized as employees, such as the safety freight system applied to freight workers.

As these discussions progress, we must closely monitor how the minimum wage system will change and what impact those changes will have on workers.
