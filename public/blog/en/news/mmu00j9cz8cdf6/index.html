Seoul City Raises Housing Floor Area Ratio by 30% to Supply 110,000 Units

Seoul City has recently announced a plan to increase the floor area ratio to 30% in transit-oriented areas to expand housing supply. Mayor Oh Se-hoon elaborated on this plan at the Shin-gil Station area in Yeongdeungpo-gu, expressing expectations that approximately 117,000 housing units could be supplied through this initiative. This policy aims to provide quality housing more quickly for young people, newlyweds, and those without homes, and will be pursued through redevelopment.

These changes include relaxing the existing standards for transit-oriented housing projects and expanding the project areas. Notably, the inclusion of intersections of major roads as project sites allows for the development of areas that are convenient for transportation but have been overlooked, potentially adding about 239 new sites across Seoul.

Key Highlights

  • Increased Floor Area Ratio: The floor area ratio for transit-oriented housing projects will be increased by up to 30% to promote housing supply.
  • Expanded Project Areas: The project area will be expanded from within 500m to within 200m of major road intersections.
  • Accelerated Project Implementation: The integration of preliminary review procedures will shorten project timelines by more than 5 months.
  • Incentives for Small Housing Supply: Additional floor area ratio incentives will be provided for supplying more than 20% of small housing units with a floor area of 60㎡ or less.
  • Housing Supply Impact: Approximately 117,000 housing units are expected to be made available.

Reasons to Consider the Background

Seoul City has been implementing various policies to address the housing supply shortage. A primary goal is to provide quality housing for young people and newlyweds who are without homes. This policy is part of that effort, readjusting past housing project standards to enable a quicker supply of more housing. Additionally, it aims to improve the living environment within the city through the development of conveniently located areas.

Why It Matters

This policy is expected to significantly contribute to resolving Seoul's housing supply issues. It particularly offers practical assistance to demographics such as young people and newlyweds who require housing stability. Furthermore, housing supply that considers transportation convenience is anticipated to enhance the quality of life. This goes beyond mere housing provision, positively impacting urban development and living conditions in Seoul.

Points of Attention

One noteworthy aspect of this policy is the incentives for supplying small housing units. This reflects the increasing demand for small housing in the market and aligns with the preferences of those without homes. Moreover, improvements in administrative efficiency, such as shortening project durations, are also critical points. However, it remains to be seen how quickly these improvements will be realized in practice.

Hidden Variables to Watch

While the article emphasizes the figures and incentives presented by Seoul City, it is uncertain how these changes will be implemented on the ground. For instance, the impact of increased building density due to higher floor area ratios on local residents, or potential issues arising from excessive development in areas lacking sufficient infrastructure, are often overlooked.

Current Points to Check

It is crucial to closely monitor the changes and residents' reactions as Seoul City's transit-oriented housing supply policy is implemented. Specifically, it is important to check how small housing units will be supplied in practice and how this will affect the living environment.

Q: Which areas will be affected if this policy is implemented?
A: It will impact not only transit-oriented areas but also regions near major road intersections, enabling additional supply.

Q: How do the incentives for small housing supply work?
