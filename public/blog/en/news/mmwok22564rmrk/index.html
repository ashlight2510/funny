Last year's domestic bank net profit of 24 trillion won, the largest ever... earned 60 trillion won from interest business

Last year, domestic banks recorded a net profit of over 24 trillion won, achieving the largest figure ever. According to the Financial Supervisory Service's '2025 Domestic Bank Business Performance', their net profit increased by 8.2% compared to the previous year due to increased profits from foreign exchange and derivatives. Notably, interest income exceeded 60 trillion won, indicating that banks' revenue structure heavily relies on interest business. In contrast, local banks showed a decrease in net profit, revealing an imbalance.

This growth is the result of a complex context that cannot be evaluated by numbers alone. As volatility in interest rates and exchange rates increased, profits related to foreign exchange and derivatives surged; however, it remains uncertain whether this situation will continue. Financial authorities emphasize the need to strengthen banks' loss absorption capacity as concerns about expanding credit losses grow.

Key Highlights

  • Last year's domestic bank net profit of 24 trillion won, a record high
  • Interest income of 60 trillion won, a 1.8% increase from the previous year
  • Foreign exchange and derivatives profits increased by 5.7 trillion won
  • Local banks saw a decrease in net profit, with slower growth compared to large banks
  • Financial authorities mention the need for stability enhancement due to concerns over expanding credit losses

Reasons to Consider the Background

This growth is the result of multiple factors working in conjunction. As interest rates continued to rise, interest income increased, and simultaneously, volatility in the foreign exchange and derivatives markets surged, leading to significant profits. However, it is uncertain how long this boom will last. External factors, such as geopolitical risks in the Middle East and U.S. tariff policies, could have serious implications for the domestic financial market.

Why It Matters

This news impacts not just the profitability of banks but also the financial environment for ordinary consumers and businesses. As interest rates rise, loan interest rates will also increase, leading to a greater burden on consumers and businesses. Additionally, the decrease in net profit for local banks could negatively affect the regional economy. Therefore, changes in this financial environment hold significant implications for the economy as a whole.

My Interpretation

In my view, the increase in banks' net profit is not merely a positive signal. There are lingering questions about the sustainability of rising interest income, and especially the difficulties faced by local banks pose a risk of further exacerbating regional economic imbalances. Strengthening the loss absorption capacity of financial authorities is essential, and through this, the stability of the financial system should be enhanced.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect not mentioned in the article is the tightening of lending standards by banks. As interest income increases, lending criteria are likely to become stricter. This could negatively impact small and medium-sized enterprises or individual borrowers. Additionally, risk management strategies in response to market volatility could also play a significant role.

Points to Check Now

  • Future changes in interest rate policies and responses from financial authorities
  • Changes in management strategies of local banks and their impact on the regional economy
  • Whether the volatility in the foreign exchange and derivatives markets will persist

A: It could exacerbate regional economic imbalances, which may directly affect local residents.

Q: What is the likelihood of continued increases in foreign exchange and derivatives profits?
A: Due to significant external factors, it is difficult to predict with certainty.

Q: Why is strengthening the loss absorption capacity of financial authorities important?
