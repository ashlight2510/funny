Jung Won-oh “Registered rental, loopholes in jeonse fraud and supporting multiple home ownership... Oh Se-hoon should stop blaming others”

Jung Won-oh, a preliminary candidate for the Seoul mayor from the Democratic Party of Korea, recently criticized Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon for attributing the instability of the jeonse market to the government. He argued that the combination of the current government's failure in housing supply and Mayor Oh's incompetent administration has exacerbated today's housing issues, pointing out that the registered rental system encourages jeonse fraud and creates a favorable structure for multiple homeowners. These remarks are reigniting controversies related to housing policies in Seoul.

This article is part of the ongoing discussion about the crisis facing Seoul's housing market and the political responsibilities involved, raising social interest in policy directions for solving housing issues. In particular, an analysis of how the registered rental system is promoting jeonse fraud is timely.

Why Background Matters

This statement comes at a time when Seoul's housing market is facing a serious crisis. In recent years, jeonse prices in Seoul have skyrocketed, and incidents of jeonse fraud have become frequent, significantly threatening citizens' housing stability. In this context, Jung Won-oh is highlighting fundamental issues in housing policy and demanding political accountability. The registered rental system was originally introduced to stabilize housing for the working class, but criticism is growing that it has instead led to negative side effects.

Why It Matters

This issue goes beyond mere political controversy. The instability in the housing market directly affects the lives of citizens, placing a significant burden on low-income and young people. Jung Won-oh's remarks provide an opportunity to rethink the direction of housing policy and convey an important message about the accountability that citizens should demand from politicians.
