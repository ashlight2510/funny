Humanoids ushering in the 'Physical AI' era… “Self-sufficiency in core components and seizing standards is urgent”

Recently, claims have been made that the 'Physical AI' era is officially opening in South Korea. Experts in the Korean robotics industry emphasize that the development of humanoid robots goes beyond mere technological innovation and has a significant impact on the country's industrial ecosystem and international competitiveness. In particular, there is a consensus that self-sufficiency in core components and seizing international standards is urgent.

In this regard, a forum held by the Korea Institute for Advancement of Technology highlighted that humanoids are not just products but a complex industry combining AI, semiconductor sensors, batteries, software, and more, discussing the necessary policies and strategies for this. As competition in the global market intensifies, the need for a systematic approach for Korean companies to lead these changes has been underscored.

Key Summary

  • The Korean humanoid robot industry is entering the Physical AI era, highlighting the need for self-sufficiency in core components and international standardization.
  • Forum participants emphasized the urgency of formulating policies to create an industrial ecosystem and secure technological competitiveness through the convergence of AI and robotics.
  • Global investment banks predict rapid growth in the humanoid market, stating that Korean companies need a strategic approach to gain an advantage in this market.

Key Points

  • Self-sufficiency in core components: There is a need to reduce dependence on overseas sources and stabilize the domestic supply chain.
  • Seizing international standards: Standardization is essential to maintain competitiveness in the global market.
  • Training specialized talent: It is crucial to cultivate specialized talent in the field where AI and robotics converge.
  • Expanding testing environments: Rapid testing in industrial settings should be conducted to enhance the applicability of technologies.
  • Need for policy support: There is a call for comprehensive promotion policies at the national level.

Why Background Matters

This issue has emerged as Korean companies feel the necessity to secure competitiveness in the humanoid industry amid intensifying global technological competition. With countries like the United States and China already positioning this field as a future strategic industry, there is a growing awareness in Korea that a response is urgently needed. As Korean companies' humanoids gain attention at CES 2026, public interest has also risen.

Why It Matters

Humanoid robots are a field that can significantly impact various industries beyond mere technological innovation. In particular, changes in employment structures and industrial ecosystems are expected, which will directly affect many people's lives. Therefore, to seize opportunities to gain an advantage in this market, swift policy formulation and technological development are essential.

Points of Attention

In my view, the most important points emphasized in this forum are 'self-sufficiency in core components' and 'seizing international standards.' If Korea does not establish a supply chain based on its own technological capabilities, it risks becoming dependent on foreign companies. This issue goes beyond merely losing technological competitiveness; it is directly linked to the country's economic independence.

Hidden Variables to Consider

While the article discusses various strategies for Korean companies to enter the humanoid industry, it does not address the technological limitations or resource shortages they may face. For instance, while advanced AI technology is necessary, there may be a lack of research and development resources to develop it. Additionally, without a systematic education system for talent development, there may be challenges in securing specialized personnel.

Current Checkpoints

  • Trends in humanoid robot development: Attention should be paid to the latest technologies and market trends.
  • Movements in international standardization: Information should be gathered on what standards Korea can seize internationally.
  • Convergence of AI and robotics industries: It is essential to examine how related technologies are developing and how companies are utilizing them.

FAQ

Q1: What is Physical AI?
A1: Physical AI refers to robotic systems where artificial intelligence operates in actual physical environments.

Q2: What direction should Korea's humanoid industry take?
A2: Korea should secure competitiveness through self-sufficiency in core components and international standardization, and formulate policies for training specialized talent.

Q3: What impact do humanoid robots have on jobs?
A3: The introduction of humanoid robots may replace some jobs, but it can also provide opportunities for new job creation.

Q4: What strategies should Korean companies adopt?
A4: Korean companies should establish collaborative systems to strengthen competitiveness in the global market alongside technological development.

In conclusion, the Korean humanoid industry needs proactive strategies to seize current opportunities. Particularly, achieving self-sufficiency in core components and seizing international standards will be crucial for establishing genuine competitiveness.
