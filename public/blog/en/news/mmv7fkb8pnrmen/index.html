‘Chungju Man’ Raised by Jo Gil-hyung “Cancellation of Chungbuk Governor Nomination Application… National Power, Not Where I Belong”

Recently, the event where former Chungju Mayor Jo Gil-hyung canceled his nomination application for the Chungbuk governor under the People Power Party and hinted at his resignation has garnered significant attention. He expressed dissatisfaction with the party's nomination process, clearly stating that there is no reason for him to remain in the People Power Party any longer. This decision raises questions about the potential impact on the political landscape in the Chungbuk region.

Jo's resignation goes beyond a personal choice, revealing the internal conflicts within the People Power Party and the complex dynamics of regional politics. The lack of transparency in the party's nomination process and the emergence of new candidates have contributed to the sense of betrayal he feels, which could have considerable repercussions in future elections.

Jo Gil-hyung's decision to resign stems from feelings of alienation and disappointment he experienced within the People Power Party. He has been a loyal party member for the past 13 years, but after experiencing unfairness in the recent nomination process, he concluded that he could no longer be associated with the party. This also includes conflicts with former National Police Agency Commissioner Yoon Hee-keun, indicating that competition among different candidates is likely to intensify.
