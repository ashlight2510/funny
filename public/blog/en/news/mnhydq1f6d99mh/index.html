Kakao's Double Bookkeeping and Labor Hour Violations: Points We Should Pay Attention To

Recently, it has been revealed that Kakao forced employees to work beyond legal working hours and operated 'double bookkeeping' to conceal this. According to the Ministry of Employment and Labor's inspection results, Kakao had employees work beyond the legal overtime limit from September of last year to February of this year, affecting 31 employees. In this process, Kakao was found to have delayed or failed to pay wages by recording actual working hours in the following month. In response to this situation, Kakao stated that it would improve its management system to prevent recurrence, but the union claims that the resolution of the issue remains insufficient.

This incident raises serious questions not only about simple legal violations but also about corporate ethics and workers' rights. Given that Kakao has established itself as an important IT company in modern society, this incident deserves particular attention due to its significant impact on the overall labor environment.

Key Summary

  • Kakao has been found to have forced employees to work beyond legal working hours and operated double bookkeeping to conceal this.
  • The Ministry of Employment and Labor's inspection confirmed that 31 employees worked beyond the legal overtime limit.
  • The union questions the sincerity of the management's statements, citing discrepancies between what is said and the actual situation on the ground.
  • Kakao announced plans to improve its management system to prevent recurrence, but skepticism remains.

Why Is It Important?

This incident raises serious questions not only about Kakao but also about Korea's labor culture and corporate responsibility. Especially as the IT industry rapidly grows, the risk of neglecting workers' rights increases, and Kakao's case could serve as a wake-up call for other companies. Issues related to working hours and wages are significant social issues in their own right, which can ultimately affect consumers and investors.

Corporate Responsibility and Workers' Rights

In my view, Kakao must respect workers' rights to fulfill its responsibilities to customers and society. A healthy internal labor environment is essential to gain customer trust, which is directly linked to the sustainability of the business. In fact, Kakao's operation of double bookkeeping appears to be a side effect of pursuing short-term profits. This will ultimately harm the company's image and brand trust in the long run.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Another point to note in this incident is the apparent negligence of Kakao's management regarding the violation of legal working hours. This goes beyond mere management oversight and shows how much workers' voices are disregarded in corporate culture. In an environment where workers' rights are not respected, attracting and retaining talent can become difficult, negatively impacting the company's growth.

Points to Check Now

  • It is also crucial to monitor how the Ministry of Labor reviews Kakao's improvement plans and what follow-up actions will be taken.
