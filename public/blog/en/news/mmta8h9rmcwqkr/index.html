President Lee Jae-myung warns hardliners on prosecution reform: "The competition for clarity gives grounds for counterattacks"

President Lee Jae-myung has cautioned against the excessive competition for clarity regarding prosecution reform among the Democratic Party's hardliners, emphasizing that this should not provide grounds for counterattacks from vested interests. He reaffirmed that the core of prosecution reform is the separation of investigation and prosecution, expressing his commitment to preventing the prosecution from interfering in investigations. He also pledged a responsible stance from the ruling party and a commitment to resolving livelihood issues.

This statement clarifies that prosecution reform should aim for substantial results rather than mere changes in titles or excessive measures, highlighting the need to reconcile various opinions within the Democratic Party. President Lee mentioned the necessity of supplementary investigative powers for the prosecution and expressed his determination to protect crime victims through cooperation with the police.

Why Background Matters

This issue has arisen as the Democratic Party prioritizes prosecution reform, with the voices of hardliners within the party growing louder. The discussions on prosecution reform, which began in 2019, have faced several conflicts, and President Lee appears to believe that a speed adjustment is necessary to reconcile these conflicts and maintain government stability. In particular, the reduction of the prosecution's investigative powers and the separation of investigation and prosecution are recognized as important measures to prevent abuse of power and balance the judicial system.

Why It Matters

President Lee's emphasis on 'substantial results' suggests that reforms must be tangible for the public. This could directly impact the credibility of future government policies and significantly affect the Democratic Party's approval ratings.
