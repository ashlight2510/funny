Introduction of 3-5 Member Major Election Districts for Basic Councilors in Integrated Special Cities: Key Points to Watch Now

March 16, 2026 · 경향신문 · View source

Social Reform Committee Proposes "Introduction of 3-5 Member Constituencies for Basic Councilors in Integrated Special City"

Recently, the Social Reform Committee under the Prime Minister proposed reforms to the local election system, including the legalization of constituencies for basic councilors with 3-5 members. This proposal emphasizes that political structure reform is essential for administrative integration to serve as a foundation for regional development and local autonomy. In particular, it shows a commitment to securing representation and checks and balances in the council of the integrated special city by introducing a large constituency system.

This proposal is being carried out in conjunction with the passage of a special law regarding the integrated special city of Jeonnam and Gwangju, which is expected to have a practical impact in the upcoming June local elections. Such changes signify not just a simple adjustment of electoral districts but a structural change in local politics, which is likely to have significant effects on future political dynamics.

Key Points

  • Introduction of Large Constituency System: Strengthening the representation and checks and balances of the council through the legalization of constituencies for basic councilors with 3-5 members.
  • Need for Political Structure Reform: Recognition that political structure reform is essential alongside administrative integration.
  • Current Status of Bill Proposal: A bill revising the Public Election Act, including the large constituency system, has been proposed in the National Assembly.
  • Integration of Jeonnam and Gwangju: The integrated special city of Jeonnam and Gwangju is scheduled to launch on July 1.
  • Changes in Local Elections: The possibility of applying the new electoral district system in the June local elections.

Background and Context

This proposal is closely related to recent discussions on administrative integration. As Jeonnam and Gwangju integrate to launch as a special city, there are growing concerns that the existing political structure will not function properly. In particular, with expectations that the authority of local leaders will increase, there have been calls for political mechanisms to check this power. Therefore, the core of this proposal is to change the electoral districts for basic councilors to a large constituency system to create a framework that reflects the diverse opinions of local residents.

Why This Issue is Important

The significance of this proposal lies in its aim for structural change to enhance the democracy and representation of local politics, rather than merely adjusting electoral districts. If this system is successfully implemented, local residents will have more opportunities to voice their opinions, potentially leading to revitalization of local politics. Conversely, if resistance from existing political forces or legal issues arise during this process, there are concerns about political chaos.

Comments from Article Reader

In my view, the most noteworthy aspect of this proposal is the effort to enhance transparency and representation in local politics. However, it remains questionable how effectively these changes will reflect the voices of residents. While the introduction of the large constituency system will naturally allow more candidates to run, it also carries the risk of strengthening the influence of specific parties or factions. Therefore, continuous monitoring and evaluation will be necessary to determine how well these institutional changes reflect public sentiment.

Easily Overlooked Aspects

One aspect not covered in the article is the potential side effects expected if this system is implemented. For instance, with the introduction of the large constituency system, there is a possibility that specific parties or factions could disproportionately occupy seats. Additionally, if other reform measures, such as the expansion of proportional representation, are not properly implemented, there may be limitations to the overall improvement of the political structure.

What to Check Now

  • Passage of the Bill: Whether the bill related to the large constituency system will pass in the National Assembly.
  • Political Reactions: Political responses and changes in public opinion regarding the launch of the integrated special city.
  • Preparation for Local Elections: Whether the new electoral district system will be applied in the June local elections.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What changes will occur if the large constituency system is introduced?
A: With the electoral districts for basic councilors set to 3-5 members, more candidates will be able to run, increasing the likelihood that diverse opinions of residents will be reflected.

Q: What impact will the implementation of this system have on politics?
A: It could enhance the representation and democracy of local politics, but there is also a risk of excessive influence from specific parties or factions.

Q: What should we pay close attention to moving forward?
A: We should closely observe the passage of the bill and the actual application of the new system in the local elections.
