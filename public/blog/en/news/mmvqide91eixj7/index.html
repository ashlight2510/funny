‘Too Expensive’ Criticism Leads LH to Change Purchase Price Standards for Rental Housing

The Korea Land and Housing Corporation (LH) has decided to unify the price assessment method for purchasing rental housing to an appraisal-based system and introduce quantitative evaluations in the decision-making process. This measure aims to better reflect surrounding market prices when LH purchases newly built housing, addressing past issues related to the construction cost linkage system. LH targets the purchase of 38,000 rental housing units this year, planning to promote a more rational and transparent housing supply.

This change is expected to have an impact not only on LH's internal policies but also on the overall housing market. As the prices LH pays are adjusted to align with local market rates, it could send positive signals to the private housing market. However, potential issues that may arise alongside this change cannot be overlooked.

Key Summary

  • LH unifies the price assessment method for rental housing purchases to an appraisal-based system and introduces quantitative evaluations.
  • The purchase price for newly built housing will be determined based on local market prices.
  • An online system will be introduced to enhance transparency in the purchase review process, along with a total quantity system for review periods.
  • The target purchase volume for this year is 38,224 units, with approximately 81% located in the metropolitan area.

Key Highlights

  • Unification of the price assessment method: standardized to appraisals.
  • Market prices reflected in the purchase of newly built housing.
  • Establishment of a transparent review process through the introduction of quantitative evaluations.
  • A system to reduce waiting times for sellers.
  • The target purchase volume for this year is 38,224 units.

Reasons to Consider the Background

LH has historically based its purchase prices for newly built housing on construction costs. However, this approach has led to purchases being made at prices higher than the surrounding market rates, resulting in criticism of LH. Therefore, LH has decided to reflect market prices to purchase housing at more reasonable prices. This institutional change can be seen as part of LH's efforts to activate housing supply and reduce criticism regarding high prices.

Why It Matters

This change provides an opportunity for LH's purchasing policy to not only alter the price assessment method but also to restore trust in the overall housing market. Particularly, changes in purchasing standards in the metropolitan area, where housing demand is high, could positively influence the private market. Additionally, a transparent review process could build trust with sellers.

Points of Interest

The actual impact of LH's changes in purchasing standards on private housing suppliers is crucial. If prices decrease, private suppliers may lose motivation to supply housing. Conversely, if purchases reflect market prices, it could stimulate positive competition in the private market. Therefore, the impact of LH's changes in price assessment methods on the private market will need careful monitoring.

Hidden Variables to Watch

As the number of houses purchased by LH increases, there is a possibility of oversupply or price declines in the market. Additionally, how efficiently the new review process operates will be an important variable. Even if the review period is shortened, unclear or inefficient review criteria could still lead to problems.

Current Points to Check

It is important to closely observe the actual impact of LH's changes in purchasing standards for newly built housing on the private market. In particular, if purchase prices are adjusted to align with local market rates, monitoring the reactions of private suppliers and changes in the housing market will be essential.

FAQ

Q: How will LH's changes in purchasing standards affect the housing market?
A: If LH adjusts its purchase prices to align with market prices, it could encourage positive competition among private suppliers. However, there are also concerns about oversupply or price declines.

Q: What are the specifics of the introduction of quantitative evaluations?
A: Quantitative evaluations will assess rental demand, transportation and convenience, and financial impacts to determine purchase eligibility. This aims to establish a more transparent review process.

Q: What advantages are there if the waiting time for sellers is reduced?
A: Sellers will be able to receive results more quickly, reducing uncertainty regarding the sale of their homes. This can alleviate the psychological burden on sellers.
