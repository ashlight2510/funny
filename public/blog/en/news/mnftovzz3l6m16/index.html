‘Nakdong River Murder Case’ wrongful conviction victim sues 5 police officers for perjury during retrial

This case sheds light on the long-forgotten issues of torture and human rights violations in Korean society. The victims of the ‘Nakdong River Murder Case’ have proven their innocence through a retrial after 21 years of wrongful imprisonment, and they have now sued the police officers for perjury. This case goes beyond mere legal issues, demanding deep reflection on the judicial system and human rights in Korean society.

Victims Choi In-cheol and Jang Dong-ik were coerced into false confessions due to police torture, resulting in life sentences. They have recently filed a lawsuit claiming that the police officers denied the torture during the retrial and committed perjury. This case holds significant meaning as it transcends simple legal procedures, becoming a matter of human rights and justice.

Why Background Matters

The ‘Nakdong River Murder Case’ occurred in 1990 when two victims were identified as murder suspects by the police and sentenced to life imprisonment. During their 21 years of wrongful imprisonment, the acknowledgment of torture by the past cases committee of the Supreme Prosecutors' Office led to a retrial. After receiving a not guilty verdict, the victims took legal action against the police officers who denied the torture. This trajectory has brought renewed attention to issues of torture and human rights violations in Korean society.

Why It Matters
