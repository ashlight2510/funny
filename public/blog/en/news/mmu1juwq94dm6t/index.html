KFTC files complaint against HDC Chairman Chung Mong-kyu... 20 affiliates controlled by brother and uncle omitted

Recently, the Korea Fair Trade Commission (KFTC) reported HDC Chairman Chung Mong-kyu to the prosecution. The reason is the allegation of omitting 20 affiliates controlled by his brother and uncle for 19 years. The KFTC determined that HDC intentionally excluded these affiliates from the submitted data and pointed out that Chairman Chung concealed this fact despite having a close relationship with them. Such actions are seen as a disregard for legal obligations, which is likely to significantly impact HDC's management transparency in the future.

This incident is a serious matter that could undermine social trust in corporate governance and fair trade. The omitted affiliates possess assets exceeding 1 trillion won annually, raising serious potential legal and financial issues.

Key Summary

  • KFTC has reported Chairman Chung Mong-kyu of HDC for omitting 20 affiliates controlled by his brother and uncle for 19 years.
  • The omitted affiliates hold assets totaling over 1 trillion won and are deemed to have violated legal obligations.
  • Allegations have arisen that Chairman Chung was aware of the omissions but concealed them.

Why Background Matters

This incident connects to suspicions that the HDC Group has not practiced transparent management in the past. Chairman Chung Mong-kyu has been designated as the individual since 2006, yet he did not clarify the governance structure of family-owned companies. Notably, after KCC Chairman Chung Mong-jin was reported for similar allegations in 2021, there were indications that HDC was more careful during the data submission process. However, the failure to take action regarding the omitted affiliates has been pointed out as a significant issue.
