Despite Fuel Tax Cuts, Gas Prices Likely to Remain in the 2000 Won Range

Recently, the government has significantly reduced fuel taxes and designated a 'second highest oil price,' but due to the surge in crude oil prices stemming from the Middle East situation, consumers are still expected to fill up gasoline and diesel at prices exceeding 2000 won per liter. The government anticipates that gasoline prices will decrease by about 200 won, and diesel and kerosene by about 500 won compared to before the implementation of the maximum price system, but the actual selling prices at gas stations are likely to be higher than this.

This fuel tax cut is intended to provide direct benefits to consumers, but the effect is expected to be limited due to rising international oil prices. The government has particularly expanded the diesel fuel tax cut rate more than that of gasoline, considering truck drivers, farmers, and vulnerable groups. However, the actual prices at gas stations are expected to be, on average, more than 100 won higher than the supply price, which will impose an additional burden on consumers.

Key Points

  • Despite the government's fuel tax cut, gasoline and diesel prices are expected to exceed 2000 won per liter.
  • The second highest price designation sets gasoline at 1934 won and diesel at 1923 won.
  • The diesel fuel tax cut rate has been expanded to 25%, higher than that of gasoline.
  • The government plans to respond strictly to any price increases by refiners and gas stations.
  • If consumers rush to purchase due to concerns about price increases, prices may rise sooner than expected.

Why Background Context Matters

This issue is closely related to the surge in crude oil prices due to the Middle East situation. The government has reduced fuel taxes to reflect rising international oil prices, but consumer prices are still on the rise. In particular, the difference between the prices supplied by refiners and the prices at gas stations directly impacts consumers. Such policy decisions can be seen as the government's efforts to reduce economic burdens, but the actual effectiveness remains questionable.

Why It Matters

Fuel tax cuts are an important policy that can provide direct economic benefits to consumers. However, when the effects are limited due to surging international oil prices, consumers will still have to fill up at high prices, which can burden household economies. Additionally, discussions on how the government's price controls will actually function in the market are necessary.

Consumer Perspective

General consumers and practitioners should closely monitor what actual impacts the fuel tax cut will have. Particularly, since prices at gas stations are likely to be higher than the supply price, it is important to check prices in advance before going to the gas station. Moreover, specific groups such as truck drivers and farmers who use diesel may feel the benefits of the fuel tax cut more significantly.

Points of Interest

In my view, while the government's fuel tax cut is a positive measure, there are concerns that its effectiveness will be limited considering the volatility of international oil prices. Especially with expected price increases at gas stations, consumers need to be sensitive to price fluctuations. It is also questionable how effectively the government's zero-tolerance principle against market disruption will be implemented.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The fuel tax cut and the highest price designation discussed in this article may appear to be positive signals for consumers, but there is a high possibility that the actual selling prices at gas stations will not reflect this. Additionally, the potential for price collusion between refiners and gas stations is also a factor that cannot be overlooked. How consumers react to anticipated price increases will also be an important variable.

Points to Check Now

  • Check the Korea National Oil Corporation's oil price information site to verify the actual selling prices and supply price differences at gas stations.
  • It is advisable to gather information in advance about when prices at gas stations are expected to rise (27th or 28th).
  • If using diesel, consider ways to maximize the benefits of the fuel tax cut.

A: While the fuel tax cut directly impacts gas station prices, consumer prices may still remain high due to the difference between supply prices and selling prices.
