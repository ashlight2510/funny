‘Manager Kim’ earns three times more than the CEO... Employees claim the title of ‘salary king’ at securities firms with hefty bonuses

Recently, there have been numerous cases in domestic securities firms where employees earn significantly higher salaries than their CEOs. In particular, employees managing customer assets in the retail sector have greatly increased their salaries through bonuses, with Noh Hye-ran, the branch manager of Samsung Securities, reportedly earning more than the CEO last year. This phenomenon is attributed to the booming stock market, which has led to a substantial increase in bonuses, and it is expected that this trend will continue in the future.

This issue goes beyond just salary concerns; it may also prompt a change in corporate compensation systems and perceptions of employee performance. In the financial industry, such as securities firms, bonuses are directly linked to employee motivation, making it essential to pay attention to the ripple effects this change may bring.

Key Summary

  • The number of cases where employees at domestic securities firms earned higher salaries than their CEOs increased last year.
  • Noh Hye-ran of Samsung Securities received 1.817 billion won, surpassing the CEO's salary.
  • The increase in bonuses is a major factor, with experts in customer asset management receiving high compensation.
  • The booming stock market is expected to continue this year, suggesting that high salaries for employees will persist.

Key Scenes

  • Proportion of Bonuses: The share of bonuses in employee compensation has increased.
  • Strength in Retail Sector: Employees managing customer assets in the retail sector are primarily benefiting.
  • Widening Salary Gap with CEOs: Some department heads are earning 2-3 times more than their CEOs.
  • Continued Stock Market Boom: Continuous stock market growth is anticipated this year, leading to expected increases in compensation.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This phenomenon began with the KOSPI soaring by 76% last year, indicating a booming stock market. This market situation improved the performance of retail sector employees managing customer assets, leading to higher bonuses. In particular, experts providing solutions tailored to clients' financial needs are gaining attention, which explains their high compensation.

Why It Matters

The phenomenon of employees earning more than their CEOs calls for a reevaluation of corporate compensation systems. This is not just an issue of individual salaries but can significantly impact corporate culture and talent management strategies. If this change continues, it is essential to consider how the relationship between corporate executives and employees may be redefined.

Personal Perspective

In my view, this phenomenon is not merely about increased bonuses; it serves as an important indicator of how corporate values and goals are evolving. Particularly, the establishment of a customer-oriented service culture that emphasizes performance can be seen as a positive change. However, whether this change is sustainable remains in question.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect that may be overlooked in this issue is that bonuses could end up being one-time income. If employees are receiving high bonuses based on short-term performance, it is crucial to achieve sustainable performance in the long run. Additionally, this compensation structure may foster competition among employees or harm teamwork.

Current Points to Check

It is important to closely monitor the current stock market situation and the salary and bonus structures of each securities firm. In particular, predictions about how this year's stock market trends will change and how these changes will impact employee compensation are necessary.
