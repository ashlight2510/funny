Introduction of Large Electoral Districts for Basic Councilors in Administrative Integration

Recently, the Social Reform Committee under the Prime Minister proposed reforms to the local election system, including the legalization of electoral districts for basic councilors with 3 to 5 members in integrated administrative regions. They emphasized that political structure reform should be the foundation for regional development and local autonomy, stating that the introduction of large electoral districts is essential for the integrated special city council to effectively represent public opinion. Currently, a related amendment to the Public Official Election Act has been submitted to the National Assembly.

This proposal is in preparation for the launch of super-regional local governments, which may strengthen the authority of local leaders, focusing on securing the representativeness and checks of regional councils. In addition to the legalization of electoral districts for basic councilors, the committee presented various reform tasks, including the expansion of proportional representation and the introduction of a runoff voting system.

Key Summary

  • Proposal for the legalization of electoral districts for basic councilors with 3-5 members in regional councils of administrative integration areas
  • Emphasis on the necessity of introducing large electoral districts
  • Related amendment submitted to the National Assembly
  • Connection between political structure reform and regional development
  • Concerns over the strengthening of authority with the launch of super-regional local governments

Key Scenes

  • Need for political structure reform: The argument is made that political structure reform is essential for administrative integration to contribute to regional development.
  • Introduction of large electoral districts: A need for a council structure that can check the strengthened authority of local leaders and represent public opinion.
  • Expansion of proportional representation: Increasing the proportion of proportional representatives in local councils to 30% to gather diverse opinions.
  • Introduction of a runoff voting system: Proposed as a measure to enhance the fairness of elections.
  • Push for legalization: Related bills have been submitted to the National Assembly, expecting substantial changes.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This issue has emerged as administrative integration between regions accelerates. In particular, the mayor of the integrated Gwangju Metropolitan City is scheduled to be elected in June, necessitating a reform of the political structure. The launch of super-regional local governments could significantly increase the authority of local leaders, leading to a growing recognition of the need for checks and balances.

Why It Matters

This proposal holds significant meaning for enhancing the democracy of local politics. If large electoral districts are introduced, a wider range of voices can be reflected in the council, providing better political representation for local residents. Additionally, the expansion of proportional representation is expected to contribute to ensuring political diversity.

Personal Perspective

In my view, the introduction of large electoral districts is not merely a change in the legal structure but could serve as an opportunity to enhance political participation among residents. In particular, expanding proportional representation in local elections is essential for gathering diverse opinions. However, for these changes to be realized, the willingness of politicians and the active participation of local residents are necessary.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One overlooked point in this article is the possibility that existing politicians may still maintain their power even with the introduction of large electoral districts. In other words, even with a new structure, substantial changes may be minimal in situations where political interests are intertwined. Therefore, additional measures for political transparency and fairness will be necessary.

Current Points to Check

  • Progress of the bill related to the introduction of large electoral districts: It is important to closely monitor the discussions in the National Assembly.
  • Expansion of proportional representation in local elections: It is necessary to check how discussions related to this are progressing.

FAQ

  • Q: What changes will occur if large electoral districts are introduced? A: A wider range of political opinions can be reflected in the council, and the voices of local residents will be better represented.

  • Q: Why is the expansion of proportional representation necessary? A: It is necessary to increase the proportion of proportional representatives to ensure political diversity and gather various opinions.

  • Q: How will political structure reform impact regional development? A: If the political structure is improved, resident participation will increase, positively impacting regional development.

Finally, the key point to pay attention to right now is the progress of the bill in the National Assembly. It is important to observe whether these changes can actually be realized and what interests may come into play during the process.
