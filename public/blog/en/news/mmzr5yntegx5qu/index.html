US Allows Limited Sale of Iranian Oil: A New Turning Point in Curbing International Oil Prices

Recently, the US has decided to allow limited sales of Iranian oil for a month to curb the surge in international oil prices due to the Iran war. Scott Vessenet, the US Treasury Secretary, announced that the sale of Iranian oil, which is currently stranded at sea, will be temporarily permitted. However, Iran has countered this US measure, claiming that there is no additional oil available. This decision illustrates how the US's strategic response unfolds amid rising global oil prices.

The allowance for the sale of Iranian oil is a short-term measure, clearly indicating that the US's sanctions policy and plans to block Iran's access to the international financial system will still be maintained. This not only impacts international oil prices but also highlights the complex relationship between Iran and the US.

Key Summary

  • The US allows limited sales of Iranian oil for a month to curb the surge in international oil prices due to the Iran war.
  • The sale of Iranian oil is only possible for quantities stranded at sea, and new purchases or production are prohibited.
  • The US emphasizes that it continues to block Iran's access to the international financial system, making it difficult for Iran to secure additional revenue.
  • Iran counters the US announcement by claiming there is no additional oil available and argues that the US statement is meant to give hope to buyers.

Key Scenes

  • Limited Oil Sales: The sale of Iranian oil is permitted in the short term, but is limited to quantities stranded at sea.
  • Goal of Price Stabilization: The US aims to supply about 140 million barrels of oil to the market through Iranian oil to stabilize prices.
  • Continuation of US Sanctions: The blockage of Iran's access to the international financial system will continue, making it difficult for Iran to secure additional revenue.
  • Strategic Response: This measure is interpreted as a strategic response from the US to curb the rise in global oil prices.

Why Background Matters

This issue arises amid escalating conflicts with Iran, leading to a surge in international oil prices. The US has already eased sanctions on Russian oil, and the easing of sanctions on Iranian oil can be viewed as an effort to stabilize the global energy market. With the Iran war continuing into its third week, oil prices have surpassed $112 per barrel, significantly impacting the economy.

Why It Matters

This measure signifies more than just stabilizing oil prices. It requires consideration of the complex relationship with Iran, political tensions in the Middle East, and trends in the international energy market. With the limited allowance for Iranian oil sales, attention should be paid to how international oil prices will change and how diplomatic tensions between Iran and the US will evolve.

In my view, Iran's backlash could undermine trust in the US strategy, and the diplomatic relationship with Iran may become even more complicated.

Additionally, the volatility of international oil prices remains, so it is essential to observe whether this short-term measure leads to long-term stability.
