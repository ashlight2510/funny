Stalking Murderer in Namyangju Claims "I Don't Remember" Despite Pre-Visit

The recent stalking murder case in Namyangju has sent shockwaves through the community. A man in his 40s attacked and killed a woman in her 20s with a weapon, and during the investigation at a medical facility after the crime, he has been claiming "I don't remember." Evidence has surfaced that he installed a tracking device on the victim's vehicle and selected the crime scene through a pre-visit. This situation has raised questions about the police response.

This incident transcends a simple crime, raising serious doubts about the broader societal issues of stalking and the police response system. The fact that the perpetrator, who had multiple reports of domestic violence and stalking in the past, was not properly managed highlights the weaknesses in our societal safety net.

Key Summary

  • A man in his 40s murdered a woman in her 20s with a weapon.
  • Evidence shows he installed a tracking device on the victim's vehicle and conducted a pre-visit before the crime.
  • The perpetrator claims he does not remember the crime, avoiding key statements.
  • The police are currently conducting an internal review of their response before and after the incident.
  • The victim was provided with a smartwatch, but it was not linked to the perpetrator's electronic monitoring device, resulting in inadequate protective measures.

Key Scenes

This incident occurred amidst growing concerns about stalking and domestic violence as serious social issues. This situation reveals the shortcomings of the police response system and the social safety net.
