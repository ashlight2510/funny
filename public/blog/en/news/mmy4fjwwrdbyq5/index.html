Air Conditioner Refrigerant Check: How and When to Do It Yourself

March 20, 2026

Air Conditioner Refrigerant Check: How to Do It Yourself and Frequency

To optimize the performance of your air conditioner, checking the refrigerant is essential. Refrigerant is a key element that absorbs and releases heat in the cooling cycle of the air conditioner. If it is insufficient, the efficiency of the air conditioner can decrease, leading to higher electricity bills. In this article, we will explore the methods and frequency of refrigerant checks for air conditioners in detail, providing a checklist for you to conduct the checks yourself. This will help maintain the performance of your air conditioner and reduce unnecessary costs.

The Role and Importance of Refrigerant

Basic Functions of Refrigerant

Refrigerant acts as a medium for transferring heat in the air conditioner. It operates on the principle of changing from gas to liquid and back to gas while absorbing and releasing heat. This process must occur smoothly for the air conditioner to function effectively.

The Need for Refrigerant Checks

Regular refrigerant checks help maintain the performance of the air conditioner and reduce electricity bills. A shortage of refrigerant can lead to decreased performance and an increased risk of equipment damage.

Problems Caused by Insufficient Refrigerant

When refrigerant is low, the following issues may arise:

  • Decreased cooling performance: No cool air is produced
  • Overheating: Increased risk of air conditioner failure

Checkpoint: If you feel the performance of your air conditioner has declined, consider checking the refrigerant immediately.

How to Check Refrigerant

Introduction to Basic Checking Tools

The tools needed for checking refrigerant are as follows:

  • Pressure gauge
  • Thermometer

You can use these tools to check the state of the refrigerant.

Checking Refrigerant Levels

Use the pressure gauge to check the refrigerant levels. Generally, the appropriate pressure range is 60-80 psi. If it falls outside this range, the refrigerant may be low or excessive.

Execution Tip: The appropriate pressure range may vary depending on the model of the air conditioner, so refer to the user manual.

Checking for Refrigerant Leaks

Refrigerant leaks are a major cause of performance degradation. Signs of a leak include poor operation of the air conditioner or a hissing sound from leaking refrigerant. You can confirm leaks using a soap bubble test, which involves spraying soapy water on the suspected leak area to see if bubbles form.

Checkpoint: If a leak is detected, contact a professional immediately for repairs.

The Need for Periodic Checks

It is advisable to perform refrigerant checks at least once a year. Checking before the summer usage is particularly effective.

Execution Tip: Consider setting a schedule to conduct checks every April.

How to Recharge or Replace Refrigerant

Procedure for Recharging Refrigerant

The tools needed to recharge refrigerant are as follows:

  • Refrigerant tank
  • Pressure gauge
  • Connecting hose

The recharging procedure is as follows:

  1. Turn off the air conditioner and disconnect the power.
  2. Connect the refrigerant tank to the pressure gauge.
  3. Slowly recharge the refrigerant while monitoring the pressure gauge.
  4. Stop recharging once the pressure reaches the appropriate range.

When to Consult a Professional

If refrigerant recharging is necessary, it is best to consult a professional. They can accurately determine the type of refrigerant and recharge it safely. The cost of recharging varies by region but typically ranges from 50,000 to 100,000 won.

Points to Note

When recharging refrigerant, wear safety equipment and comply with environmental regulations. Refrigerants can be substances that deplete the ozone layer, so proper disposal methods must be followed.

Refrigerant Check Checklist

Items to Prepare Before Checking Refrigerant

The items you need to prepare for checking refrigerant are as follows:

  • Pressure gauge
  • Thermometer
  • Soapy water (for leak detection)
  • Refrigerant tank

Step-by-Step Checklist for Checking and Recharging

  1. Turn off the air conditioner
  2. Connect the pressure gauge
  3. Check the refrigerant level
  4. Check for leaks
  5. Recharge refrigerant if necessary

Setting a Regular Check Frequency

It is important to set a regular check frequency that fits your schedule. For example, it is recommended to conduct checks every April.

Real-Life Examples of Refrigerant Checks

Example 1: Breakdown Due to Low Refrigerant

One customer sought repair services when their air conditioner did not work during the summer. Upon inspection, it was found that the refrigerant was low, and after recharging, the air conditioner operated normally. This case highlights the importance of refrigerant checks.

Example 2: Prevention Through Regular Checks

Another customer has been conducting regular refrigerant checks every year. During this check, the refrigerant level was maintained within the appropriate range, and they received feedback that the air conditioner was performing excellently. Regular checks played a significant role in improving performance.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  1. How often should refrigerant checks be performed?

    • It is generally recommended to check at least once a year, especially before summer usage.

  2. What symptoms indicate low refrigerant?

    • Symptoms may include decreased cooling performance, the air conditioner not operating, or overheating.

  3. What should be noted when recharging refrigerant?

    • Safety equipment should be worn, and environmental regulations must be followed. Consulting a professional is safer.

Checking the refrigerant in your air conditioner plays a crucial role in maintaining performance and reducing electricity bills. Plan your refrigerant check now. Use the checklist to prepare your items and maintain your air conditioner in optimal condition through regular checks. As a first step, gather the necessary tools and set a check schedule!
