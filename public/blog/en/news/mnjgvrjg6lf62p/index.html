Controversy Over Health Insurance Coverage for Hair Loss Treatment: Reasons We Can't Move Forward

The issue of health insurance coverage for hair loss treatment has seen the government repeatedly deny claims of ‘imminent introduction’ whenever such claims arise from the political realm. Currently, hair loss is classified as a non-covered item, and in order to include it in health insurance, there exists a complex legal and medical dilemma that requires redefinition as ‘essential functional improvement.’ If hair loss is deemed a cosmetic issue, equity concerns with other cosmetic procedures may also arise.

This controversy goes beyond a simple health insurance issue; it highlights the reality faced by patients suffering from psychological distress and social disadvantages due to hair loss. Nevertheless, the government maintains a fundamental stance of ‘under review,’ resulting in a lack of progress in discussions.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The discussion about including hair loss treatment in health insurance is not new. Following President Lee Jae-myung's remarks last year, discussions about hair loss coverage have become active in the political sphere, yet the government still fails to present a clear stance. This is because hair loss is often viewed as a mere cosmetic issue, while many individuals suffer from psychological distress. Particularly, the prevalence of hair loss among those in their 20s and 30s indicates that this issue extends beyond personal suffering to become a societal concern.

Why It Matters

The question of whether hair loss treatment should be covered by health insurance is not merely a policy issue. It directly impacts social inequality and the quality of individual lives. The psychological distress caused by hair loss affects employment and interpersonal relationships, and there is a growing call for policies to address this issue. However, simultaneously, concerns about financial burdens could undermine trust in national healthcare policies.

Hidden Variables to Consider

If hair loss treatment is covered, equity issues with other cosmetic procedures must also be considered. For instance, there are concerns that other non-covered items, such as wrinkle or pigmentation treatments, could be covered under the same criteria. This could ultimately increase the financial burden on the government.
