Analysis of the Causes of Low Voter Turnout Among 2030s by Former Military Rights Center Secretary General Kim Hyung-nam

Recently, despite being eliminated from the preliminary election for the Seoul mayoral candidacy, former Military Rights Center Secretary General Kim Hyung-nam provided insights into the low voter turnout among the 2030 generation. He pointed out that this low turnout is not simply due to indifference, but rather because established politics fails to adequately reflect the voices of this generation. According to him, policies that feel "not like my story" hinder political participation among the 2030s.

Kim emphasized the need to create a new political flow and argued that policies that resonate with the younger generation must be developed. He particularly suggested innovative policies such as the cohabitation partner system and public rental housing, stressing that these policies could have a tangible impact on the lives of young people.

Reasons to Consider the Background

In recent years, the interest of Korea's younger generation in politics has decreased, leading to a decline in voter turnout. The phenomenon of lower voter turnout among the 2030 generation compared to the overall turnout in the 2022 Seoul mayoral election illustrates this trend well. Former Secretary General Kim argues that to address this issue, established politics must listen to the voices of the younger generation. He analyzes that the appeal of political extremism reflects the anxieties of the youth.

Why It Matters

This issue is not merely about declining voter turnout; it can act as a threat to political participation and the health of democracy. If the younger generation becomes indifferent to politics, it could significantly influence the direction of future politics. Kim's argument serves as a wake-up call for established politicians, emphasizing the need to seek new political alternatives.

Points of Interest

Policies like the cohabitation partner law proposed by Kim Hyung-nam reflect the practical needs of modern society, where single-person households are increasing. If such policies are actually implemented, they have the potential to bring about positive changes in the lives of the younger generation. However, whether established politics can accept and implement these ideas remains another question.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The "appeal of extremism" mentioned by Kim does not merely pertain to political phenomena but reflects broader societal anxieties and discontent. Overlooking this aspect could lead to failures in formulating policies that encourage political participation among the younger generation. Additionally, for policies to be effectively implemented, political will and social consensus are necessary.

Current Points to Check

It is important to observe whether Kim Hyung-nam's proposals can be reflected in political discussions and whether this can lead to an improvement in the voter turnout of the younger generation. Continuous monitoring of how established politics reflects the voices of the younger generation is also needed.
