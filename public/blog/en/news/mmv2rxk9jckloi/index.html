Germany and Spain, Judicial Justice Changed by Constitutional Complaints… “Whom Does Court Formalism Exist For?”

Recently, it has been reported that the constitutional complaint system in Germany and Spain has had a significant impact on judicial justice. The cases from these two countries illustrate how formalism in courts can infringe on individual rights and pose profound questions about the reason for the existence of courts. In particular, discussions on how the constitutional courts of each country are overcoming this formalism provide important implications for the future of the judicial system.

This article explains how the formal procedures of courts can infringe on individuals' fundamental rights through case law from Germany and Spain. In Germany, a case where a defendant lost due to their absence was overturned by the constitutional court, while in Spain, a case where the court imposed a harsher sentence than the prosecution's request led to a constitutional complaint. These incidents emphasize that courts must go beyond mere formal procedures to realize substantive justice.

Key Points

  • Importance of Constitutional Complaints: In Germany and Spain, constitutional complaints serve as an important means of protecting individuals' fundamental rights.
  • Problems of Formalism: It clearly states that when courts focus on formal procedures, the rights of defendants can be infringed.
  • Role of Constitutional Courts: Constitutional courts play a crucial role in overcoming formalism and implementing substantive justice.
  • Specific Case Examples: The German case of absence and the Spanish case of excessive punishment illustrate the need for improvements in the judicial system.
  • Differences in Legal Approaches Between Countries: Although the approaches of the two countries differ, they all emphasize the necessity of legal protection.

Why Background Matters

This issue arises from the awareness that reliance solely on procedural formalism can infringe on individuals' fundamental rights. In Germany, a ruling from 1976 became the starting point, while in Spain, the constitutional court emphasized procedural legitimacy, creating new precedents. This trend reflects the need for judicial systems in each country to evolve in response to the demands of the times.

Why It Matters

This news goes beyond mere legal precedents and addresses fundamental issues within the judicial system. When courts fall into formalism, the real impact on someone's life can be substantial. Particularly, judgments in criminal trials directly affect individual freedom and rights, making it urgent to recognize and improve these issues.

Personal Comment

In my view, the reason these cases are gaining attention is that they remind us that courts are not merely mechanical entities applying the law. Courts, as institutions that must realize social justice, should focus on protecting substantive rights without being bound by formal procedures. Especially when such cases arise, courts should reflect on themselves and contemplate their purpose for existence.

Hidden Variables to Consider

An aspect not emphasized in the article is that the constitutional complaint system cannot be uniformly applied across all countries. The effectiveness of constitutional complaints may vary depending on each country's legal culture and social context, and misunderstanding this could lead to confusion.

Points to Check Now

  • Development of the Judicial System: It is necessary to examine whether our country's judicial system sufficiently protects individual rights.

FAQ

Q: What is a constitutional complaint?
A: A constitutional complaint is a procedure in which individuals petition the constitutional court when they believe their fundamental rights have been infringed by the court.

Q: How do the cases in Germany and Spain differ?
A: Germany emphasizes the necessity of constitutional complaints through case law on procedural rights infringements, while Spain strengthens constitutional standards by questioning the legitimacy of punishments.

Q: Why is court formalism problematic?
A: Formalism creates a risk that courts rely solely on procedural requirements, which can infringe on the fundamental rights of defendants.

Through this article, I hope readers recognize that the realization of judicial justice is not merely a legal procedure but is crucial for protecting individual rights.
