The Madness Diplomacy of Trump, Where the 'Unthinkable' Has Disappeared

Recently, President Trump's shift in Middle Eastern policy has surprised many experts. In particular, the news of a large-scale military attack on Iran has broken down the boundaries of what was previously considered 'unthinkable.' The second term of Trump has entered a phase where traditional diplomacy and military strategies are ignored, and unpredictable actions are willingly taken, further escalating tensions in the international community. This is creating a situation that could seriously impact the diplomatic and economic structures worldwide, beyond just the conflict between the U.S. and Iran.

Trump's change has evolved from the past 'madman strategy' to a method of pressuring adversaries through unpredictable actions. As a result, U.S. foreign policy is moving in a more aggressive and uncertain direction, which could have significant repercussions for the global economy and security.

Key Summary

  • The foreign policy of Trump's second term is characterized by unpredictable actions, with the military attack on Iran as an example.
  • The administration, composed of loyalists, is immediately executing his extreme decisions, disregarding existing diplomatic and military strategies.
  • U.S. foreign policy is shifting towards comprehensive pressure that prioritizes interests over traditional relationships with allies.
  • The state of war in the Middle East poses increasing risks to the global economy, intensifying pressure on energy-importing countries, including South Korea.

Key Scenes

  • The foreign strategy of Trump's second term has transitioned to unpredictable actions.
  • Military tensions in the Middle East could negatively impact the global economy.
  • U.S. foreign policy is changing to be interest-centered, ignoring relationships with allies.
  • Military pressure on energy-importing countries, such as South Korea, is increasing.
  • Trump's decisions are no longer subject to internal controls.

Reasons to Consider the Background

During President Trump's first term, his foreign policy had a strong character of 'show' aimed at pressuring opposing countries. At that time, experienced bureaucrats like Chief of Staff John Kelly helped maintain balance by controlling his extreme decisions. However, in the second term, these controls have disappeared, and Trump's loyalists are executing his directives immediately. This background leads him to act beyond the previous boundaries of 'unthinkable.'

Why It Matters

These changes could lead to economic instability, not just diplomatic tensions. For example, if the war in the Middle East escalates, oil prices could skyrocket and maritime logistics could be paralyzed, directly affecting energy-importing countries like South Korea. Furthermore, there is a high possibility that U.S. foreign policy will undermine trust with allies and lead to divisions in the international community.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The changes brought about by Trump's foreign policy are not limited to the conflict between the U.S. and Iran. In-depth analysis is needed on how military tensions in the Middle East will affect various countries, including South Korea. Additionally, the impact of U.S. economic policy on the global economy may manifest not only in trade deficit adjustments but also in ways that shake global supply chains.

Current Points to Check

  • Attention should be paid to how the South Korean government will respond to Trump's pressures and the direction of changes in its diplomatic strategy.
  • Economic analysis is needed on the impact of war in the Middle East on South Korea's energy imports.
