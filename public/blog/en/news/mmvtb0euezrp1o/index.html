Park Chan-woon “Maintaining the Special Judicial Police Command Authority is a Delusion of Prosecutor Revival”

Recently, Park Chan-woon, advisory chairman of the Prosecutorial Reform Promotion Committee, expressed concerns regarding the command authority of SJPOs (Special Judicial Police Officers). He warned that if SJPOs do not receive guidance from prosecutors, there is a high possibility of illegal and inadequate investigations occurring, and he argued that the prosecutor's command authority must be included in the amendment to the Criminal Procedure Act. He also criticized the deletion of the prosecutor's command authority as a reckless decision made without alternatives, emphasizing the practical confusion and the number of victims it could bring.

This issue is not just a simple legal discussion; it raises significant debates about the relationship between prosecutors and SJPOs, as well as the reliability of law enforcement. Chairman Park's remarks illustrate how discussions are progressing regarding the direction of prosecutorial reform and are increasing social interest in future legal amendments.

Key Summary

  • Advisory Chairman Park Chan-woon expressed concern that the deletion of the prosecutor's command authority over SJPOs would lead to illegal and inadequate investigations.
  • He argued that the prosecutor's command authority must be included in the amendment to the Criminal Procedure Act.
  • He pointed out the lack of experience among SJPOs and emphasized the practical necessity of prosecutor guidance.
  • He warned of the confusion and harm that could result from the deletion of the prosecutor's command authority.
  • He criticized the reckless decision to delete the command authority without alternatives.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This issue arose during the process of legal amendments related to prosecutorial reform. Amid movements to reduce the powers of prosecutors and strengthen the independence of investigations, the command authority of SJPOs was deleted. However, legal expert Park Chan-woon expressed concern that such changes would cause significant problems in practice. He maintains that prosecutor guidance is essential, pointing out potential issues that could arise during investigations if SJPOs lack legal knowledge.

Why It Matters

This issue goes beyond mere legal discussions and has a significant impact on the reliability of actual investigations and prosecution processes. If SJPOs do not receive guidance from prosecutors, the consistency and fairness of law enforcement could deteriorate. This poses a potential risk of undermining the authority of the law and citizens' trust in fair investigations.

Personal Comment

In my view, Chairman Park Chan-woon's arguments are very important. Especially, having inexperienced SJPOs conduct independent investigations carries various risks. Mistakes or misjudgments that could occur during the investigation process could lead to victimization, making the need for prosecutor guidance very persuasive. Additionally, the absence of alternatives in the process of reducing prosecutorial power could lead to significant practical confusion.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect that may be overlooked in this discussion is that the deletion of SJPOs' command authority is not limited to legal issues. It could also affect the cooperation system between police and prosecutors, as well as the overall reliability of law enforcement. Particularly, if the principle of separation of investigation and prosecution does not function properly, it could create favorable conditions for criminals.

Points to Check Now

  • The changes in the cooperation system between prosecutors and SJPOs should be observed, along with the practical issues that may arise from it.
  • There is a need to closely examine the impact of future legal amendments on citizens' legal rights and safety.
