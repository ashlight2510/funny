Recently, a U.S. court has put a halt to President Donald Trump's plan to construct a banquet hall at the White House. Judge Richard Leon ruled that President Trump's attempt to demolish the East Wing of the White House and build the banquet hall without congressional approval is legally unjustifiable. He emphasized that the White House is not merely a building but a symbol of the nation, and cannot be approached differently from the way it has been managed by Congress and past presidents for centuries. As a result, President Trump's $400 million banquet hall construction will be suspended for the time being, and the court has postponed the enforcement of the order for 14 days, considering the possibility of an appeal.

This case goes beyond a simple halt to a construction plan and raises significant discussions about President Trump's authority and his relationship with Congress. While President Trump claims he has the right to modernize and beautify the White House, the court did not recognize this. It is important to observe how this conflict may impact future political situations.

Reasons to Consider the Background

President Trump's plan to construct a banquet hall at the White House began in October of last year. He proposed to demolish the East Wing and build a new structure, citing the existing dining hall as too small; however, his decision to proceed without congressional approval sparked legal controversy. The Historical Preservation Society filed a lawsuit, arguing that such construction plans could undermine the historical value of the White House. This case is evolving into a matter of power distribution between the president and Congress, rather than just a construction issue.
