Houthi Rebels' Official Declaration of Participation: What is the Fate of the Red Sea?

Recently, the Houthi rebels officially announced their participation in the Iran war. On the 28th, they launched missile attacks targeting Israel, demonstrating their solidarity with Iran and its supporters. Although the Houthis had previously shown a cautious attitude towards military intervention, they have now declared their intention to continue military operations. This raises concerns about the potential blockage of the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait, which connects the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden. Such a situation could have serious implications for the global economy.

This article serves as a reminder that we must pay attention to the ripple effects of military tensions in the Middle East on global economy and security. It is necessary to consider how the Houthi rebels' military intervention will impact the international community and how their relationship with Iran may evolve.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The Houthi rebels have been part of Iran's 'Axis of Resistance' but have maintained a cautious approach to military intervention. However, as tensions between Iran and Israel have escalated recently, the Houthis' stance has begun to change. Following Iran's airstrikes, the Houthis deemed military response necessary and officially declared their participation. This background complicates the already intricate political situation in the Middle East.

Why It Matters

The Houthi rebels' participation is not just a military event; it is a significant issue that could reshape the security landscape in the Middle East. The Red Sea and Gulf of Aden are crucial maritime routes for the global economy, and if safety in these areas is threatened, it could have severe repercussions for global logistics and supply chains. Such a situation will act as a source of instability for businesses and the economy as a whole.
