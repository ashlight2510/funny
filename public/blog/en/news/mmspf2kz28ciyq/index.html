Voices of Opposition to the Integration of Incheon, Gadeokdo, and Gimpo Airports… Union Says “Burden of New Airport Construction Costs Shifted”

Recently, strong opposition has emerged, primarily from the Incheon International Airport Corporation union, as the government considers the integration of Incheon International Airport Corporation, Korea Airports Corporation, and Gadeokdo New Airport Construction Corporation. The union argues that the integration undermines the competitiveness and public nature of Incheon Airport and claims that the government is attempting to shift the financial burden of regional airports onto Incheon Airport. Consequently, the union has expressed a strong willingness to resist, even mentioning the possibility of a strike.

This situation goes beyond a mere discussion of airport integration; it intertwines with issues of the efficiency of public institution operations and the burden of taxpayer money. The union's opposition is a significant issue that could greatly influence not only labor relations at Incheon Airport but also the future direction of regional airport policies.

Key Points

  • Strong opposition from the union regarding the consideration of integrating Incheon International Airport Corporation and Gadeokdo New Airport Construction Corporation.
  • Claims that the push for integration undermines the competitiveness and public nature of Incheon Airport and shifts financial burdens.
  • Doubts about the government's rationale for increasing efficiency through integration.
  • Voices within the union are considering strikes or external protests.
  • The government has not yet confirmed whether integration will proceed but is currently gathering opinions.

Background and Context

This integration discussion was proposed as a measure to enhance the operational efficiency of Gadeokdo New Airport. However, it is intertwined with past failures of regional airport policies, and the union criticizes the government's attitude of pursuing another new airport construction without acknowledging these policy failures. Incheon Airport is globally recognized, and the union is concerned about the repercussions of changes to its operational methods.

Why Is This Issue Important?

This issue transcends mere airport integration and leads to discussions about the operational methods of public institutions and the fairness of financial burdens. If the government forcibly proceeds with the integration, it is likely that not only the employees of Incheon Airport but the entire public will bear the burden. Additionally, questions are being raised about whether integration can actually lead to increased efficiency, which could significantly influence future policy directions.
