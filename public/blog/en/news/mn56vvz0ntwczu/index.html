Did the government spend more money than HYBE? Questions raised by the BTS concert

Recently, the comeback concert of BTS was successfully held at Gwanghwamun in Seoul, and it is estimated that the government invested more resources than HYBE. The government allowed the use of Gyeongbokgung Palace and Gwanghwamun, which are national heritage sites, and mobilized large-scale public resources to support a safe event. However, criticisms have been raised regarding whether the inconveniences experienced by citizens and the associated costs were justified. Ultimately, this concert remains a case that sparked both an opportunity for cultural promotion and controversy over the infringement of citizens' rights.

This concert served as more than just an event to celebrate BTS's return; it raised serious questions about the relationship between the government and private enterprises, as well as citizens' rights. Citizens had to endure inconveniences such as road closures and traffic disruptions due to the concert, which encompasses a complex social context beyond a simple cultural event.

Key Summary

  • BTS's comeback concert was held at Gwanghwamun in Seoul, with the government investing large-scale public resources to support it.
  • HYBE paid approximately 90 million won, but the government's tangible and intangible costs are estimated to be much higher.
  • Citizens experienced inconveniences such as traffic control due to the concert, leading to criticisms.
  • There is a need for discussions regarding the relationship between the government and private enterprises, as well as citizens' rights.

Key Scenes

  • Cost Imbalance: It appears that the costs borne by the government exceed those paid by HYBE.
  • Infringement of Citizens' Rights: The inconveniences and rights infringements experienced by citizens due to the concert have been raised as issues.
  • Cultural Events and Public Resources: There is controversy over whether it is appropriate to mobilize large-scale public resources for a specific artist's concert.
  • National Image and Cultural Promotion: The BTS concert served as an opportunity to elevate the status of Korean culture.
  • Need for Event Standards: There are calls for the establishment of standards and citizen consent procedures for similar events in the future.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This concert was a significant event announcing BTS's comeback and served as an opportunity to elevate the global status of Korean culture. However, the impact of such large-scale events on citizens' daily lives should not be overlooked. Especially in the context of enhanced safety management following the Itaewon tragedy, the deployment of large-scale personnel for the concert resulted in restrictions on citizens' rights to movement and freedom. The government should devise measures to minimize citizens' inconveniences in light of these circumstances.

Why It Matters

This issue extends beyond a single concert. The discussions surrounding how much public resources the government can allocate to private events and how citizens' rights can be protected in the process will significantly influence future cultural policies. Additionally, if the support criteria for cultural events are not clearly defined, similar controversies are likely to recur.

My Thoughts

The issues revealed by this concert demand deep reflection on the values of culture and the public beyond the commercial success of BTS and HYBE. While government support for cultural events is important, it should not come at the expense of citizens' fundamental rights. Therefore, moving forward, a balanced policy that considers both support for cultural events and the rights of citizens is necessary.

Hidden Variables to Consider

  • Although the concert was free, HYBE is expected to generate substantial profits through merchandise and album sales. This raises concerns about the government’s support contributing to the profits of private enterprises.
  • On the day of the concert, there were cases of citizens whose weddings or other events were disrupted due to traffic control. This illustrates that the concert was more than just a cultural event.

Points to Check Now

  • Attention should be paid to how the government's support criteria and citizen consent procedures for similar large-scale cultural events will change in the future.
  • Comprehensive studies are needed to analyze the positive and negative impacts of concerts by major artists like BTS on citizens.

FAQ

Q: Why is the government's support for the BTS concert problematic?
A: The large-scale support from the government may infringe on citizens' rights while contributing to the commercial interests of private enterprises.

Q: What inconveniences did citizens experience?
A: Citizens faced traffic control due to the concert, making it difficult for them to move, and some were forced to take leave from work.

Q: How can such events be improved in the future?
A: Clear support criteria for events should be established, and procedures for obtaining citizen consent should be implemented.

This concert highlights the need for deep discussions about culture and citizens' rights. We should pay attention to future policy changes and hope that citizens' voices are more reflected.
