What to Prepare Along with the Enactment of the ‘Patient Rights Act’

Recently, the National Assembly's Health and Welfare Committee passed the bill for the ‘Patient Rights Act’. If this bill is finally passed, Korea will establish itself as a country that clearly guarantees patient rights through law. The key is to view patients not merely as subjects of treatment but as entities who make decisions and participate actively. This perspective is rooted in the World Health Organization (WHO) charter, and emphasizes the need for Korea's healthcare system to shift towards a patient-centered approach, referencing modern concepts of patient rights established in the United States and Europe.

However, the enactment of the law does not immediately lead to an enhancement of patient rights. Even with the implementation of the Patient Rights Act, if there is no change in public perception, substantial changes in the healthcare field may be slow. Therefore, it is time for the government, the medical community, and patient organizations to work together to develop strategies to raise awareness about patient rights.

Key Summary

  • Passage of the Patient Rights Act: The National Assembly's Health and Welfare Committee has passed the Patient Rights Act, preparing to legally guarantee patient rights.
  • Patient Empowerment: The law recognizes patients as decision-makers rather than mere subjects of treatment.
  • Need for Public Perception Change: Even after the law is enacted, a change in perception regarding patient rights is essential.
  • Reference to International Cases: Examples of patient rights legislation from various countries, including the United States and Finland, are presented as models for change.
  • Preparations After Legislation: The government and medical community must devise plans to improve awareness of patient rights.

Why Is It Important?

The passage of the Patient Rights Act is a significant turning point that could change the paradigm of the Korean healthcare system. Recognizing patients as active participants in medical decision-making not only enhances patient rights but also contributes to the improvement of healthcare service quality. Additionally, as the legal status of patient organizations is strengthened, the likelihood of patients' voices being reflected in policy-making processes increases.

Perspectives of Patients and Practitioners

From the patient's perspective, the Patient Rights Act means gaining more rights in the treatment process. Decisions will no longer be made unilaterally by doctors; patients will have more say regarding their health and treatment. Practitioners will need to redefine their communication methods with patients to adapt to these changes. In particular, establishing a system that reflects patient opinions will be essential.

Points of Interest

In my view, the enactment of the Patient Rights Act signifies more than just the creation of a law. It is crucial to closely observe how the relationship between patients and healthcare providers will change, as well as how various conflicts and adjustments that may arise during this process will be handled in actual healthcare settings. While the enactment of the law is the starting point, the subsequent changes are what truly matter.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Even after the law is enacted, there may still be differences in perception between patients and healthcare providers. As seen in Finland, even after legislation is passed, the attitudes and practices of healthcare professionals often do not change easily. Therefore, for the Patient Rights Act to be effective, continuous education and efforts to improve awareness must be pursued after the law is enacted.

Points to Check Now

  • Attention should be paid to what specific measures the government and medical community will prepare for changing perceptions after the passage of the Patient Rights Act.
  • It is important to monitor how educational programs regarding patient rights will be implemented and what changes will occur in healthcare settings.

FAQ

Q: What rights will patients gain if the Patient Rights Act is passed?
A: Patients will have the right to receive sufficient explanations about treatment methods and the right to receive quality healthcare without discrimination.

Q: What is the response of the medical community after the enactment of the Patient Rights Act?
A: The medical community will need to work on redefining communication methods with patients and establishing systems that reflect patient opinions.

Q: Will conflicts between patients and healthcare providers decrease with the implementation of the Patient Rights Act?
A: While the law guarantees patient rights, conflicts may still exist if there is no accompanying change in perception.

The enactment of the Patient Rights Act presents an opportunity for fundamental change in the Korean healthcare system. At this moment, discussions are needed on how the relationship between patients and healthcare providers will evolve and the various issues that may arise during this process.
