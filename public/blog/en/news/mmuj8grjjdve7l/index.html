Prosecution Office 'Abolition of Special Judicial Police Command and Supervision'... Provisions to 'Prevent Restoration' of Investigation Rights

Recently, the Democratic Party announced the final draft of the law for the establishment of the Prosecution Office and the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency (SIA). This bill includes the abolition of the command and supervision authority of Prosecution Office prosecutors over special judicial police officers (SJPOs) and limits the scope of prosecutors' duties by law. These changes are interpreted as an intention to reduce the authority of the prosecution and make it difficult to restore investigation rights.

If this bill passes, about 20,000 SJPOs will be freed from judicial control by prosecutors, and the investigation authority and scope of the SIA will also expand. As a result, the crime of legal distortion will be included in the investigation targets of the SIA, and the scope of public officials subject to investigation will also be expanded to include court-affiliated officials.

Key Summary

  • Abolition of Prosecution Office prosecutors' command and supervision authority over SJPOs
  • Limitation of prosecutors' duties by law, blocking amendments to enforcement decrees
  • Expansion of the SIA's investigation authority and inclusion of legal distortion crimes
  • Addition of court-affiliated officials to the investigation targets
  • Title of the head of the Prosecution Office remains as Prosecutor General

Key Scenes

  • Reduction of Prosecutors' Authority: The removal of prosecutors' command and supervision authority reduces the power of the prosecution.
  • Independence of SJPOs: About 20,000 SJPOs will be free from prosecutors' control.
  • Expansion of Investigation Scope: The investigation scope of the SIA will broaden to include legal distortion crimes.
  • Inclusion of Court Officials: Court-affiliated officials will be added to the investigation targets, strengthening legal accountability.
  • Blocking Amendments to Enforcement Decrees: The regulations on prosecutors' duties are set by law, making it difficult to restore authority through amendments to enforcement decrees.

Why Background Matters

This bill reflects the Democratic Party's intention to limit the prosecution's authority again, in response to the expansion of investigation rights under the Yoon Seok-yeol administration. The Democratic Party has been pushing for legislation aimed at prosecutorial reform, continuously working to reduce the prosecution's power and strengthen investigative independence. In particular, the abolition of the command and supervision authority over SJPOs can be seen as a significant measure to diminish the influence of the prosecution.

Why It Matters

This bill attempts to balance the powers of investigative agencies by limiting the authority of the prosecution, which could contribute to strengthening democracy and the rule of law. Reducing the prosecution's power is expected to decrease undue pressure or abuse of power during investigations. However, there are concerns that such changes may actually impair the efficiency of law enforcement.

Personal Comment

In my view, the core of this bill is the reduction of prosecutorial power and the strengthening of investigative independence. However, discussions are needed on how these changes will actually play out in practice. If the prosecution's authority diminishes, the responsibility for investigations may shift to other agencies, necessitating preparations for potential confusion or inefficiencies that could arise.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Some media outlets emphasize only the positive aspects of this bill, but there may be side effects such as a lack of cooperation among investigative agencies or evasion of responsibility. In particular, if conflicts between the SIA and the police intensify, it could lead to inconsistencies in investigations, which is a point to watch closely.

Points to Check Now

If this bill passes in the National Assembly, it is important to closely monitor how the distribution of authority between the prosecution and the SIA changes and how this will affect the efficiency of law enforcement.

FAQ

Q1: What changes will occur if Prosecution Office prosecutors do not command SJPOs?
A1: The independence of SJPOs will be strengthened, allowing them to conduct investigations without being under the control of prosecutors. This can enhance investigative autonomy, but may also lead to unclear accountability.

Q2: What impact will the expansion of the SIA's investigation scope have?
A2: The SIA will be able to investigate various crimes, including legal distortion, further strengthening legal accountability for public officials. However, this may increase the investigative burden on the SIA.

Q3: What is the impact of reducing prosecutorial authority on law enforcement?
A3: Reducing prosecutorial authority may decrease the risk of abuse of power during investigations, but it could also negatively affect the consistency and efficiency of investigations.
