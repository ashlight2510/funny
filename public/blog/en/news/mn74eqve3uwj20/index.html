Jeong Myeong-geun, Mayor of Hwaseong, conducts emergency inspections of local businesses and farms due to 'energy supply instability'

Recently, Mayor Jeong Myeong-geun of Hwaseong conducted emergency inspections of local manufacturers and flower farms to address the energy supply instability caused by the unrest in the Middle East. The city of Hwaseong revealed that, following a survey of 220 companies conducted from the 17th to the 20th, a staggering 86.4% of these companies are facing operational disruptions. In response, Hwaseong has expanded the eligibility for working capital support and increased funding for small and medium-sized enterprises in Gyeonggi Province.

This measure is not just about alleviating the economic burden on businesses but is also an effort to minimize the overall impact on the local economy. Mayor Jeong emphasized that the central government's emergency economic policies must be felt practically on the ground.

Key Summary

  • Mayor Jeong Myeong-geun inspects local businesses and farms due to energy supply instability.
  • A survey of 220 companies found that 86.4% are experiencing operational disruptions.
  • Expansion of working capital support eligibility and increase in funding for small and medium-sized enterprises in Gyeonggi Province.
  • Additional support measures announced, including an increase in export logistics cost support limits and extension of local tax deadlines.
  • Mayor Jeong is committed to looking after the lives of businesses and citizens on the ground.

Key Scenes

  • Operational Disruptions: 86.4% of companies are struggling with operations, necessitating immediate support.
  • Support Expansion: The number of companies eligible for working capital support increased from 202 to 302.
  • Tax Support: Local tax deadlines extended and collection delays implemented to ease the burden on businesses.
  • Logistics Cost Support: The limit for export logistics cost support raised from 3 million won to 5 million won per company.
  • Caring for Livelihoods: Attention is drawn to whether Mayor Jeong's commitment can bring about tangible changes in the lives of businesses and citizens.

Why Background Matters

This issue arose as the instability in the Middle East increasingly impacts energy supply. In particular, the sharp rise in energy prices has increased production costs for manufacturers and the agricultural sector, leading to operational disruptions. Hwaseong conducted an urgent survey to understand the realities faced by businesses and to prepare swift support measures.

Why It Matters

This action goes beyond mere economic support; it holds significant implications for ensuring the sustainability of the local economy. By supporting businesses in normalizing operations, local jobs and economic activities can be maintained. Furthermore, such measures can serve as a good precedent for companies in other regions facing similar issues.
