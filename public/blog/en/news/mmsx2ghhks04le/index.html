Supreme Court, concerned about judges' intimidation due to the crime of distorting the law, “will create a task force to protect and support criminal trials”

Recently, the Supreme Court announced that it will form a 'task force to protect and support criminal trials' to prevent judges from feeling intimidated due to the crime of distorting the law. This initiative aims to support judges in actively conducting trials according to the law and their conscience. According to the announcement by the Deputy Chief of the Court Administration Office, a follow-up research group for trial petitions will also be formed within the court. Concerns have also been raised regarding the proposed amendment to the Court Organization Act related to increasing the number of judges.

This announcement reflects the commitment to ensure that the judiciary's functions are not diminished and is interpreted as a measure to alleviate judges' fears of being punished under the crime of distorting the law. The Court Administration Office stated that it will strive to ensure that judges can conduct trials with composure through institutional improvements and budget securing.

Key Summary

  • The Supreme Court announced the creation of a task force to protect and support criminal trials in response to concerns about judges' intimidation due to the crime of distorting the law.
  • Plans to enhance the stability of the judicial system by forming a 'follow-up research group for trial petitions.'
  • Concerns have been raised regarding the proposed amendment to the Court Organization Act related to increasing the number of judges, and measures to strengthen judges' trial capabilities are also being explored.

Key Scenes

Reasons to Consider the Background

This measure arises from the situation where judges may fear legal responsibility due to the establishment of the crime of distorting the law and the introduction of trial petitions. The Supreme Court is actively working to create an environment where judges can conduct trials according to the law and their conscience. In particular, the proposed amendment to the Court Organization Act related to increasing the number of judges is expected to play a crucial role in enhancing the court's trial capabilities.

Why It Matters

This news represents an important turning point for the independence of the South Korean judiciary and the protection of judges' trial rights. An environment where judges can conduct trials freely without intimidation ultimately contributes to the protection of citizens' rights. Additionally, changes within the court are expected to enhance the credibility of the judicial system.
