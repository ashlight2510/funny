From a Pirate Species to a High-Income Variety... Jeonnam Marine Research Institute Accelerates the Cultivation of the Horned Sea Snail

The Jeonnam Marine Research Institute has recently applied for a patent on the artificial seed production technology of the horned sea snail and has begun developing new aquaculture varieties. This research is a result of complete aquaculture studies that started in 2024. The horned sea snail, previously recognized as a pirate species, is now gaining attention for its aquaculture potential due to increasing consumer preference. This species has strong resilience to environmental changes, making it a promising candidate to respond to climate change and deteriorating fishing environments.

This issue is not merely a change in aquaculture but carries the potential to provide new sources of income for local fishermen. The Jeonnam Marine Research Institute plans to enhance the artificial seed production technology of the horned sea snail through future research, thereby promoting sustainable aquaculture development.

Why Background Matters

The horned sea snail has long been perceived as a pirate species that preys on other shellfish. However, recent increases in demand from the restaurant industry, along with its chewy texture and flavor, have made it popular among consumers. This shift is the background for the Jeonnam Marine Research Institute's attempt to cultivate the horned sea snail. Previous research was limited to collecting and cultivating wild juvenile specimens, but this patent application signifies advancements in artificial seed production technology.

Why It Matters

This research goes beyond simply developing a new aquaculture variety. The need for a new high-income aquaculture variety that can respond to climate change and environmental degradation means that the cultivation of horned sea snails offers local fishermen practical opportunities for income increase and sustainable fisheries development. Additionally, it will play a crucial role in enhancing the competitiveness of the fisheries industry in Jeollanam-do.

Points to Note

In my view, if the cultivation of horned sea snails is successfully achieved, it will not only positively impact the local economy but also serve as a good example for the cultivation of other marine organisms. Particularly, if varieties resilient to environmental changes are developed, we can expect positive ripple effects across the entire fisheries industry in the future.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Several variables exist for this research to proceed successfully. It is crucial to determine whether the artificial seed production technology can be applied in actual aquaculture and whether consumers will continue to maintain their preference for horned sea snails. Additionally, research on various environmental factors that may arise during the cultivation process is also necessary.
