Blockade of the Strait of Hormuz: A Warning Signal for the Oil Market

Recently, oil analyst Lori Johnston warned about the serious situation that the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz could bring. He pointed out that this blockade has halted the supply of 20 million barrels of crude oil per day, which accounts for one-fifth of the global oil supply. He also expressed concerns that the financial market is not properly recognizing the seriousness of the situation. If the situation continues for a long time, he predicted that it could lead to an economic crisis at the level of the Great Depression.

This issue goes beyond a simple energy supply crisis and could have serious impacts on the global economy. In particular, developing countries in Asia and Africa are already struggling with fuel shortages and rising prices, and if the conflict between Iran and the United States continues, the situation is likely to worsen.

Key Points

  • The blockade of the Strait of Hormuz has halted the supply of 20 million barrels of crude oil per day.
  • The global financial market is underestimating the seriousness of the supply shortage.
  • Experts warn that this situation could lead to an economic crisis at the level of the Great Depression.
  • The damage is primarily concentrated in developing countries in Asia and Africa.
  • If the Strait of Hormuz remains closed, there is a high possibility of a sharp rise in oil prices.

Background and Context of the Issue

The recent blockade of the Strait of Hormuz occurred as tensions escalated between Iran and the United States. Iran's military actions and U.S. sanctions have blocked oil transport routes. Compared to the oil embargo measures of the 1970s, the scale of this blockade is much larger, and the economic repercussions of a supply chain collapse could be beyond imagination.

Why It Matters

This issue is not just about rising oil prices. The interruption of crude oil supply affects various industries, including automotive, aviation, and shipping, and ultimately impacts consumers as well. In particular, developing countries with high energy dependence are at significant risk of social unrest due to fuel shortages.

Points to Note

In my view, the seriousness of this issue goes beyond just price increases. If energy supply is interrupted, people may feel a threat to their survival beyond mere inconveniences in daily life. For example, countries like the Philippines are declaring states of emergency and taking urgent measures to secure energy supply stability. Such situations could spread to other developing countries, causing significant repercussions in the international community.

Hidden Variables to Watch

Experts warn that even if the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz is lifted, it will take at least four months for crude oil supply to normalize. Even if negotiations with Iran are concluded, shipping insurers are likely to refuse operations, and it will take time to restart refining facilities. Therefore, this issue will not be resolved with a short-term solution.

Current Checkpoints

The most important thing in the current situation is to closely monitor the situation in the Strait of Hormuz and changes in international oil prices. In particular, if energy prices surge, individuals and businesses need to prepare response strategies.

FAQ

Q: When is the Strait of Hormuz likely to reopen?
A: Currently, it depends on the outcome of negotiations between Iran and the United States, but if the situation worsens, the reopening may be delayed.

Q: How much more can oil prices rise?
A: Experts mention the possibility of prices rising to $160 per barrel, which may vary depending on Dubai crude prices.

Q: How will developing countries be affected?
A: Economic turmoil may arise due to fuel shortages, and phenomena like hoarding are already occurring.

Q: How should individuals cope in this situation?
A: It is important to reduce energy consumption and prepare resources that can be stockpiled for emergencies.
