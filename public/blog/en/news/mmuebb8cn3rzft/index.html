Gradual Constitutional Reform, the People Power Party Should Join

Recently, President Lee Jae-myung expressed support for the gradual constitutional reform proposed by National Assembly Speaker Woo Won-sik during a Cabinet meeting. He suggested that constitutional amendments should be pursued step by step, starting with issues agreed upon by the public. Specifically, discussions are underway to include the spirit of the May 18 Democratic Movement and the Busan-Masan Uprising in the preamble of the constitution, and to make prior consent from the National Assembly mandatory when declaring a state of emergency. However, there are still significant differences in opinions between the ruling and opposition parties, and the People Power Party is treating the constitutional discussion as a political tool, raising concerns.

This article critiques the necessity of constitutional discussions and the attitudes of the political sphere, emphasizing the need for political efforts to ensure that the constitutional amendments reflect the will of the people.

Key Summary

  • President Lee Jae-myung supports gradual constitutional reform and directs government-level preparations.
  • Expression of intent to include the spirit of the May 18 Democratic Movement and the Busan-Masan Uprising in the preamble of the constitution.
  • Proposed constitutional amendments include making prior consent from the National Assembly mandatory when declaring a state of emergency.
  • Concerns arise as differences in opinions between the ruling and opposition parties delay constitutional discussions.
  • 70% of the public supports constitutional reform, urging responsible actions from the political sphere.

Key Scenes

  • Gradual Constitutional Reform: Pursuing step-by-step amendments based on public consent.
  • Constitutionalization of Democratic Movement Spirit: Specifying the spirits of May 18 and the Busan-Masan Uprising in the preamble of the constitution.
  • Strengthening Emergency Declaration Requirements: Protecting constitutional order by mandating prior consent from the National Assembly.
  • Political Attitude: Delays in constitutional discussions due to differences in positions between the ruling and opposition parties.
  • Public Support: 70% support for constitutional reform, highlighting the need for responsible actions from the political sphere.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The current discussions on constitutional reform are intended to reflect the historical significance of various events that occurred during Korea's democratization process in the constitution. After 39 years since 1987, there is a growing voice for amendments that align with the demands of the times. In particular, there is an increasing awareness of the urgent need for legal mechanisms to prevent the recurrence of tragic events like the declaration of a state of emergency.

Why Is It Important?

Constitutional reform is not merely a legal procedure. It is a crucial process for incorporating the voices of the people into the constitution and strengthening democracy. Although conflicts between the ruling and opposition parties are intensifying, failure to achieve constitutional reform could once again threaten the constitutional order. With 70% of the public wanting constitutional reform, ignoring this demand could lead to a significant loss of trust in the political sphere.

Role of the Political Sphere

Politicians from both the ruling and opposition parties must actively participate in constitutional discussions. In particular, the People Power Party should view constitutional reform not as a political tool for elections but as an essential task for the advancement of democracy. If constitutional reform does not occur, the consequences will ultimately fall back on the people.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect that may be overlooked in the current discussions on constitutional reform is the need to consider how the amendments will actually impact the lives of the people, rather than merely reaching a political agreement. When constitutional amendments are made, it is essential to clarify how the content will bring about real changes to the rights and obligations of the citizens.

Points to Check Now

  • Change in Political Attitude: Check if dialogue is beginning between the ruling and opposition parties.
  • Public Reaction: Observe how public opinion is changing through opinion polls.
  • Constitutional Reform Schedule: Pay close attention to the formation of the constitutional reform committee and the progress of related bills.
