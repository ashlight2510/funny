Outbreak of African Swine Fever at a Pig Farm in Hampyeong, Jeollanam-do···“Strengthening Quarantine Management”

An outbreak of African Swine Fever (ASF) has occurred at a pig farm in Hampyeong, Jeollanam-do. In response, Jeollanam-do has immediately implemented quarantine measures, restricting access to the affected farm and conducting emergency disinfection. The pigs being raised at this farm will be culled, and post-management to prevent environmental contamination in the surrounding area will also be conducted. Currently, livestock movement control and inspections are being carried out for pig farms and related facilities within the 10 km radius designated as a quarantine area.

This incident marks the fourth occurrence of ASF this year, highlighting the urgent need for robust quarantine measures to prevent its spread in the Jeollanam-do region. Jeollanam-do is conducting thorough quarantines for farms and epidemiological investigations into the infection routes within the region.

Why Background Matters

African Swine Fever is a deadly viral disease for pigs, exhibiting high mortality rates upon infection. It has occurred multiple times in South Korea over the past few years, significantly impacting the livestock industry, and this incident is a continuation of that trend. Jeollanam-do has already experienced ASF outbreaks in other regions, and this occurrence reveals vulnerabilities in the local quarantine system.

Why It Matters

This ASF outbreak is not just a problem for one farm; it has significant implications for the livestock industry across Jeollanam-do and South Korea. ASF is highly contagious, and if it spreads, it could lead to rising prices for livestock products and economic damage to farms. Additionally, it could raise concerns about food safety for consumers.
