“We Must Prevent the Attraction of SMR Without Safety Verification”··· Opposition Movement by Busan Civic Groups Intensifies

As Gijang County in Busan plans to present an agenda for attracting innovative small modular reactors (i-SMR) at the plenary session, civic groups are protesting. They are planning rallies urging the county council to reject the application due to the unverified safety of SMRs. Gijang County argues that utilizing the site of the previously constructed Shin-Kori Units 7 and 8 allows for rapid commencement of construction, but civic groups claim this could threaten the safety of Busan citizens.

This article addresses issues related to significant decisions that will affect the safety and future of local residents, going beyond the simple pro and con debate over attracting nuclear power plants. The matter is further complicated by the intertwining concerns about safety and the potential positive impact of SMR attraction on the local economy.

Key Summary

  • The Busan Gijang County Council is reviewing the agenda for the SMR attraction application, and civic groups are strongly opposing it.
  • The opposition movement is intensifying due to the unverified safety of SMRs.
  • Gijang County emphasizes the advantage of being able to quickly commence construction by utilizing existing nuclear power plant sites.
  • Civic groups warn that attracting SMRs will increase risks for the entire Busan area.
  • The government is pushing for the construction of new nuclear power plants, intensifying competition for SMR attraction.

Key Scenes

  • Safety Concerns: SMRs have not been used anywhere in the world, and safety verification has not been conducted.
  • Civic Group Opposition: Civic groups, including the Busan Nuclear-Free Citizens' Alliance, are planning rallies urging the rejection of the application.
  • Economic Incentives: If SMRs are attracted, significant economic support for the region is expected.
  • Significance of Political Decisions: There are claims that the decision by the Gijang County Council will be a turning point in Busan's nuclear power policy direction.
  • Competing Local Governments: Other regions, such as Gyeongju, are also entering the competition for SMR attraction.

Reasons to Consider the Background

Gijang County has formalized the attraction of SMRs through cooperation with Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power. This has the advantage of allowing for rapid construction by utilizing the site of the previously constructed Shin-Kori Units 7 and 8. However, civic groups are concerned that such economic incentives may overlook safety issues. The government is currently pursuing plans for new nuclear power plant construction, and the conflict between the positive economic impacts of SMR attraction and safety concerns is intensifying.

Why It Matters

This issue is not just about whether Gijang County will attract nuclear power plants; it is a significant issue that will determine the safety and future of residents in the Busan area. If SMRs are attracted, Busan citizens may be at risk without verified safety. Conversely, if economic support is provided, it could positively impact the local economy. Thus, this situation illustrates a complex conflict between two important factors: safety and economics.

Personal Perspective

In my view, the core of this issue is safety verification. The fact that SMRs have not been used anywhere poses a significant risk. The opposition from civic groups can be seen not merely as resistance but as a necessary action to protect the lives and safety of Busan citizens. Ultimately, it will be crucial how seriously the Gijang County Council takes this issue and how much it reflects the voices of the residents.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect that may be overlooked in this incident is the tendency to overemphasize the economic impact of SMR attraction on the local economy. If economic support is actually provided, there needs to be a concrete plan for how that support will be distributed to local residents. Additionally, measures to prepare for potential safety incidents that may occur after the attraction of SMRs should also be discussed.

Points to Check Now

  • It is important to pay attention to how the Gijang County Council makes decisions regarding the SMR attraction application agenda.
  • Observing how the civic groups' opposition movement unfolds and what results it produces is crucial.
  • Finding materials that analyze the positive or negative impacts of SMR attraction on the local economy would also be beneficial.

FAQ

  • What is SMR? SMR stands for Small Modular Reactor, which is designed to be smaller in scale than existing nuclear reactors.

  • Why are civic groups opposing it? Civic groups argue that the safety of SMRs has not been verified and express concerns about the associated risks.

  • What economic benefits would there be if SMRs are attracted? Regions that attract SMRs are expected to receive significant funding and coexistence funds throughout the design life of the reactors.

  • When will the Gijang County Council decide on the SMR attraction agenda? The Gijang County Council is scheduled to review it at the Urban Safety Committee on the 24th and make a final decision at the plenary session on the 25th.

Considering these points, the decision on whether Busan Gijang County will attract SMRs is not merely a policy decision but a significant choice regarding the safety and future of local residents. I recommend checking how discussions on this issue are progressing right now.
