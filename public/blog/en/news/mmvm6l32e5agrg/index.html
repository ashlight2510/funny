“No Complicity in Aggressive Wars”… 660 Civil Organizations Declare Opposition to Deployment in Hormuz

Over 660 civil organizations have announced a declaration opposing the deployment of South Korean naval vessels to the Strait of Hormuz. They classify the attacks by the United States and Israel on Iran as an aggressive war and urged the South Korean government to reject this involvement. They argue that actions violating international law exacerbate global economic and social suffering, and that South Korea's participation in war contradicts the constitution.

This declaration goes beyond mere opposition; it emphasizes the power of citizen solidarity in protecting global peace and human rights. They raised their voices, stating that it is the current citizens—not those who experienced the horrors of war—who must act to prevent war.

Key Summary

  • Over 660 civil organizations announced a declaration opposing the deployment of South Korean naval vessels to the Strait of Hormuz.
  • They classify the attacks by the United States and Israel on Iran as war crimes and urge the South Korean government to refuse the request for deployment.
  • Concerns were expressed that the global economic crisis and social suffering would worsen due to the deployment.
  • They strongly oppose participation in war, citing potential constitutional violations.

Key Scenes

  • Solidarity of Civil Organizations: A declaration signed by 660 organizations and 1,715 individuals.
  • Violation of International Law: The attacks by the United States and Israel on Iran are considered invasions that violate the UN Charter.
  • Economic Impact: Concerns that the militarization of the Strait of Hormuz could lead to rising international oil prices and economic burdens falling on vulnerable populations.
  • Constitutional Issues: Claims that South Korea's deployment would violate Article 5, Section 1 of the Constitution.
  • Horrors of War: Emphasis on the importance of citizen solidarity to prevent war.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This declaration comes in the context of escalating attacks by the United States and Israel on Iran, raising the possibility of South Korea's military involvement. The Strait of Hormuz is a major route for international trade, and instability in this region significantly impacts the global economy, prompting civil organizations to actively voice their concerns. The conflict with Iran is not limited to military issues but also extends to economic and social problems, necessitating a more cautious approach.

Why It Matters

This declaration reflects citizens' awareness of peace and human rights in South Korean society, transcending mere military issues. Opposition to war is not just an expression of opinion; it is a critical issue that determines the direction in which our society should move. Citizens voicing their concerns is a vital element of democracy, and through this, they can influence government decisions.
