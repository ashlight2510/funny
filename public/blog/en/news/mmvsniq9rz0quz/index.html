‘Forced Dissolution of the Anti-Japanese Special Committee’ 77 Years Later… First Truth Commission Application

Recently, the forced dissolution of the Anti-Japanese Special Committee, or Anti-Japan Committee, has come back into the spotlight 77 years later with a truth-finding application. The Anti-Japan Committee was established in 1948 by the National Assembly to investigate and punish pro-Japanese activities during the Japanese colonial period, but it was illegally dissolved by the Syngman Rhee government. Now, the family of the committee's chairman has submitted an application to the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, seeking to clarify the illegality of the forced dissolution, initiating a movement to uncover the truth of this incident once again.

This truth-finding application holds significant meaning beyond mere historical resolution, prompting reflection on the historical pain and suffering of Korean society. In particular, it provides an opportunity to re-examine the voices of independence activists and their families who suffered during the illegal dissolution of the Anti-Japan Committee, and it serves as a reminder of the necessity to eradicate pro-Japanese elements.

Why Background Context Matters

The Anti-Japan Committee was established in 1948, shortly after the founding of the Republic of Korea, to investigate and punish pro-Japanese activities during the Japanese colonial period. However, the Syngman Rhee government strongly opposed it and took various measures to undermine its activities. Notably, the National Assembly spy incident in 1949 had a decisive impact on the dissolution of the Anti-Japan Committee. This incident involved the illegal arrest and torture of opposition-minded lawmakers by the government, resulting in most of the committee's members being imprisoned.

In this historical context, the current truth-finding application poses an important question about how Korean society remembers and heals from past pains, transcending mere legal procedures.

Why It Matters

This case goes beyond merely clarifying historical facts. The forced dissolution of the Anti-Japan Committee symbolically reveals the relationship between power and law in modern Korean history and reawakens the necessity to eradicate pro-Japanese elements. Additionally, it provides an opportunity for victims and their families to express long-held pain. This can help foster social empathy and lay the groundwork for historical justice.
