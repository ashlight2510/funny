Danish General Election: Left Wins but Fails to Secure Majority... Frederiksen Faces Challenge for Third Term

In the recent Danish general election, the Social Democrats led by Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen won, but they failed to secure a majority of seats. The left coalition obtained a total of 84 seats, narrowly surpassing the right coalition (77 seats), but the task of forming a coalition government remains. In particular, Prime Minister Frederiksen has expressed her intention to seek a third term, but dissatisfaction regarding livelihood issues and immigration policies persists, making future governance challenging.

This election garnered significant attention not only in Denmark but also in Greenland, holding important significance in the historical context of Greenland. Prime Minister Frederiksen's firm response to President Trump's attempt to annex Greenland boosted her approval ratings, leading to the call for an early election. However, amid this political backdrop, livelihood issues continue to emerge as major points of contention.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This election took place amid significant changes in Denmark's political environment. In particular, Prime Minister Frederiksen's firm response to President Trump's attempt to annex Greenland led to a rise in her approval ratings. However, the ongoing surge in housing and living costs, along with dissatisfaction regarding immigration policies, greatly influenced voters, reflecting in the election results.
