Special Investigation Officer's Investigative Authority Abolished... 'Illegal Investigation Controversy' May Intensify

Recently, the ruling party, government, and Blue House agreed on an amendment to the law for the establishment of the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency and the Prosecution Service, resulting in the abolition of prosecutors' investigative authority over special investigation officers (SIOs). This amendment significantly reduces the authority of prosecutors in the Prosecution Service and is interpreted as an effort to block the vertical involvement of prosecutors in all investigations. However, concerns have been raised that if SIOs, who lack legal knowledge, conduct investigations independently, it may lead to increased controversy over illegal investigations.

This issue goes beyond simple legal changes and could increase the potential for human rights violations during investigations, as well as weaken the independence of the Prosecution Service. Therefore, discussions are needed on what impact this change will have and how relevant stakeholders will respond.

Key Summary

  • Abolition of SIO's investigative authority blocks vertical involvement of prosecutors in the Prosecution Service.
  • Concerns over lack of investigative expertise increase potential for illegal investigation controversies.
  • The indictment rate for cases referred by SIOs falls below 50%, raising questions about investigative procedures.
  • Need for balance between securing the independence of the Prosecution Service and protecting human rights.

Key Scenes

  • Abolition of Prosecutors' Investigative Authority: Blocks all possibilities for prosecutors in the Prosecution Service to intervene in SIO investigations.
  • Concerns Over Lack of Expertise: Increased potential for illegal investigations due to SIOs' lack of legal knowledge.
  • Potential for Human Rights Violations: Human rights protection mechanisms during investigations may weaken.
  • Indictment Rate Issues: Low indictment rates for cases referred by SIOs raise doubts about the reliability of investigative outcomes.
  • Political Controversy: Ongoing criticism that the Prosecution Service is merely a renamed prosecutor's office.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This amendment is part of efforts for prosecutorial reform, aimed at reducing the authority of prosecutors and strengthening the independence of SIOs. However, the government has maintained that some level of prosecutor oversight is necessary for SIO investigations. Discussions have continued on how the relationships between investigative agencies will be established in this context. In particular, controversies surrounding the potential for human rights violations and the legality of investigative procedures are expected to persist.

Why It Matters

This change carries significance beyond mere legal adjustments. If SIO investigations proceed without legal verification, it could directly affect the rights and freedoms of ordinary citizens. Additionally, if the independence of the Prosecution Service is strengthened while human rights protection is neglected, it could undermine social trust. Therefore, this issue is important not only for the legal community but also for the general public.

Points to Watch

In my view, the actual impact of this change depends on how SIOs operate during investigations. If legal standards and human rights protections are not properly implemented during the investigative process, controversies over illegal investigations will likely become more serious. Furthermore, if SIOs conduct investigations independently without sufficient legal knowledge, the trustworthiness of the outcomes may decrease.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The fact that prosecutors in the Prosecution Service will lose minimal oversight authority over investigative agencies in this amendment cannot be overlooked. As mechanisms to prevent human rights violations during investigations weaken, there is a need for preemptive measures to address potential investigative issues that may arise in the future. Additionally, it is crucial to devise strategies to bridge the gap in expertise and legal knowledge among SIOs.

Current Checkpoints

  • Legal Procedures and Human Rights Protection: We must closely observe how mechanisms for human rights protection will function during SIO investigations.
  • Reliability of Investigative Outcomes: Continuous monitoring is necessary to assess how trustworthy the results of investigations conducted independently by SIOs will be.

  2. What impact does this change have on ordinary citizens?

    • It may increase the potential for human rights violations during investigations and raise concerns about the reliability of investigative outcomes.

  3. How can the lack of expertise among SIOs be addressed?

    • There is a need to enhance legal education and training to improve the legal knowledge of SIOs.

  4. What problems might arise from strengthening the independence of the Prosecution Service?

In conclusion, the abolition of the SIO's investigative authority represents more than just a legal change; it leaves important questions about how to maintain human rights protection and legal standards in future investigative processes.
