The Secrets of the Strange Exoplanet L98-59d Discovered in Space

Recent researchers have suggested that there is a high possibility of a large amount of hydrogen sulfide in the atmosphere of the exoplanet L98-59d, located about 35 light-years from Earth. This gas is known to be the cause of the foul smell of rotten eggs or flatulence, and it is believed to be a major component of the planet's atmosphere. The research team observed this planet using the James Webb Space Telescope, where sulfur dioxide was discovered, leading to the hypothesis that hydrogen sulfide must be continuously supplied. This discovery is noted as an important case that expands the diversity of exoplanets.

This research goes beyond simply discovering a new exoplanet; it presents a new perspective on the possibility of life in space. The atmospheric composition of L98-59d represents an environment that is entirely different from that of past Earth, which will serve as important foundational data for exploring the possibility of life on other planets.
