Discussion on the Opening of the Strait of Hormuz by Foreign Ministers from Over 40 Countries

Recently, foreign ministers from over 40 countries gathered to discuss ways to open the Strait of Hormuz. The purpose of this meeting was to secure safe navigation through the strait, which has been effectively blocked due to conflicts with Iran. The video conference, chaired by British Foreign Secretary Yvette Cooper, included foreign ministers from various countries, including South Korea, and addressed key issues such as ship attacks in the strait and the safety of trapped crew members. Although the United States did not attend this meeting, there was a consensus on concerns regarding the impact of Iran's actions on the global economy.

These discussions emphasize the need for international cooperation and pressure, rather than merely diplomatic dialogue. This is particularly crucial for European and Asian countries that rely on safe navigation for importing oil and gas.

Why Background Matters

The Strait of Hormuz is a crucial maritime route through which approximately 20% of the world's oil exports pass. As tensions with Iran have escalated, safety issues in the strait have come to the forefront, prompting various countries to seek solutions. Notably, the lukewarm response of allied nations to U.S. requests for military intervention has sparked new discussions on international security.
