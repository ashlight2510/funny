Third ‘No Kings’ Protest Sets New Record Again

The third ‘No Kings’ protest held in the U.S. on the 28th is estimated to have drawn approximately 8 million participants, marking the largest turnout in history. This protest was a reaction against the policies of the Donald Trump administration and occurred simultaneously in cities across the U.S., including Washington, New York, and Minneapolis, as well as in major cities abroad. Participants shouted slogans against war and deportations of immigrants, expressing their resistance to the government's unilateral decisions. Notably, the demands of the participants have become increasingly diverse with each protest.

These protests are not merely anti-government movements but have established themselves as significant phenomena revealing political discontent and social conflicts within the U.S. Participants expressed concerns about war and fascism, urging the government to listen to the voices of its citizens.

Key Summary

  • Protest Scale: Approximately 8 million participants in the third ‘No Kings’ protest, setting a new record.
  • Main Slogans: Various demands emerged, including ‘NO ICE’, ‘NO WAR’, ‘NO BOMB’.
  • Participants' Background: Diverse groups including veterans and ordinary citizens expressed concerns about the government's war policies.
  • Political Context: Declining approval ratings for the Trump administration and increasing political pressure ahead of the midterm elections.
  • Spread of Protests: A surge in participation even in areas with strong Republican support.

Why It Matters

This protest goes beyond mere opposition to the Trump administration; it reveals deep-seated conflicts and divisions within American society. In particular, dissatisfaction is growing regarding the impact of diplomatic decisions, such as the Iran War, on citizens' daily lives. This can be interpreted as a warning that if the government ignores the voices of the people, it may provoke even greater social resistance.

Background and Context of the Protest

The ‘No Kings’ protest is the third to be held following those in June and October of last year, with each event seeing an increase in participation. Notably, the combination of opposition to the U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and anti-war slogans has made the nature of the protests more complex. The backlash against the Trump administration's policies has become a symbol of social solidarity and resistance rather than just political opposition.

Personal Interpretation

In my view, this protest is evolving into an important platform that gathers diverse voices from American society rather than being merely an anti-government movement. Especially as concerns about war and foreign policy grow among citizens, the government needs to pay attention to these voices. Otherwise, the scale of protests and the number of participants will likely increase, leading to political instability.

Hidden Variables to Consider

While the growing scale of protests can be seen as a positive sign, it is also important not to overlook that the government's response could exacerbate conflicts. The White House has downplayed the protests as a “product of a leftist funding network,” but this could be interpreted as a statement that further incites backlash from younger generations.

Points to Check Now

  • Political Sentiment: It is necessary to pay attention to changes in approval ratings for the Trump administration and reactions from younger people.
  • Sustainability of Protests: We should monitor how much larger future protests will become and how the government’s responses will change.
