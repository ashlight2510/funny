Recently, Kim Byeong-heon, the representative of the far-right civic group 'National Action for the Abolition of the Comfort Women Law', was arrested on charges of insulting victims of Japanese military comfort women. The Seoul Central District Court issued an arrest warrant, determining that there were concerns he might flee, and he is facing legal punishment due to his insulting remarks and actions toward comfort women victims. This incident has sparked social controversy regarding historical facts and human rights issues, going beyond a mere individual crime.

This case highlights not only the crime of Kim Byeong-heon but also exacerbates social conflicts related to the issue of Japanese military comfort women. In particular, the activities of far-right groups have a serious impact on comfort women victims and their human rights, prompting a reevaluation of how Korean society perceives historical facts.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The 'National Action for the Abolition of the Comfort Women Law', led by Kim Byeong-heon, is a far-right group that has claimed the forced mobilization of Japanese military comfort women victims is not a fact. Since last year, they have been staging protests insulting comfort women victims at various locations in Seoul, during which they have been accused of defaming the victims' honor. This incident gained more attention after President Lee Jae-myung mentioned it directly, leading to focused police investigations.
