Controversy Over Jeongeup Waste Wood Power Plant Rises as Election Issue

The conflict surrounding the construction of a waste wood thermal power plant in Jeongeup, Jeollabuk-do, is becoming a major issue in the local elections. The Jeongeup Waste Wood Thermal Power Plant Opposition Committee has announced the end of their 95-day tent sit-in and declared a transition to a "judgment system" aimed at the political sphere. They are demanding that local election candidates adopt the cancellation of the power plant as part of their pledges, clearly stating that they will continue to oppose the project citing residents' health and environmental concerns.

This situation is expected to extend beyond a mere local residents' opposition movement and lead to significant political pressure that will greatly influence the local elections. The committee emphasizes the negative impact of the power plant plans on the local environment and expresses its determination to hold politicians accountable.

Key Highlights

  • The Jeongeup Waste Wood Thermal Power Plant Opposition Committee ends its 95-day sit-in and transitions to political struggle.
  • Candidates in the local elections are being asked to pledge the cancellation of the power plant.
  • The power plant is expected to incinerate up to 552 tons of waste wood per day, raising environmental concerns.
  • The project implementer, Jeongeup Green Power, emphasizes safety and promises communication with residents.
  • The committee plans to continue its struggle through ongoing monitoring activities and individual protests.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This controversy is an extension of the opposition movement that began in April of last year. Residents have expressed concerns about the impact of the power plant on the local environment, advocating for their rights to health and a clean environment. As Jeongeup City pushes for the construction of the power plant, the committee has represented residents' voices through their tent sit-in. It is now anticipated that this struggle will influence the elections and that political pressure will increase.

Why It Matters

This issue transcends a simple local conflict, serving as an important case study on how environmental issues and residents' rights are politically addressed. As the conflict between the positive economic effects of the power plant and environmental protection intensifies, politicians will need to handle this issue with care. Ultimately, this will directly affect the lives of local residents, significantly influencing voters' choices.
