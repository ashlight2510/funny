EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism: A New Challenge for Korean Exports

The European Union's (EU) Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) is set to be implemented in earnest from 2031. This system aims to establish a low-carbon supply chain by imposing carbon emission costs on companies exporting to the EU. The Korea International Trade Association's International Trade and Commerce Research Institute recently analyzed the impact of this system on Korea's exports to the EU in a newly released report. The report warns that if the burden of carbon costs increases significantly, a 1% rise in export prices could lead to a decrease in export volume by up to 18%.

This article emphasizes that Korean companies must expedite their low-carbon transition and process innovations within the next five years. As the EU reduces its free allocation of carbon allowances, Korean companies could face serious damage to their export competitiveness if they do not respond appropriately to these changes.

Key Points

  • Implementation of CBAM: Set to begin in 2031, with an expansion of target items starting in 2028.
  • Increase in Export Prices: If the burden of carbon costs rises, a 1% increase in export prices could lead to a maximum 18% decrease in export volume.
  • Reduction of Free Allocations: The EU's free allocation rate for carbon allowances is expected to decrease to 0% by 2034.
  • Need for Low-Carbon Transition: Companies must maintain competitiveness through low-carbon facility transitions and process innovations.
  • Expansion of Target Items: The scope will expand beyond steel and aluminum to include various industries such as machinery and electronics.

Why Background Context Matters

The EU has introduced the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism to address environmental issues, which is part of a global effort to reduce carbon emissions. With increasing international pressure related to climate change, the EU is strengthening regulations to cut carbon emissions. As one of the EU's major trading partners, the impact of these changes on the Korean economy is expected to be significant.

Why It Matters

This system could have a substantial impact not only on environmental issues but also on the export competitiveness of Korean companies. Particularly, if carbon costs are reflected in export prices, Korean companies may lose price competitiveness, which could ultimately lead to a decrease in export volumes. Therefore, companies need to invest in and strategize for low-carbon transitions starting now.

Personal Perspective

In my view, this issue represents a critical juncture that demands a transformation of the Korean industrial structure, transcending simple economic cost-benefit calculations. Many companies tend to view carbon emissions solely as a cost, but this could hinder sustainable development in the long run. The transition to low-carbon practices is becoming a necessity rather than an option.

Hidden Variables to Consider

While many focus on the items or rates to which CBAM applies, the actual investment in technology and infrastructure necessary for low-carbon transitions is more crucial. Additionally, companies should not overlook the potential additional costs or risks that may arise during the preparation for this system.

Key Points to Check Now

  • Low-Carbon Transition Plans: Companies need to establish concrete plans for low-carbon transitions.
  • Monitoring EU Regulatory Changes: Continuous monitoring of regulatory changes related to CBAM is necessary.
  • International Cooperation Strategies: There is a need to share and develop low-carbon technologies through cooperation with other countries.

FAQ

Q1: What is CBAM?
A1: CBAM is the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism introduced by the EU, which imposes carbon emission costs on exports to establish a low-carbon supply chain.

Q2: How will CBAM affect Korean companies?
A2: Under CBAM, the export prices of Korean companies may rise, which could lead to a decrease in export volumes.

Q3: What preparations should companies make?
A3: Companies should proactively respond by transitioning to low-carbon facilities and innovating processes to maintain competitiveness.

Q4: What are the target items for CBAM?
A4: Initially focused on steel and aluminum, the scope is expected to expand to various industries, including machinery and electronics.

It is now time for companies to seriously consider the influence of the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism. Establishing practical plans for low-carbon transitions is more important than ever.
