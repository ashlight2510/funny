Why Did the Government Join the Statement Condemning the Closure of the Strait of Hormuz?

Recently, the South Korean government joined a joint statement condemning the closure of the Strait of Hormuz by seven countries. This statement strongly criticizes Iran's actions regarding the closure of the strait and emphasizes the safety and freedom of navigation. The South Korean government clarified that this decision is made considering the impact on energy supply and the economy, reaffirming its commitment to maintaining the safety of international maritime traffic routes.

The South Korean government's participation in this statement carries significance beyond mere support. It serves as an opportunity to reaffirm its responsibility as a member of the international community and its willingness to cooperate for the normalization of global maritime logistics.

Why Background Context is Important

This statement was issued as concerns from the international community regarding the Iranian military's closure of the Strait of Hormuz have grown. The strait is a vital transport route for global energy, and Iran's military actions could have serious implications for the international economy. The South Korean government's participation can be seen as a strategy to secure its energy security amid this international pressure.

Why It Matters

This statement is not merely a diplomatic gesture; it directly relates to South Korea's energy supply and economic stability. Since a significant portion of the crude oil imported by South Korea passes through the Strait of Hormuz, any military actions by Iran that affect navigation could have substantial repercussions for the economy. Therefore, this participation can be interpreted as a demonstration of the South Korean government's commitment to securing economic stability.
