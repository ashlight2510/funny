Last year's second half virtual asset trading down 14%... 90 trillion won flows overseas

In the second half of last year, domestic virtual asset trading decreased by 14% due to an overall market downturn, with a total of 90 trillion won flowing overseas. This indicates a significant reduction in trading volume at domestic exchanges compared to the first half. Notably, Bitcoin prices showed a downward trend, and the average volatility of virtual assets was overwhelmingly higher than that of the stock market. This is closely related to the structural contraction of the domestic virtual asset market.

These changes have been attributed to regulations on derivative trading in the virtual asset market as one of the main causes. Under current law, virtual assets are not recognized as underlying assets for derivatives, making leverage and short selling transactions impossible on exchanges. As a result, many investors are moving to overseas exchanges.

Key Summary

  • Domestic virtual asset trading volume in the second half of last year decreased by 14% compared to the first half.
  • The amount of virtual assets transferred overseas reached 90 trillion won, an increase of 14% compared to the first half.
  • Bitcoin prices fell from around 160 million won in the second half of last year to around 120 million won, and to around 90 million won at the beginning of this year.
  • The average volatility of virtual assets was 73%, significantly higher than KOSPI (28.3%) and KOSDAQ (18.8%).
  • While the number of exchange users increased, the majority of accounts held less than 1 million won in virtual assets.

Why It Matters

This issue reveals structural problems in the virtual asset market beyond just a decrease in trading volume. In particular, the outflow of assets overseas can weaken the competitiveness of domestic exchanges and undermine investor confidence. This increases the likelihood that revitalization of the virtual asset market will become more challenging.

Why Background Context Matters

The virtual asset market has shown instability globally since last year. In particular, the continued decline in prices of major virtual assets like Bitcoin has left many investors feeling psychologically constrained. In this situation, domestic exchanges have been unable to offer leverage and short selling, leading investors to turn to overseas exchanges.

Personal Perspective

I believe that regulatory easing for virtual asset exchanges is necessary. The current legal constraints are encouraging investors to move overseas, which will lead to a contraction of the domestic market. If the government adopts a more flexible approach to virtual assets, it could contribute to the recovery of domestic exchanges' competitiveness and market revitalization.

Hidden Variables to Consider

The volatility of the virtual asset market poses significant risks to investors, but it can also present opportunities. However, many investors overlook this and focus solely on short-term declines. It is important to develop investment strategies from a long-term perspective.

Points to Check Now

  • Changes in regulations in the virtual asset market and the government's policy direction.
  • Trading conditions and fee structures of overseas exchanges.
  • Price fluctuation trends of Bitcoin and major virtual assets.

FAQ

Q: Why is there an increase in the transfer of virtual asset exchanges overseas?
A: Domestic exchanges do not allow leverage and short selling, prompting investors to seek better trading conditions overseas.

Q: Why is the average volatility of virtual assets high?
A: The virtual asset market has relatively small scale and high speculation, leading to significant price volatility, which poses greater risks to investors.

Q: How will the virtual asset market change in the future?
A: If the government eases regulations and stabilizes the market, there is a possibility of recovering the competitiveness of domestic exchanges. Conversely, if regulations persist, the outflow of assets overseas may deepen.

I hope this article helps you understand the current situation and future prospects of the virtual asset market well. The most important point to focus on now is the impact of regulatory changes by the government on the virtual asset market.
