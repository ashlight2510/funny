Samsung ‘Technology’ SK ‘Partnership’… Divergent Paths at GTC, HBM4 ‘Rivals’

At NVIDIA's annual conference ‘GTC 2026’, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix garnered attention with their distinct strategies. Samsung emphasized its technological superiority by unveiling its latest memory semiconductor, the HBM4E, while SK Hynix focused on strengthening partnerships with global big tech companies, led by Chairman Chey Tae-won. Both companies are solidifying their positions in the AI memory semiconductor market, which is emerging as a crucial issue that could reshape the market landscape in the future.

This move goes beyond mere technological competition, showcasing the potential for collaboration within the AI ecosystem and the differences in corporate strategies. While Samsung expressed its intent to compete through technology, SK Hynix demonstrated a focus on strengthening its market position through collaboration. These differentiated strategies are expected to complicate the competitive landscape in the memory semiconductor market moving forward.

Key Summary

  • Samsung Electronics emphasized its technology by unveiling the HBM4E at GTC 2026.
  • SK Hynix concentrated on strengthening partnerships with global big tech companies.
  • NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang visited both companies' booths, showcasing their collaborative relationships.
  • SK Hynix hinted at the possibility of listing on the US stock market, anticipating a reassessment of its corporate value.
  • Concerns about ongoing memory semiconductor supply shortages persist.

Key Scenes

  • Samsung improved the performance of the HBM4E, enhancing data processing speed to 16Gbps.
  • SK Hynix aimed to expand market share by showcasing memory products through collaboration with NVIDIA.
  • Chairman Chey Tae-won’s mention of the potential for a US stock market listing could positively impact SK Hynix's market value.
  • Long-term forecasts regarding the memory semiconductor supply shortage have been presented.
  • The strategic differentiation between the two companies is expected to significantly influence future market competition.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The memory semiconductor market has become more critical than ever due to rapid advancements in AI technology and surging demand for data processing. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are establishing their strategies to respond to these market changes, particularly as their relationships with NVIDIA significantly impact their competitiveness. Samsung aims to maintain market leadership through technological advancements, while SK Hynix seeks to expand its influence within the ecosystem through partnerships.

Why It Matters

The actions of both companies at GTC 2026 serve as an important gauge for predicting the future of the memory semiconductor market, transcending mere technological competition. As demand for AI-related memory increases, it is essential to observe how the strategies of both companies will bring about changes in the market. In this process, Samsung's technological prowess and SK Hynix's partnership strategy appear to follow different paths, leveraging their respective strengths to compete.

Notable Points

In my view, the key issue will be how quickly Samsung's HBM4E technology can be commercialized in the actual market. Technological superiority does not always translate into market share. Conversely, while SK Hynix's partnership strategy may yield short-term benefits, questions about sustainability may arise if it is not backed by long-term technological development.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Both technological capability and partnership strategies are important, but supply chain stability must not be overlooked. The issue of memory semiconductor supply shortages remains unresolved, and how both companies respond in this market environment will be a determining factor in their future success.

Current Checkpoints

  • The timing of the actual product launch of Samsung's HBM4E technology and its performance.
  • Tracking the actual outcomes of SK Hynix's global partnerships.
  • Changes in the memory semiconductor market's supply chain and the resulting price stability.
