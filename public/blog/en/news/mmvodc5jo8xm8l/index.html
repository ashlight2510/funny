90 Victims of the Jeju Spy Fabrication Case Officially Confirmed: Background and Significance

Recently, it was reported that Jeju Province has officially confirmed 90 victims of the spy fabrication case. This incident addresses the suffering of Jeju residents who were falsely accused of espionage due to their connections with Japan from the 1960s to the 1980s. The investigation revealed that these individuals were suspected of espionage for reasons such as visiting relatives in Japan and smuggling for economic reasons, with many experiencing illegal detention and torture during the process. This investigation was conducted based on the Jeju Special Self-Governing Province Ordinance on Support for Victims of the Spy Fabrication Case and is expected to serve as foundational data for restoring the victims' honor and promoting human rights.

This case holds significant meaning beyond mere historical facts, as it continues to resonate today. The intertwining of Jeju residents' identity and historical pain illustrates how the erroneous judgments of security agencies can severely impact individuals' lives.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This case reflects the complex social context stemming from the historical relationship between Jeju residents and Japan. Due to events like the Japanese colonial period and the Jeju 4.3 Incident, many Jeju residents resorted to smuggling into Japan, deepening their ties with the country. However, from the 1960s to the 1980s, security agencies exploited this historical background to fabricate espionage cases. The investigation confirmed that 92.2% of the spy fabrication cases in Jeju were linked to Japan, highlighting the complex situation involving the social identity and historical pain of Jeju residents.
