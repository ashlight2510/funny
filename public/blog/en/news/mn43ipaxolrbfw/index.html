Daejeon Safety Industry Fire: Repeated Warnings and the Resulting Tragedy

The large fire that occurred at the Daejeon Safety Industry factory is not a mere coincidence. Since 2009, there have been a total of 7 reported fires to 119, most of which were caused by dust or oil residues in dust collection facilities. The recent fire resulted in 14 fatalities and a total of 70 casualties, clearly demonstrating that the internal environment of the factory is highly vulnerable to fires. This incident can be interpreted not as an accident but as a consequence of ignoring repeated warnings.

This incident prompts us to reconsider how we view industrial safety. The tragic results of negligence in safety management should serve as a social wake-up call.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The Daejeon Safety Industry factory has experienced multiple fires in the past, with inadequate management of dust collection facilities being cited as a cause. In particular, metal dust sludge or oil residues inside the dust collector have played a significant role in causing fires. These repeated incidents suggest that factory operators and relevant authorities have been negligent in safety management.

This fire is not merely a coincidence but can be seen as a result of continuously ignoring warnings. The fact that the internal environment of the factory is vulnerable to fires has already been revealed through several previous incidents.
