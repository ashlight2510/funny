"Is the Weakness of the Korean Won Excessive? Key Issues Discussed by Minister Park Hong-geun"

April 1, 2026 · 경향신문 · View source

Original Article Overview Title: Koo Yoon-cheol “The Weakness of the Won is Excessive”… Had First Meeting with Minister Park Hong-geun Source: Kyunghyang Shinmun On April 1, Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Finance Koo Yoon-cheol stated that the recent rise of the won-dollar exchange rate above 1500 won due to the Middle Eastern war is “an excessive weakness of the won that is disconnected from fundamentals and does not benefit our economy.” On this day, Deputy Prime Minister Koo held the first official 'trilateral consultation' with Minister of Planning and Budget Park Hong-geun and Chairman of the Financial Services Commission Lee Ok-won to discuss responses to the Middle Eastern war. Deputy Prime Minister Koo emphasized the importance of organic coordination among economic policies.

This article not only addresses fluctuations in the exchange rate but also serves as a warning about the structural problems currently facing the South Korean economy and the potential social instability that may arise from them. Additionally, it presents a blueprint for how the government plans to respond to this situation.

Key Summary

  • Deputy Prime Minister Koo Yoon-cheol warned that the weakness of the won is excessive and emphasized the impact of the Middle Eastern war on the macroeconomy.
  • The government plans to respond to economic uncertainties through organic coordination of budget, tax, financial, and foreign exchange policies.
  • This meeting highlighted the importance of cooperation and discussion among economic ministries.

Key Scenes

  • The won-dollar exchange rate surpasses 1500 won, intensifying the weakness of the won.
  • Increased macroeconomic uncertainty due to the Middle Eastern war.
  • Emphasis on the need for organic coordination among government economic policies.
  • Urgent need for the swift passage of the supplementary budget bill.
  • Necessity for in-depth discussions to resolve structural problems.

Why Background Matters

The recent weakness of the won is closely related to global economic anxiety caused by the Middle Eastern war. In particular, the situation where the won-dollar exchange rate exceeds 1500 won can impose a significant burden on the South Korean economy. This economic pressure is likely to hit vulnerable groups harder, and the government is at a point where it needs to prepare measures for this.

The meeting between Deputy Prime Minister Koo and Minister Park is interpreted as an effort to enhance policy consistency through cooperation among economic ministries. This can be seen as a first step toward addressing the complex structural problems of the economy.

Why It Matters

This statement reveals factors that threaten the stability and sustainability of the entire South Korean economy, going beyond just the exchange rate issue. If the weakness of the won continues, it could negatively impact not only export companies but also general consumers. In particular, vulnerable groups may suffer more due to rising prices. Therefore, the government's policy decisions will inevitably have a direct impact on all citizens.

My Focus Point

In my view, the emphasis on "organic coordination" in this meeting is very important. This means that beyond simply adjusting economic policies, each ministry must collaborate to develop more effective responses. Especially, efforts are needed to minimize the impact of external factors like the Middle Eastern war on the South Korean economy. Such cooperation is expected to significantly influence future economic policies.

Hidden Variables to Consider

One aspect not covered in the article is the specific policy measures for stabilizing the exchange rate. Deputy Prime Minister Koo mentioned the passage of the "Three Laws for Exchange Rate Stabilization," but there was a lack of concrete explanations on how this legislation will contribute to the economy. Additionally, a clear roadmap is needed for how the South Korean economy will respond if international circumstances change.

Current Checkpoints

  • The timing of the government's supplementary budget bill passing through the National Assembly.
  • Specific contents and effects of the Three Laws for Exchange Rate Stabilization.
  • Progress of the Middle Eastern war and the corresponding reactions of the South Korean economy.

FAQ

Q: What impact will the continued weakness of the won have?
A: The weakness of the won can lead to rising import prices, negatively affecting consumer prices, which will be a significant burden, especially for vulnerable groups.

Q: What are the government's countermeasures?
A: The government plans to respond to economic uncertainties through organic coordination of macroeconomic policies and intends to expedite the implementation of the supplementary budget bill.

Q: What impact does the Middle Eastern war have on the South Korean economy?
A: The Middle Eastern war causes global economic anxiety, which can negatively affect South Korea's exports and investments.

Q: What are the Three Laws for Exchange Rate Stabilization?
A: These are laws enacted to stabilize the exchange rate, expected to contribute to exchange rate stability by promoting the repatriation of overseas investment funds.

In conclusion, what we need to focus on now is the government's policy decisions and their implementation process. In particular, there is a need for policy preparations to address the potential long-term implications of the Middle Eastern war, ensuring the stability of the economy.
