OpenAI folds 'Sora' after 2 years... Is monetization the key now? Big tech's 'selection and concentration'

OpenAI has decided to discontinue its AI video generation tool 'Sora'. This decision reflects a commitment to concentrate resources on more critical areas for improving profitability, including hiring an advertising executive from Meta and introducing an advertising revenue model. OpenAI is shifting its focus from competition in AI model performance to monetization, a trend that is not only an issue for OpenAI but is also common among global big tech companies.

This change is noteworthy as companies are restructuring their strategies to secure revenue models amid the acceleration of the commercialization of AI technology. In particular, OpenAI appears to be taking measures to maximize profits ahead of its initial public offering (IPO) in 2024.

Key Summary

  • OpenAI has decided to end the 'Sora' service after two years and focus on improving profitability.
  • Hiring an advertising executive from Meta and introducing ads to ChatGPT users are part of diversifying revenue models.
  • The competition for AI monetization is spreading globally, with domestic companies adopting similar strategies.
  • OpenAI still faces the challenge of securing stable revenue sources.

Reasons to Consider the Background

OpenAI's Sora service gained attention for its ability to easily produce high-quality video content since its debut in February 2024. In particular, a $1 billion licensing deal with Disney raised high expectations, but the recent decision was made amid uncertainties regarding the revenue model. OpenAI is modifying its strategy to not only enhance the performance of AI technology but also to create business models that can actually generate revenue.

Why It Matters

This news goes beyond just the termination of OpenAI's service; it illustrates what strategies are necessary for AI technology to succeed commercially. The fact that companies are focusing on generating revenue through AI technology serves as an important benchmark for gauging the future direction of the AI industry. Additionally, this change is likely to impact other companies, potentially altering the competitive landscape.

In my view, OpenAI's decision is not merely about ending a service but represents a case of a new paradigm in the AI industry. Hidden Variables to Consider

While the article highlights OpenAI's decision, it is important to note that other companies are also adopting similar strategies. For example, Naver and Kakao are moving towards integrating AI technology across their services. This underscores the point that the commercialization of AI technology is not limited to a single company but affects the entire industry.
