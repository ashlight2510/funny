BTS Concert and Forced Annual Leave: Legal Issues and Workers' Rights

Recently, ahead of the BTS concert in Gwanghwamun, there have been cases of nearby businesses forcing employees to take annual leave or notifying them of temporary closures. The civic group Workplace Gapjil 119 warns that these cases may involve legal violations and emphasizes that workers' rights to use annual leave must be respected. This incident has sparked an important discussion about workers' rights and corporate obligations that goes beyond just one concert.

This incident illustrates how legal standards regarding annual leave usage can be violated and how workers' rights can be infringed. In particular, the timing of annual leave should be determined by the workers, and companies cannot unilaterally change this, which raises legal issues. Such situations frequently occur during large-scale events, highlighting how workers' rights can be overlooked.

Key Points

  • It is highly likely illegal for companies to unilaterally designate annual leave on specific days.
  • The principle is that the timing of annual leave should be determined by the worker, and companies cannot change this solely based on their circumstances.
  • Even in businesses with fewer than five employees, if the annual leave system is stipulated in employment rules or contracts, it must be followed.
  • Workers who have applied for annual leave can file a complaint with the labor office if the company ignores this and forcibly deducts leave.
  • The burden from large-scale events tends to disproportionately fall on vulnerable workers.

Background and Context of the Issue

The BTS concert attracts significant public attention and draws large crowds. In such situations, nearby businesses often pressure employees to take annual leave to avoid congestion. However, such measures can be legally problematic, as, according to labor standards law, the decision to take annual leave should be made by the worker. This incident reveals issues of workers' rights violations due to large-scale events, extending beyond just the BTS concert.

Why It Matters

This incident leads to important discussions about the rights and obligations between companies and workers, going beyond just the issue of annual leave. If companies do not respect workers' rights, it could lead to legal liabilities, and workers need to know how to assert their rights. Furthermore, if such cases continue to occur, awareness of workers' rights will become even more critical.

My Perspective on Workers' Rights

In my view, this incident highlights not only legal issues but also the importance of awareness and action among workers to protect their rights. In situations where companies force annual leave, if workers do not assert their rights, they ultimately suffer the consequences. Therefore, workers should clearly understand their rights and learn how to take legal action if necessary.

Hidden Variables to Consider

A notable point in this incident is that workers in businesses with fewer than five employees, freelancers, special employment, and platform workers find it difficult to receive legal protection. These workers may not be covered by or may have limited application of labor standards law, making it challenging to demand annual leave or temporary closure pay. This can lead to serious issues, as the burden from large-scale events disproportionately affects relatively vulnerable workers.

Key Points to Check Now

  • Are you aware of your rights regarding annual leave?
  • Do you fully understand your company's policies on annual leave usage?
  • Do you know what procedures to follow if legal issues arise?

FAQ

Q1: Can companies unilaterally designate annual leave?
A1: Generally, companies must determine the timing of annual leave according to the worker's wishes and cannot designate it unilaterally.

Q2: What should I do if the company ignores my annual leave request?
A2: In this case, you can file a complaint with the labor office and take legal action.

Q3: Do annual leave regulations apply to businesses with fewer than five employees?
A3: Yes, if the annual leave system is stipulated in the employment rules or contract, it applies.

This incident emphasizes the need for workers to know and assert their rights. We hope for a society where basic rights, such as the right to annual leave, are respected.
