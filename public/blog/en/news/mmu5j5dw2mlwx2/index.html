The Cause of the Failure of Korea's First Commercial Launch Vehicle Space Mission is 'Combustion Gas Leakage'

Last December, Innospace's launch vehicle 'Hanbit-Nano' was launched from the Alcântara Space Center in Brazil, but it failed during ascent due to combustion gas leakage. Innospace recently confirmed this issue through a joint investigation with the Brazilian Air Force and is planning a relaunch in the third quarter of this year. The combustion gas leakage was found to have occurred due to deformation of the sealing device during the process of replacing the combustion tube cap during launch preparations. This led to the rupture of the body, causing the launch vehicle to break into several pieces, and Innospace has decided to improve its assembly processes and component designs.

This incident is not just a simple technical failure; it is a significant issue that could greatly impact the future of the domestic space industry. As Korea's first commercial launch vehicle, Hanbit-Nano aimed to transport satellites into Earth's orbit, making this failure raise concerns about the reliability and safety of the future private space market.

Why Background Matters

This incident marks the first failure in the commercialization process of the domestic space industry, as it is the first time a launch vehicle developed by a private company has attempted an actual launch. Innospace aimed to open up the possibilities of the domestic space launch vehicle market with Hanbit-Nano, but it suffered a significant setback due to technical issues. Such failures prompt a reassessment of the technological capabilities and safety of the domestic space industry, indicating that more verification and preparation will be necessary for future space launch vehicle development.

Why It Matters

In particular, the success or failure of commercial launch vehicles can influence the investments and participation of other companies, making this incident significant for the entire Korean space industry.
