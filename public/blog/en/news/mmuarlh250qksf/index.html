Impact of the Hormuz Blockade on the Basic Chemical Industry···Japanese Companies Reduce Ethylene Production

Recently, the blockade of the Hormuz Strait has had a serious impact on Japan's basic chemical industry. Japanese petrochemical companies have begun to reduce production of various chemical raw materials, including ethylene, due to significant disruptions in the supply of crude oil and naphtha from the Middle East. In particular, naphtha, a key raw material for ethylene, heavily relies on imports from the Middle East, making the instability in this region ripple through Japan's chemical industry.

This situation has worsened as more than half of Japan's ethylene production facilities have entered into production cuts, and related companies are struggling with raw material procurement. Naphtha prices have already surged, and this is expected to affect the prices of final consumer goods as well.

Key Summary

  • Japanese ethylene producers have begun to reduce production due to the Hormuz Strait blockade.
  • Over 70% of Japan's naphtha imports come from the Middle East, with a 90% reliance on crude oil.
  • More than half of ethylene production facilities have initiated production cuts, leading to a spike in naphtha prices.
  • Japan's naphtha inventory is only sufficient for 20 days, making it difficult to maintain the supply chain.
  • Efforts are underway to seek naphtha procurement outside the Middle East, but alternatives are not easy to find.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The Hormuz Strait is a major route for global oil transportation, and the blockade has become a reality as tensions with Iran have escalated. Japan heavily depends on crude oil and naphtha imports from the Middle East, and since the outbreak of the Iran war, naphtha prices have surged by 66%. In this context, Japanese ethylene producers have anticipated difficulties in raw material procurement and decided to reduce production. This is not just a simple reduction in production but a complex issue that could impact the entire Japanese chemical industry.

Why It Matters

Japan's basic chemical industry is a massive sector with an annual shipment value of about 50 trillion yen (approximately 468 trillion won). Basic chemical raw materials like ethylene are used in a variety of final products, including packaging materials, automotive tires, and detergents, so a contraction in this industry is likely to lead to rising consumer prices. Both consumers and businesses may face price increases, which could have negative ripple effects across the economy.

Personal Comment

In my view, the most noteworthy aspect of this situation is the reason why Japanese ethylene producers have begun to cut production and the subsequent chain reactions that may arise. It is not merely a matter of reduced production; the instability in raw material supply could lead to price increases that directly affect consumers. Additionally, it should not be overlooked that finding alternative raw materials to replace naphtha within Japan is not an easy task.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Many people may understand the impact of the Hormuz Strait blockade on Japan's basic chemical industry solely as a rise in raw material prices. However, it is important to note that this could lead to price increases for consumer goods and trigger structural changes across Japan's chemical industry ecosystem. Considering how challenging it is to find naphtha supply chains outside the Middle East, this issue is unlikely to be resolved with short-term solutions.

Points to Check Now

  • The scale of production cuts by Japanese ethylene producers and the resulting price trend.
  • The response strategies of the Japanese government and companies to secure naphtha supply chains outside the Middle East.
  • Changes in the situation in the Hormuz Strait and the corresponding fluctuations in international oil and naphtha prices.

FAQ

Q: How long is the blockade of the Hormuz Strait expected to last?
A: It is difficult to predict the current situation, but it may be prolonged depending on the political circumstances.

Q: Are other countries outside Japan experiencing similar impacts?
A: Yes, other countries like South Korea that also have a high dependence on Middle Eastern naphtha are facing similar situations.

Q: What impact does the reduction in ethylene production have on consumers?
A: Since ethylene is a raw material for various consumer goods, there is a high likelihood that the prices of final consumer goods will rise.
