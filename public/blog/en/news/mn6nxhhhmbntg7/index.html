“A 100,000 Won Loan Can Affect Your Credit Even if Delayed by Just 5 Days”… Financial Supervisory Service Urges Consumer Caution

According to a recent announcement by the Financial Supervisory Service, a loan of 100,000 won or more can lead to credit disadvantages if delayed by just 5 business days. When a delay occurs, financial institutions send short-term delinquency information to credit rating agencies, which can negatively impact various financial transactions, including card suspensions, loan rejections, and credit score declines. It emphasized that delinquency records remain for a certain period even after repayment, highlighting the need for careful credit management.

This warning aims to address the increasing issues related to loan usage among consumers and is interpreted as an effort to enhance the transparency and reliability of financial transactions.

Key Summary

  • Credit disadvantages occur if a loan of 100,000 won or more is delayed for 5 business days.
  • Delinquency information is shared with financial institutions, posing risks such as card suspension and loan rejection.
  • Delinquency records remain for a certain period after repayment, necessitating credit management.
  • Card usage performance is crucial when taking out bank loans, and complex procedures are required in case of erroneous transfers.
  • Guidance on the possibility of interest rate increases after signing fixed-rate mortgage contracts.

Key Scenes

  • Credit Disadvantages from Delinquency: Delays exceeding 5 business days negatively impact credit information.
  • Sharing of Credit Information: Financial institutions transmit short-term delinquency information to credit rating agencies, which is shared among multiple financial institutions.
  • Record Maintenance After Repayment: Delinquency records persist for a certain period after repayment, continuously affecting credit scores.
  • Card Usage Performance: Card usage performance is essential to receive loan interest rate reductions.
  • Transfer Precautions: Caution is needed due to the complexity of procedures in case of erroneous transfers.

Reasons to Consider the Background

As the importance of loan and credit management in the financial sector increases, the Financial Supervisory Service has issued this warning to raise consumer awareness. Particularly, as small loans are on the rise in the current economic climate, the impact of credit disadvantages from delinquency on consumers is becoming more significant. Therefore, financial authorities believe that consumers need to be more cautious when using loans and manage their credit diligently.

Why It Matters

This warning goes beyond merely cautioning against loan delinquency; it provides essential information for consumers to manage their credit scores. For consumers seeking loans, disadvantages such as credit score declines can significantly affect loan conditions, making this information very important. This is especially crucial for younger generations who frequently use loans or consumers with limited financial means.

A decline in credit scores can lead to worse loan conditions or even loan rejections, which can impose a significant long-term burden on consumers. FAQ

Q: How much will my credit score drop if I become delinquent?
A: It varies depending on the duration and amount of the delay, but generally, a delay of more than 5 business days can significantly lower your credit score.

Q: How long does a delinquency record remain?
A: Delinquency records can remain for up to 5 years after repayment.

Q: How can I verify my card usage performance when applying for a loan?
A: When applying for a loan, you can verify your card usage performance according to the bank's policies, and you can also check through statements issued by the card company.

Q: What should I do if I make an erroneous transfer?
A: In the case of an erroneous transfer, you can generally request a refund through your bank or the deposit insurance corporation, and if the funds were transferred to a seized account, legal procedures may be required.

This information is crucial for helping consumers make more informed decisions in their financial transactions. It is advisable to check your credit status right away.
