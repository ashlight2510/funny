As LNG Import Burdens Increase, Asian Countries Turn to Domestic Coal Power

Recently, it has been reported that Asian countries are increasing coal power generation to alleviate the burden of liquefied natural gas (LNG) imports. Due to the conflict between the U.S. and Iran, LNG prices have surged, and supply disruptions such as Qatar's production halt have led major Asian countries to pivot towards increasing the share of coal power generation. This shift is significantly impacting energy supply and is bringing a new phase to the electricity production methods in the Asian region.

This situation is not merely a fluctuation in energy prices; it can have serious implications for each country's energy policies, environmental issues, and international relations. Therefore, this issue deserves attention not only from an economic perspective but also from political and environmental viewpoints.

Key Summary

  • Asian countries are expanding coal power generation due to soaring LNG prices and supply disruptions.
  • Qatar has halted LNG production, and countries like Pakistan and Vietnam are increasing their coal power generation.
  • Energy supply disruptions are expected to continue, leading to downward adjustments in the outlook for LNG imports in Asia.

Key Scenes

  • Surge in LNG Prices: Recently, LNG prices have more than doubled compared to pre-war levels.
  • Increase in Coal Power Share: Countries in Asia, including Pakistan, Vietnam, and Japan, are expanding coal power generation.
  • Ongoing Supply Disruptions: Supply disruptions in the Middle East are expected to continue for two months.
  • Changes in Energy Policy: There is a high likelihood that countries' energy policies will be restructured to focus on coal.
  • Environmental Issues: Concerns are growing about the negative environmental impacts of increased coal power generation.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This issue has arisen as tensions between the U.S. and Iran have intensified. In particular, Asian countries are facing difficulties in LNG supply due to Qatar's production halt and the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. The Asian region accounts for about two-thirds of global LNG demand, so instability in energy supply in this region could have a significant impact on the global energy market. A similar situation occurred during the Russia-Ukraine war, which led to large-scale blackouts.

Why It Matters

These changes are not limited to fluctuations in energy prices. The increase in coal power generation raises environmental issues, and if countries' energy policies shift towards coal, it could negatively impact international efforts to address climate change. Additionally, if energy supply instability continues, Asian countries will have no choice but to intensify their efforts towards energy independence.

Personal Perspective

Hidden Variables to Consider

Coal remains one of the most polluting energy sources, which could accelerate air pollution and climate change. Additionally, the impact of each country's expansion of coal power on international climate change efforts should not be overlooked.

Points to Check Now

  • Changes in energy policies of various countries and the resulting shifts in international relations.
