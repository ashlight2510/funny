Commemorative Stamps Issued for Foreigners Who Dedicated Themselves to Korean Women's Healthcare

Commemorative stamps featuring foreign doctors and nurses who contributed to the development of women's healthcare in Korea will be issued. The Ministry of Science and ICT's Korea Post announced that on April 7, it will release two types of commemorative stamps depicting Rosetta Sherwood Hall and Margaret Jane Edmonds, who worked at 'Bogu Nyogwan', Korea's first women's specialty hospital. They played a significant role in treating women, children, and the disabled, as well as in training female medical professionals during the Confucian constraints of the Joseon Dynasty.

These commemorative stamps will serve as more than just souvenirs; they will provide an opportunity to re-examine the importance of these individuals in the history of Korean healthcare. Celebrating their dedication sends an important message to women and minorities who still face limited access to healthcare today.

Why Background Matters

The issuance of these commemorative stamps is part of an effort to highlight important figures in the history of Korean women's healthcare and to widely communicate their contributions. In the late 19th century, Confucian customs and social constraints made it difficult for women to access healthcare in Korea. During this time, Hall and Edmonds laid the groundwork for women's healthcare in Korea and played a significant role in training female medical professionals. Their activities contributed not only to the medical field but also to changing the position and role of women in society at that time.

Why It Matters

The issuance of these commemorative stamps carries significance beyond being mere souvenirs. Acknowledging the contributions of foreigners in the medical field sends an important message to women and minorities who still face limited access to healthcare today. Furthermore, these stamps provide an opportunity to reflect on the development of women's rights alongside the history of healthcare in Korea.
