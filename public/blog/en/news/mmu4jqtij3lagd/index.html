‘Jinan-type Basic Income’ to be implemented in June···Experimenting with ‘Local Economic Circulation’ using Local Gift Certificates

Jinan County in North Jeolla Province is set to implement its ‘Jinan-type Basic Income Pilot Project’ in earnest following the completion of discussions with the Ministry of Health and Welfare regarding the establishment of a social security system. This policy aims to revitalize the local economy and preserve income, planning to utilize local love gift certificates as a means of payment to promote economic circulation within the region. Starting in June, Jinan County will launch a pilot project targeting specific areas or demographics and will decide on the expansion of the project after analyzing its effects.

This pilot project aims to establish itself as an effective system that local residents can genuinely feel, going beyond simple support. Given the serious situation of population decline and aging in Jinan County, this policy is seen as an essential response rather than just an experiment.

Key Summary

  • Jinan County to implement ‘Jinan-type Basic Income’ pilot project in June
  • Completion of discussions with the Ministry of Health and Welfare, finalizing the establishment of a social security system
  • Policy aimed at addressing population decline and aging issues
  • Use of local love gift certificates as a payment method to induce local economic circulation
  • Decision on project expansion to be made after effect analysis

Key Scenes

  • Policy Goal: Income preservation and local economic revitalization in areas with population decline
  • Payment Method: Restricted to local love gift certificates, promoting the circulation of funds within the region
  • Implementation Timing: Pilot project to start in June targeting specific areas and demographics
  • Policy Necessity: Urgent experiment to respond to the crisis of local extinction
  • Effect Analysis: Performance to be analyzed post-pilot project to decide on expansion

Reasons to Consider the Background

Jinan County, which had a population exceeding 100,000 in the 1960s, has recently faced a serious population decline, now down to 24,289. Particularly, with the proportion of the population aged 65 and older exceeding 40%, entering a super-aged society, there was a need for measures to address the simultaneous structural issues of outflow of the working-age population and deteriorating living conditions. It is against this backdrop that the basic income pilot project has been initiated.

Why It Matters

This policy is expected to provide economic effects that local residents can genuinely feel, going beyond simple economic support. If the support funds circulate within the region through local love gift certificates, it could positively impact local businesses. This could play a crucial role in securing the sustainability of the local economy, beyond mere financial assistance.

Personal Comment

In my view, the success of this policy largely depends on the participation and perception of local residents. It is important to observe what changes basic income will actually bring to residents and whether those changes can positively impact the local economy. Additionally, consideration should be given not only to short-term effects but also to long-term sustainability.

Hidden Variables to Consider

Even if this pilot project is successfully established, attention should be paid to the limitations that may arise due to the restricted use of local love gift certificates. Some residents may find it inconvenient to use the gift certificates, which could affect the policy's effectiveness. Furthermore, even if economic support shows short-term effects, additional policies will be necessary to create a sustainable economic structure.

Current Points to Check

  • Specific implementation plans and target demographics for the Jinan-type basic income pilot project
  • Actual effects of using local love gift certificates and residents' reactions
  • Results of effect analysis after the pilot project ends and future policy directions

FAQ

Q: How will the Jinan-type basic income be distributed?
A: Local love gift certificates will be used as the payment method, which can only be used within the region.

Q: What is the goal of this pilot project?
A: The goal is to preserve income and revitalize the local economy in areas facing population decline.

Q: How will the effects of the pilot project be analyzed?
A: After implementation, the economic changes experienced by residents and the impact on the local economy will be analyzed to decide on expansion.

Q: What alternatives will be available if this policy fails?
A: If the policy fails, other forms of support programs or economic revitalization measures should be sought, and alternatives reflecting residents' opinions will be necessary.

Considering these points, the Jinan-type basic income pilot project will not merely be a policy experiment but a significant test that will determine the future of the local economy. It is essential to closely monitor the progress of this policy from now on.
