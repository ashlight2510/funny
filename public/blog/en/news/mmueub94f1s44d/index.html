The 3rd Prosecutorial Reform Plan that Controlled Prosecutor Investigations and Privileges Established a Framework

Recently, the Democratic Party of Korea announced the 3rd Prosecutorial Reform Plan, which includes the establishment of the Public Prosecutor's Office and the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency, moving towards significantly reducing the authority of the prosecution. This bill aims to minimize the possibility of investigative intervention by public prosecutors and clarify the separation of investigation and prosecution to prevent the abuse of prosecutorial power. This reform plan is the result of intense internal discussions, along with direct intervention from President Lee Jae-myung, and is scheduled to be processed in the National Assembly on the 19th.

This reform plan represents the second phase of prosecutorial reform, embodying efforts to control the authority of the prosecution and enhance the transparency of investigative and prosecutorial agencies. However, issues such as the controversy over the retention of high-ranking prosecutors and the assurance of investigative quality remain, indicating that additional improvements may be necessary in the implementation process.

Key Summary

  • Announcement of the Prosecutorial Reform Plan: The Democratic Party announced the law for the establishment of the Public Prosecutor's Office and the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency, reducing prosecutorial authority.
  • Strengthening Separation of Investigation and Prosecution: Reducing the possibility of investigative intervention by public prosecutors to prevent abuse of power.
  • Political Background: Direct intervention by President Lee Jae-myung and reflection of unified opinions among the party, government, and the Blue House.
  • Bill Processing Schedule: The bill is scheduled to be processed in the National Assembly plenary session on the 19th.

Key Scenes

  • Significant reduction of public prosecutor authority and deletion of investigative intervention clauses.
  • Regulation that the scope of prosecutors' duties must be defined only by law.
  • Retention of the title of Prosecutor General, controversy over the retention of high-ranking prosecutors.
  • Establishment of mechanisms to prevent abuse of power by investigative and prosecutorial agencies.
  • Need for protection of crime victims' rights and assurance of investigative quality.

Reasons to Consider the Background

This prosecutorial reform plan has evolved through discussions and conflicts within the Democratic Party regarding the long-standing issue of prosecutorial abuse of power. In particular, President Lee Jae-myung's direct involvement aimed to minimize disagreements among the party, government, and the Blue House, further highlighting the necessity of prosecutorial reform. Given that past instances of expanded prosecutorial power under the Yoon Seok-yeol administration have continued to foster public distrust, this reform plan can be seen as a reflection and improvement in response to that situation.

Why It Matters

This reform plan is an important first step towards controlling prosecutorial authority and enhancing the transparency of investigations and prosecutions. If prosecutorial power is abused, it can negatively impact individual rights and social justice. Therefore, if this bill is successfully implemented, it could contribute to restoring public trust and strengthening the rule of law.

My Thoughts

The success of the prosecutorial reform plan ultimately depends on how well it is implemented on the ground. Continuous monitoring and supplementary measures are necessary to ensure that the principle of separation between investigation and prosecution is upheld. Without concrete measures to protect the rights of crime victims, it will be difficult to avoid criticism that the reform is merely formal. Thus, I hope that this reform plan leads to substantial changes rather than just a reduction of authority.

Hidden Variables to Watch

The provision in the bill that defines the scope of prosecutors' duties by law is a significant change. However, since it leaves room for the prosecution to operate autonomously, management and oversight in this area are essential. Additionally, as there are criticisms regarding the retention of high-ranking prosecutors, the actual effects of the bill will become apparent during the implementation process following its passage.

Current Checkpoints

  • Changes in Investigative and Prosecution Agencies: Whether the transparency and efficiency of actual investigations and prosecutions will improve after the implementation of the reform plan.
  • Crime Victim Protection Measures: Whether additional supplementary measures will be established to protect the rights of victims.
