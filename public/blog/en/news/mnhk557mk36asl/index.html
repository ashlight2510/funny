Questions Remaining After 24 Days of the Yellow Envelope Law Implementation on Subcontractor Unions

April 2, 2026 · 경향신문 · View source

Original article at a glance Title: Recognition of the 'Yellow Envelope Law' for subcontractor unions by primary public institutions after 24 days of implementation Source: Kyunghyang Shinmun Just 24 days after the implementation of the 'Yellow Envelope Law' (amendments to Articles 2 and 3 of the Trade Union and Labor Relations Adjustment Act), the Labor Commission recognized the employer status of primary public institutions towards subcontractor unions for the first time. On the 2nd, the Chungnam Regional Labor Commission's adjudication committee ruled that four public institutions, including the Korea Asset Management Corporation and the Korea Atomic Energy Research Institute, have substantial employer status in the safety management and personnel placement of subcontracted workers. As a result, subcontractor unions have established a legal basis to negotiate with the primary employers.

This case is expected to serve as an important milestone emphasizing the protection of subcontracted workers' rights and the social responsibility of public institutions. The Public Solidarity Labor Union is demanding negotiations with subcontracted workers and urging the Ministry of Employment and Labor to take effective measures.

Key Summary

  • Recognition of employer status for subcontractor unions just 24 days after the implementation of the 'Yellow Envelope Law'.
  • The Chungnam Regional Labor Commission judged the employer status of four public institutions.
  • Subcontractor unions secure the right to negotiate with primary employers.
  • The Public Solidarity Labor Union demands negotiations and urges action from the Ministry of Employment and Labor.

Key Scenes

  • Strengthening the legal basis for the protection of subcontracted workers' rights.
  • Increased possibility of negotiations with subcontracted workers due to the recognition of employer status by public institutions.
  • Increased social pressure on the Ministry of Employment and Labor regarding its role and responsibilities.
  • Clarification of management responsibilities for the safety and personnel placement of subcontracted workers.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The 'Yellow Envelope Law' is an amendment to the Trade Union and Labor Relations Adjustment Act, enacted to protect the rights of subcontracted workers. Since the implementation of this law, legal grounds have been established for subcontracted workers to assert their rights through negotiations with primary employers. This incident showcases how such legal changes can impact subcontracted workers and amplifies the call for public institutions to fulfill their social responsibilities.

Why It Matters

This incident is significant as it opens a pathway for subcontracted workers to assert their rights. With public institutions recognized as employers, subcontracted workers are no longer marginalized but are positioned as legally protected laborers. This could have a substantial impact on other companies and institutions in the future, potentially leading to positive changes across the labor market.

Noteworthy Points

In my view, what we should focus on in this incident is that negotiations with subcontracted workers are not merely a matter of legal rights but also hold social responsibility and ethical dimensions. The recognition of employer status by public institutions will significantly influence how these institutions treat subcontracted workers in the future. Given that subcontracted workers have long suffered from unstable employment and low treatment, it remains to be seen whether this decision will lead to tangible changes for them.

Hidden Variables to Consider

A crucial aspect of this incident is whether subcontracted workers can actually exercise their rights. Just because legal rights are granted does not mean all issues are resolved. There is a need for systems and support that enable subcontracted workers to enjoy tangible benefits through negotiations. Additionally, how public institutions implement this law is also important. Without specific measures in place, this decision may only have legal binding power but lack practical effectiveness.

Points to Check Now

  • It is essential to observe how public institutions will proceed with negotiations with subcontracted workers.
  • We need to verify what effective measures the Ministry of Employment and Labor will take regarding this matter.
  • It is also important to examine what voices subcontracted workers can raise in negotiations and their responses and demands.
