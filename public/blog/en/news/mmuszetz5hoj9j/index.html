US Securities Regulators to Abolish Quarterly Earnings Reports: Key Issues Explored

March 18, 2026 · 연합뉴스 · View source

Original article at a glance Title: US Securities Authority to announce proposal to abolish mandatory quarterly earnings reports as early as next month Source: Yonhap News Published on: March 17, 2026, 10:34 PM (New York=Yonhap News) Reporter Lee Ji-heon = The US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is expected to announce a proposal to abolish the mandatory quarterly earnings reports for listed companies and allow them to choose semi-annual reports twice a year as early as next month. This change is part of an effort to enable companies to focus on long-term strategies rather than short-term performance, stemming from a proposal by former President Donald Trump. However, concerns are also growing regarding the impact on small investors and corporate transparency.

This news is not just a simple regulatory change; it is a significant issue that could have a major impact on the transparency and efficiency of the US capital markets and corporate management practices. Therefore, it is necessary to understand how this new regulation will actually be implemented and the various impacts that may arise as a result.

Key Summary

  • SEC is reviewing the abolition of mandatory quarterly earnings reports and the possibility of choosing semi-annual reports.
  • This measure is part of an effort to allow companies to focus on long-term strategies.
  • The proposal is based on a suggestion from former President Donald Trump.
  • Concerns have been raised about decreased corporate transparency and potential harm to small investors.

Key Scenes

  • Regulatory Change: Companies will be able to choose semi-annual reports instead of quarterly reports.
  • Strengthening Long-term Strategies: Encouraging companies to focus on long-term visions rather than short-term performance.
  • Investor Concerns: Concerns have been raised regarding small investors and corporate transparency.
  • Market Efficiency: There is a risk of decreased efficiency in the capital markets.

Reasons to Consider the Background

The current mandatory quarterly earnings reporting system has been in place since the 1970s and has been criticized for causing companies to focus excessively on short-term performance. Consequently, some companies and management teams have claimed difficulties in establishing long-term visions and strategies. On the other hand, opposition to the abolition of quarterly reports emphasizes that it will negatively impact small investors and undermine market transparency.

Why It Matters

This change is not merely about altering the reporting cycle for companies. It is a significant issue that could affect corporate management practices, relationships with investors, and the credibility of the capital markets. In particular, small investors may lose the opportunity to regularly verify company performance, which could negatively impact their investment decisions.

Personal Perspective

In my view, if this amendment is implemented, large corporations will have more opportunities to focus on long-term strategy development. However, in the process, small investors may become disadvantaged due to information asymmetry. Therefore, the SEC needs to adequately reflect these concerns in its regulatory adjustments. In practice, if companies choose semi-annual reporting, monitoring how the quantity and quality of information they provide changes will be necessary.

Hidden Variables to Consider

It is important to note that this regulatory change will not have the same impact on all companies. Large corporations may have more resources and access to information, making it easier for them to establish long-term strategies, while small and medium-sized enterprises may face the opposite situation. Additionally, the uncertainties that may arise during the implementation of this amendment should not be overlooked.

Points to Check Now

  • Pay attention to the SEC's official announcement schedule and the specifics of the proposed amendments.
  • Check whether additional measures for investor protection will be discussed.
  • It is necessary to continuously monitor the actual changes and impacts when companies choose semi-annual reporting.

FAQ

Q1: What changes will occur for companies if the mandatory quarterly earnings reports are abolished?
A1: Companies will have the option to choose semi-annual reports instead of quarterly reports, allowing them to focus more on long-term strategies.

Q2: How will small investors be affected?
A2: Small investors will lose the opportunity to regularly check company performance, potentially placing them in a disadvantageous situation due to information asymmetry.

Q3: What plans does the SEC have for gathering opinions on this amendment?
A3: The publicly released amendment will undergo a final vote after a comment period of more than 30 days.

Now we need to carefully examine the impact of this change on the capital markets. In particular, discussions on how corporate transparency and the rights of small investors can be harmonized seem necessary.
