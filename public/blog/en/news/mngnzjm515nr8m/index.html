Manned Lunar Exploration Resumed After Half a Century: Launch of Artemis 2

A significant milestone has been established in the history of human space exploration. The manned lunar orbiter 'Artemis 2' was launched at 6:35 PM on April 1 from the Kennedy Space Center in Florida, USA. This marks the first manned lunar exploration in 54 years since Apollo 17, with a crew of three Americans and one Canadian. The mission's objectives include testing life support systems and investigating the effects of space radiation on humans.

The flight duration of Artemis 2 is ten days, covering a total distance of 1,102,400 kilometers. Notably, this mission provides an opportunity to directly observe parts of the lunar surface that humanity has not yet examined. The success of this exploration will be a crucial factor in determining the direction of future space exploration.

Why It's Important to Understand the Background

The Artemis program is a next-generation space exploration project led by NASA, aiming for humanity's return to the Moon and exploration of Mars. Interest in space exploration waned after the Apollo program, but recent advancements in space technology and the involvement of private space companies have reignited enthusiasm for space exploration. Particularly, as international cooperation and competition in space exploration intensify, the launch of Artemis 2 carries symbolic significance.

Why It Matters

The launch of Artemis 2 goes beyond mere technological achievement; it presents a new vision for humanity's exploration of space. Lunar exploration is the first step in space exploration, laying the groundwork for future Mars missions and further ventures into the cosmos. Additionally, the data obtained from this mission will play a crucial role in assessing the impact of space radiation and the efficiency of life support systems.
