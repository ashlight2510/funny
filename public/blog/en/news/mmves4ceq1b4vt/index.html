Myanmar's Parliament Opens After 5 Years of Coup: Key Issues at Stake

March 18, 2026 · 경향신문 · View source

Original article at a glance Title: Myanmar, Parliament opens after 5 years of coup... Military effectively takes control Source: Kyunghyang Shinmun After about five years since the military coup in Myanmar, an elected parliament has been established. However, criticism has arisen that the military-led government under Min Aung Hlaing has effectively seized control of the parliament, rendering the transfer of power to civilian rule merely a facade. According to local media Irrawaddy, the first lower house meeting since the 2021 coup was convened in the capital Naypyidaw on the 16th (local time). Military-affiliated lawmakers wore light green military uniforms, while members of the pro-military party, the National Unity Party, attended in traditional white attire. This parliament was formed based on the results of last year's general elections, but elections were not held in regions where the military could not exert control. As a result, military forces occupy more than 77% of the seats in parliament, and real power remains concentrated in the military.

The opening of this parliament can be seen as an important milestone in Myanmar's democratization process, rather than just a political change. However, there is a significant possibility that the new parliament may become a tool to legitimize military rule. In this situation, the people of Myanmar and the international community must seriously consider whether this parliament is a first step toward true democracy or merely a means to further entrench military governance.

Key Summary

  • The parliament opened in Myanmar after 5 years since the military coup, but the military effectively controls it.
  • Elections were only held in some regions, allowing military forces to secure over 77% of the seats.
  • The majority of the parliament is composed of military forces, making the possibility of restoring democracy appear low.
  • The anti-military democratic camp protests the parliament's opening, calling it a sham election.

Key Scenes

  • Military Control: The military occupies over 77% of the seats in parliament, effectively continuing military rule.
  • Facade of Democracy: The new parliament may function as a means to legitimize military dominance.
  • Election Incompleteness: Elections were held in only 263 out of 330 townships, lacking democratic representation.
  • International Response: The UN special rapporteur warns that it will become a military regime without uniforms.
  • Public Backlash: The anti-military democratic camp criticizes the parliament's opening, claiming to be the legitimate leadership.

Why Background Matters

Since the 2021 coup, the military has seized power in Myanmar, and attempts to establish a democratic government have been thwarted. Prior to the coup, democratization was underway under the leadership of former State Counsellor Aung San Suu Kyi, but everything was overturned due to military intervention. The opening of this parliament seems to indicate an attempt by the military to transfer power to civilians, but real power remains concentrated in the military. This situation is further complicated by the civil war in Myanmar and the resistance from anti-military forces.

Why It Matters

The opening of this parliament is not just a political event. It could rekindle international interest and potential intervention in Myanmar's democratization process. Additionally, if military rule continues, the human rights situation in Myanmar will worsen, significantly impacting international humanitarian aid and diplomatic policies.

Personal Perspective

In my view, the opening of this parliament is likely to result in further entrenchment of military power. The fact that military forces hold the majority in parliament suggests that military governance will persist rather than a democratic process. In this process, the voices of the Myanmar people are likely to be increasingly ignored, leading to criticism and backlash from the international community.

Hidden Variables to Watch

A key point to note in this parliament's opening is the backlash from the anti-military democratic camp. They claim to be the legitimate leadership and emphasize that the military is maintaining power through illegal means. These voices could serve as important references when addressing the Myanmar issue in the international community. Moreover, it is essential not to overlook the uncertainty regarding how the new political landscape will unfold with the military's control over parliament.

  • Movements of the anti-military democratic camp and the military's responses to them.
